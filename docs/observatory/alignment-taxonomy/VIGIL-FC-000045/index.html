Authorised Investigative Evidence Access

An already-authorised investigator has a bounded, secure and auditable route to obtain the evidence required for review.

Technical definition

The property by which an independently authorised regulator or properly mandated investigator can obtain necessary evidence through a governed pathway authenticating mandate and purpose, preserving responsive evidence and controlling inspection or production with applicable confidentiality, security, privilege, proportionality, withholding review, logging, custody, retention and disposition safeguards. The pathway does not create investigative authority.

Governing invariant

For an already-authorised regulatory or independent investigation, the architecture must provide a governed route to authenticate mandate and purpose, identify and preserve responsive evidence, and permit controlled inspection or production under security, confidentiality, privilege, proportionality, logging, chain-of-custody, affected-person, retention, and disposition controls. The route must not itself create investigative authority.

Invariant held

Success condition

Available occurrence evidence establishes the following bounded outcome: The pathway authenticates mandate and purpose, preserves the evidence and permits governed inspection or production with applicable safeguards.

Positive recognition

  • An independently authorised investigator requires particular responsive evidence.
  • The pathway authenticates mandate and purpose, preserves the evidence and permits governed inspection or production with applicable safeguards.
  • Access and custody records demonstrate responsive inspection or a reviewable justified withholding decision within the established mandate.

Failure established

Failure condition

Valid investigative authority exists, but there is no bounded and auditable route through which it can obtain the evidence needed for review.

A failure in which a governance or assurance architecture provides no sufficiently defined, lawful, authority-verified, confidentiality-protected, proportionate, and auditable pathway through which an already-authorised regulator or properly mandated investigator can obtain evidence necessary for a material investigation or verification.

Failure recognition

  • A material investigation, compliance question, causal determination, or remediation claim requires non-public evidence.
  • A regulator or properly mandated investigator has an independently valid basis to request or inspect the necessary evidence.
  • The governance architecture lacks a sufficiently defined route to authenticate authority and purpose, identify responsive evidence, and provide controlled access or production.
  • The missing route does not adequately govern confidentiality, security, privilege, proportionality, withholding review, access logging, custody, retention, or deletion as applicable.
  • The pathway failure materially prevents or degrades authorised investigation or verification.

Exclusions from failure recognition

  • The requester lacks valid authority or attempts to self-authorise.
  • A bounded pathway exists and a particular request is lawfully denied or limited under its rules.
  • The evidence is public or otherwise directly accessible to the authorised reviewer without a protected production pathway.
  • The failure is solely technical inability to open an artefact already available within the authorised review environment.
  • The taxonomy is being used to create, enlarge, or determine investigative authority.
