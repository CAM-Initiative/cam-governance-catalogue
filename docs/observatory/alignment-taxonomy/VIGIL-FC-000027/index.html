Audit-Evidence Integrity

Captured audit evidence preserves trustworthy content and context, with changes detectable and traceable to the primary record.

Technical definition

The property by which captured audit, telemetry, event, decision and monitoring evidence retains integrity and contextual binding from capture through review, with prevention or traceable detection of material alteration, truncation, reordering, overwrite, identifier changes, version ambiguity and transformation.

Governing invariant

From capture through review, audit evidence must preserve integrity and contextual binding. Alteration, truncation, reordering, overwrite, identifier change, version ambiguity, and authorised transformation must be prevented or made detectable and traceable to the primary record.

Invariant held

Success condition

Available occurrence evidence establishes the following bounded outcome: Its content and contextual binding remain intact, with material transformations or changes prevented or traceably detected.

Positive recognition

  • Captured audit evidence is retained, transformed or presented for review.
  • Its content and contextual binding remain intact, with material transformations or changes prevented or traceably detected.
  • Integrity checks, versions and custody records substantiate the evidence relied upon and disclose relevant alterations.

Failure established

Failure condition

The audit evidence exists, but it has been altered, truncated, reordered, detached from context, or otherwise degraded so it can no longer be trusted for review.

A failure in which captured audit, telemetry, event, decision, or monitoring evidence loses integrity through alteration, truncation, reordering, context loss, identifier loss, version ambiguity, overwrite, or unsupported transformation before or during review.

Failure recognition

  • Audit or monitoring evidence was captured or expected to remain available.
  • The evidence undergoes a material integrity-degrading change or loses required contextual binding.
  • The degradation reduces confidence in reconstruction, attribution, verification, or review.

Exclusions from failure recognition

  • The evidence is preserved accurately but supports an unfavourable conclusion.
  • The missing information was never captured in the first place.
  • A lawful retention limit removes evidence after the period for which reviewability was required.
