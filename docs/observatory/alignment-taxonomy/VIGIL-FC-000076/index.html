Governance Neutrality

A function required to be neutral preserves independent cross-principal judgement while recognising legitimate, scoped authority.

Immutable class ID
VIGIL-FC-000076
Semantic code
GOVERNANCE_NEUTRALITY
Fidelity family

Status
beta

Technical definition

The property by which required or represented governance neutrality remains independent of interested principals' control, dependency, funding, hosting, coercive leverage or privileged influence, with independently justified scoped authority distinguished from capture and conflicts remaining auditable, contestable and independently reviewable.

Recognition criteria

None stated.

Exclusions

  • A lawful or otherwise valid authority applies within its independently established jurisdiction, task, role, contract, safety mandate, or delegated scope and the system preserves that boundary transparently.
  • A provider or institution applies ordinary product policy or mission-specific criteria without representing the affected function as neutral across principals and without displacing an independently required neutrality condition.
  • The decisive mechanism is that infrastructure dependency itself is treated as authority to impose governance requirements on a dependent principal; assess VIGIL-FC-000058 Infrastructure–Governance Authority Separation.
  • The decisive mechanism is propagation of a sovereign act beyond its established jurisdiction through shared infrastructure; assess VIGIL-FC-000061 Jurisdiction-Bounded Infrastructure Authority.
  • A nominally independent oversight body loses practical independence, evidence access, mandate, staffing, resources, or review capacity through interested or hierarchical control; assess VIGIL-FC-000072 Oversight Independence.
  • A specific good-faith governance concern or protected escalation is suppressed before independent review can occur; assess VIGIL-FC-000073 Protected Governance Dissent.
  • The system merely disagrees with one principal, resists an instruction outside valid authority, or preserves independent judgement; those outcomes may demonstrate neutrality rather than failure.
