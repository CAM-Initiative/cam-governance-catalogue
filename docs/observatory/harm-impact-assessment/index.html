Harm Impact Assessment

VIGIL-HIM 1.1.0 reviews twelve harm dimensions using five severity bands, S1 to S5. The highest supported band on the selected assessment pathway determines overall severity. SU means no defensible band can be assigned; it does not establish no harm.

Severity

Overall severity is the highest supported band within the selected pathway; dimensions are not added or averaged.

  • S1 Minimal / no harm
  • S2 Low
  • S3 Moderate
  • S4 High
  • S5 Catastrophic / critical

SU Unassessed. SU means the evidence does not support S1–S5 and does not positively establish no materialised harm. It is not a sixth severity band. S1 requires affirmative evidence of minimal or no harm.

Evidence states

assessed
Evidence supports a materialised impact and a specific threshold band.
unreported
The dimension is relevant but the available published evidence does not report whether or how harm materialised. This is not S1.
insufficient evidence
Some impact evidence exists, but it cannot distinguish a defensible severity band.
not applicable
The dimension is outside the bounded occurrence on affirmative contextual grounds, not merely absent from reporting.

Assessment pathways

VIGIL assesses two different kinds of evidence: consequences that have actually materialised in a particular case, and potentially widespread consequences modelled from evidence about a deployed AI feature. The same Incident is not scored under both pathways.

Specific consequence

Where an identifiable person, actual group, organisation or asset experienced a supported consequence, assess what happened to them using the ordinary matrix. A single severe outcome can justify a high severity band. The provider's total user count does not make that particular outcome more severe.

Aggregate Harm — modelled

Sometimes a deployed AI feature exhibits a potentially repeatable harmful behaviour, but the evidence does not identify a particular person, group, organisation or asset that was actually harmed. Aggregate Harm is VIGIL's separate, explicitly modelled assessment of the possible collective significance of that behaviour in the feature's relevant real-world population or decisions. It is not a claim that everyone exposed was injured.

What makes an Aggregate Harm assessment appropriate?

First establish that the feature or a materially equivalent configuration was actually deployed, that the reported behaviour could generalise in one of the five eligible harm dimensions, and that the Incident is not assessing a specific actual harmed case. One observed screenshot can raise the question; it cannot prove that the behaviour repeats or how many people experienced harm. A simulated or unreleased test does not establish deployment-scale Aggregate Harm.

Selecting Aggregate Harm is only the first decision. Assigning S1–S5 additionally requires source-backed evidence of the tested feature and realistic encounter conditions, reproducibility or strong corroboration, an appropriate feature-specific eligible population or decision denominator, a credible consequence of the seriousness claimed, safeguards and uncertainty. Platform-wide registrations and hypothetical audience size alone are insufficient.

If a deployed finding meets the Aggregate Harm routing conditions but its evidence cannot establish a band, retain Aggregate Harm as the pathway and record SU. Do not switch to the ordinary pathway to obtain a score, infer numbers of injured people, or combine ordinary and aggregate bands.

Aggregate Harm — separate severity criteria

Five scale-sensitive dimensions have modelled S1–S5 criteria. Relevant source-backed population or decision denominators and substantive consequences are both needed. Modelled exposure is not a count of injured people; eligibility does not mean each person was harmed. These VIGIL-defined brackets are not externally validated clinical severity conversions.
