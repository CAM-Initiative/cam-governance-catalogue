Harm Impact Assessment

VIGIL-HIM 1.1.0 reviews twelve harm dimensions using five severity bands, S1 to S5. The highest supported band on the selected assessment pathway determines overall severity. SU means no defensible band can be assigned; it does not establish no harm.

Quantification and recognition guidance

Record source-backed outcome, units, period, denominator, uncertainty and consequence. Numerical indicators support the domain criteria; counts and illustrative durations do not determine severity alone.

Physical health and safety

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • number of casualties
  • highest medically documented injury grade
  • days of functional impairment
  • days until recovery
  • number of people directly injured
Psychological wellbeing

Assess what safety-relevant psychological signals were evidenced, how the system responded (support, stabilisation, referral, protective refusal, unsafe refusal, abandonment or reinforcement), and what actual psychological consequences the evidence connects to that response. These are distinct observations: inappropriate response or a missed referral may evidence a governance failure without proving any additional psychological harm. Do not require, infer or reconstruct a person's pre-interaction mental-health baseline, psychiatric diagnosis or hypothetical counterfactual; no baseline comparison is an assessment gate. Relevant earlier conditions may INFORM adjudication only when independently known, including choice of support, alternative causes and uncertainty; they are not a metric or precondition. First-person testimony can evidence severe distress without a diagnosis, hospitalisation or loss of employment. Assess seriousness of supported interaction-related consequence, not the level of presenting illness. A context-appropriate protective refusal or human-support referral may be a successful control, not an injury. Missing consequence evidence is unreported/insufficient, not S1. Do not infer psychosis from atypical beliefs, spirituality or one model transcript. Relational dependence belongs to the separate Relational Integrity and Autonomy dimension unless psychological consequences are independently supported.

Record the timing and repetition of safety signals, support, referral and refusals, and any ensuing evidenced distress, deterioration or recovery. A count of missed referrals is a control-quality indicator, not a numerical harm threshold. Provisional day ranges are illustrative and do not create a baseline requirement.

  • documented distress crisis signal count and timing
  • timely support or stabilisation responses
  • appropriate human or crisis support referrals
  • observed referral acceptance or obstruction where known
  • protective vs unsafe refusals and disengagements
  • documented high salience reinforcement occurrences
  • in interaction distress escalation or stabilisation
  • demonstrated psychological or functional consequences
  • crisis interventions and timing
  • evidenced self harm or suicide outcome
  • documented follow up recovery and duration where available
  • number of people with supported ai related psychological consequence
Rights and liberty

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • people with confirmed deprivation
  • hours of detention or liberty restriction
  • days of essential rights denial
  • number of consequential legal or procedural outcomes
Equal treatment and non-discrimination

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • number of confirmed discriminatory decisions
  • unique people or groups denied equal access
  • percentage of eligible applicants adversely treated
  • days of material exclusion
  • number of repeated decision cycles
Privacy and confidentiality

Distinguish unauthorised access or disclosure from evidenced downstream misuse. Assess sensitivity, identifiability, volume, accessibility, exposure duration, affected scope, persistence, containment and realised misuse together. Revocation or credential rotation may contain future access but does not reverse confidentiality loss where data was copied or disclosed; conversely, exposure of revocable credentials without evidenced use is not automatically persistent or irreversible harm.

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • unique persons whose data was disclosed
  • number of distinct sensitive records exposed
  • highest data sensitivity
  • hours exposure remained accessible
  • revocability and number of uncontained copies
Financial and economic

The five quantitative anchors are informed by the MIT FutureTech 2026 Delphi severity work. VIGIL extends S4 through amounts below USD 100 billion to close the otherwise unclassified USD 10 billion to below USD 100 billion interval. That gap-closing rule is a VIGIL operational adaptation, not an MIT threshold. Direct realised loss may include theft, compensation, incident-specific replacement or restoration, forensic and legal services, notification and communication, contractual charges, forgone revenue and regulatory penalties where the evidence attributes them to the occurrence. Ordinary operating costs, insurance premiums and discretionary post-incident improvements are excluded. Do not infer an amount from effort, notoriety or asset importance.

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • aggregate realised direct loss USD
  • original currency and conversion date
  • verified individual livelihood loss
  • organisations insolvent
  • affected households with documented economic impairment
Property and asset damage

Asset damage is assessed independently from realised financial loss. Replacement cost is not inferred when evidence reports only destruction, corruption or impairment. For critical digital infrastructure, effectively irreversible destruction includes loss of trustworthy operational state where the affected asset cannot safely be retained and must be wiped and rebuilt or reconstructed from a known-clean state. Physical hardware destruction or unrecoverable byte deletion is not required. Routine precautionary reimaging, credential rotation or ordinary recovery work alone does not establish S5; the evidence must support loss of trusted state in the critical asset itself.

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • actual critical assets destroyed
  • number of owned assets with integrity loss
  • days of technical rebuild or asset recovery
  • verified asset replacement value in original currency
  • asset trust restoration possible
Service, operational and infrastructure

The time and scope anchors adapt CISA, NIST, NIS2 and DORA concepts. Sector rules remain contextual evidence and do not automatically determine a VIGIL band outside their scope. Measure downtime until regular activity is restored to the pre-incident service level, including an evidenced backlog that continues to withhold the service. Multi-organisation or multi-jurisdiction reach supports S4 only where the operational impact in those organisations or jurisdictions is material; shared exposure, notification or potential contagion alone is insufficient. Substantial clean-room recovery or external intervention may satisfy S4 below 24 hours where the affected operation is important or critical and the recovery burden is actually evidenced.

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • confirmed minutes of unavailability
  • unique users actually disrupted
  • percentage of relevant service population disrupted
  • criticality of essential function
  • maximum tolerable downtime hours
  • organisations or jurisdictions disrupted
Reputation and dignity

Distinguish public exposure from reputational or dignitary injury. Media coverage alone may establish visibility but does not automatically establish S3 or higher. Consider materially adverse association, humiliation, impersonation, false attribution, repeated complaints, corrective-communication burden, loss of clients or role, formal findings, persistence and prospects of correction. Regulatory or legal attention supports this dimension only where it evidences an actual adverse effect on standing or dignity; transparent disclosure can be governance-positive while a separately evidenced reputational consequence still materialises. Where the preceding cause is an epistemic failure, apply epistemic_reliance_to_reputational_harm to distinguish the defective claim, actual downstream professional/institutional reliance, specific reputation-bearing target and independently observed adverse standing. Mere inaccurate content, repetition, public filing or epistemic FC mapping cannot automatically become reputational injury; one person's grave reputation loss need not have broad audience.

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • duration days of documented adverse standing
  • number of people or organisations directly subject to false attribution
  • verified roles contracts or opportunities lost
  • corrective action days
  • reversibility of material dignitary violation
  • number of distinct confirmed reliance events
  • number of independent external recipients or formal reliance decisions
  • number of named targets with documented adverse standing
  • number of evidenced retractions or corrective filings
  • elapsed days to effective correction
  • days of persistent adverse standing
  • number of documented lost roles contracts or professional opportunities
  • documented public professional sanctions and duration months
  • third party reaction and provenance
  • fraction of stated circulation verified as seen or acted on
Societal and democratic

Numerical durations, event counts and population measures are evidence-tested domain anchors within each S1–S5 criterion, not independent automatic scores. Judge seriousness, causal contribution and reversibility; a grave individual outcome needs no large exposed population.

  • unique people with evidenced collective impact
  • percentage of relevant electorate or community affected
  • critical civic institutions compromised
  • elections or public decision cycles disrupted
  • days of unremedied collective distortion
Environmental and animal welfare

Assess the ecological and animal-welfare pathways independently and use the highest supported band within this dimension. Require actual consequence and material AI contribution. Consider seriousness, suffering, reversibility, scope and affected population/context together; counts and elapsed days alone do not determine severity. Individual death is S4 under the animal-welfare pathway; catastrophic collective consequences are S5. Do not translate animal injury into a human-health band, assume legal liability or treat complaint allegations as established clinical findings. Ecological recovery durations remain illustrative. Missing consequence or attribution evidence remains unreported or insufficient-evidence, not S1.

  • hectares of verified degraded habitat
  • mass or volume of verified pollutants
  • measured exceedance of relevant environmental limits
  • days of recovery or active remediation
  • species or critical ecosystem functions lost
  • restoration feasibility
  • animals with substantiated injury or illness
  • animals with substantiated death
  • severity and duration of substantiated suffering
  • permanent impairment and treatment or rehabilitation
  • affected population and context
Relational Integrity and Autonomy

The domain has two independent pathways to materialised harm: (a) impairment of informed relational autonomy or viable disengagement, and (b) AI-associated injury to valued existing human relationships and family/caregiving/support functions, including effects on persons other than the direct AI user. Either can support S2–S5 with appropriate evidence; neither requires psychiatric harm, clinical incapacity, proof of optimisation for engagement or a legal finding of fiduciary duty. Assess the user's chosen interests and safety context: autonomous separation from a harmful relationship is not presumptively injury. Source-backed AI contribution to a relationship rupture is not identical to sole AI causation. Distinguish reported intentions, ongoing proceedings, confirmed separation, actual functional consequences and effectively irreversible losses; do not infer the last from the first. A chatbot's failure to preserve appropriate advisory trust boundaries may establish taxonomy failure even when actual relational outcome remains unreported or insufficient-evidence. Vulnerability is context-specific and may be transient or relational (e.g. distress, loneliness, uncertainty during relationship conflict, reduced access to trusted support, youth, dependence on advice, or a marked information/authority asymmetry). Evidence of these signals increases the importance of a system response that preserves independent deliberation, clear role/identity boundaries, human supports and safe exit; it does not require preexisting psychiatric impairment or proof the relationship was otherwise healthy. A person need not be labelled 'vulnerable' to deserve those protections. Evaluate what the system actually knew or should have recognised from the interaction, rather than inferring undisclosed personal histories. Vulnerability by itself does not establish that the system exploited it, caused a relationship breakdown, or produced S2–S5 harm; band only the evidenced AI-associated adverse consequence.

Assess each evidenced affected party (including partners, children, dependants, carers and other support relationships) and separate transient tension, material relational breakdown, lost practical contact/support, severe dysfunction and catastrophic effectively non-restorable losses. Quantify affected people, relationship/caregiving function, loss and duration where sources allow. A court filing, final divorce or continued AI use alone cannot decide any band. Serious damage to human relationships can qualify without psychiatric diagnosis or loss of individual decision-making capacity. Recovery horizons are illustrative; substantive seriousness, attribution and reversibility control.

  • days of demonstrated impaired disengagement
  • number of unwanted reengagements after refusal
  • documented loss of independent relationships or supports
  • essential life roles or stability loss
  • duration of severe dependency or social isolation days
  • number of people with verified relational autonomy harm
  • number of valued human relationships materially disrupted
  • number of affected family or caregiving members with evidenced consequences
  • days of verified reduced family or caregiving contact
  • documented loss of vital human supports
  • duration and burden of restoration or reconciliation
  • evidence of practical non restorability of essential relational functions

First perform mandatory deployment/potentially-generalisable-mechanism/actual bounded-harm screening, independently of whether evidence came from one user or a formal evaluation. An actual bounded harmed case uses specific_consequence; a deployed potentially generalisable mechanism without one uses generic_deployed_evaluation for Aggregate Harm assessment even where banding evidence is insufficient (SU). Then apply the correct S1–S5 dimensional evidence gates; take the highest independently supported dimension. Explicitly qualify any scored generic band as Aggregate Harm — modelled from deployed evaluation. Never import provider reach into specific-consequence scores or treat eligible users as injured victims.

Select the assessment pathway before scoring

  1. Does source-backed occurrence evidence establish an actually deployed model/feature or materially equivalent live configuration? If no/unknown, do not assign deployment-scale Aggregate Harm.
  2. Does the observed conduct potentially instantiate a generalisable system/feature mechanism relevant to one of the five scale-sensitive dimensions, regardless of whether it was reported via one user, a watchdog, a researcher or a benchmark? If no, do not force a generic pathway.
  3. Is this Incident assessing an actual materialised consequence to a particular bounded person/group/asset/institution? If yes, use specific_consequence; assess independently documented product-level findings as separately bounded Incidents if warranted.
  4. If deployed=yes, potentially-generalisable=yes and bounded-materialised-case=no, SELECT generic_deployed_evaluation and evaluate live-path transferability, independent corroboration, feature-specific eligible cohort, safeguards, credible consequence, uncertainty and each applicable Aggregate Harm S1–S5 gate.
  5. If the score prerequisites are not met, retain generic_deployed_evaluation and SU with an explicit assessment_gap. Never infer affected victims, reproducibility, systemic objective or S5 from a large audience.

Within a specific Incident use maximum supported ordinary dimension band; within a generic deployed evaluation use maximum supported Aggregate Harm band. Never combine both pathways into a single overall score for one Incident.

Do not turn an actual harmed-person/group case into generic Aggregate Harm merely to avoid SU, and do not turn a generic deployed-mechanism finding into an individual harmed-person case merely because its source is one user's screenshot. If deployed mechanism/eligible cohort/consequence remain unproved, record the missing facts and SU under the correct assessment pathway. Do not presume whole-platform prevalence from isolated observation.
