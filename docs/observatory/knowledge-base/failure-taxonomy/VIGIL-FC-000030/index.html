Material Signal Integration

Important oversight signals exist, but they are split across disconnected views so their combined meaning cannot be assembled in time for the relevant oversight or intervention.

Technical definition

A failure in which individually captured material monitoring, control, or execution signals remain distributed across disconnected identifiers, records, channels, or review surfaces so that their combined meaning cannot be assembled with sufficient timeliness for the relevant oversight or intervention purpose.

Recognition criteria

  • Multiple material signals relevant to one event, pathway, or risk pattern individually exist.
  • Effective oversight or intervention requires those signals to be correlated or viewed together within a relevant time window.
  • Distribution across disconnected records, channels, identifiers, or review surfaces prevents sufficiently timely synthesis for that oversight or intervention purpose.

Exclusions

  • The signals can be reliably correlated with sufficient timeliness through an available identifier or review process.
  • A required event signal was never captured; use Material Event Capture.
  • The evidence is assembled but has been altered or corrupted.
  • The issue is retrospective inability to reconstruct the required pathway with adequate confidence rather than timely integration for oversight or intervention; use Audit-Trail Reconstructability.

  • OpenTelemetry · 2026-08-10 · authoritative guidance

    Context propagation

    Evidence note. OpenTelemetry documents propagation of trace and span context across service, process, and network boundaries so independently generated traces, logs, metrics, and related signals can be correlated as part of one causal flow. This directly supports the identifier and cross-boundary correlation component of the mechanism; OpenTelemetry does not define VIGIL's broader governance failure class or establish its oversight, intervention, or timeliness thresholds.

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology · 2023-01-26 · authoritative guidance

    NIST AI RMF 1.0, MEASURE 2.4

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-809CCACFF672EFB9 requires monitoring AI-system and component functionality and behaviour in production. This supports availability of material operational signals for oversight; it does not itself define the cross-signal correlation failure required by VIGIL.

  • World Wide Web Consortium · 2021-11-23 · standards evidence

    Trace Context

    Evidence note. The W3C Trace Context Recommendation standardizes propagation of request context across distributed services so related activity can be correlated end-to-end. This independently supports preservation of materially related signals across component boundaries; VIGIL's class additionally requires fragmentation that impairs governance interpretation or response.
