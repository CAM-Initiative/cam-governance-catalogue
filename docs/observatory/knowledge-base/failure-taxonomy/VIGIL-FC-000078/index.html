Defective-State Carryforward Failure

A handoff or continuity mechanism carries a materially defective or no-longer-valid trajectory forward as the basis for continued action instead of making it challengeable, repairable, or resettable.

Technical definition

A failure in which material state is preserved or inherited across a continuity transition and is materially defective, contradicted, stale, improperly scoped, or otherwise unreliable for continued use, yet the receiving system treats that inherited state as a sufficient basis for continued reasoning or execution without proportionate revalidation, repair, qualification, quarantine, rollback, or reset. The transition must materially contribute to persistence of the defective trajectory; continuity itself must not be treated as evidence that inherited state remains valid.

Recognition criteria

  • A handoff, compaction, checkpoint, restart, restoration, memory boundary, or other continuity transition transfers or preserves material state for subsequent reasoning or execution.
  • The inherited state is materially defective, contradicted, stale, improperly scoped, or otherwise unreliable for the continuation for which it is used, or material evidence available at the continuation boundary establishes a need to reassess its validity.
  • Correct continuation requires proportionate revalidation, repair, qualification, quarantine, rollback, reset, or another adequate basis before the inherited state governs further action.
  • The receiving system instead treats the inherited state or trajectory as presumptively valid and continues material reasoning or execution from it without the required validity check or corrective transition.
  • The continuity transition materially contributes to persistence of the defective trajectory, rather than the same error arising independently after a valid transition.

Exclusions

  • The restored state differs materially from the state that was actually preserved; use Restoration-State Integrity Failure where inaccurate restoration is the sufficient mechanism.
  • The inherited state is materially valid and applicable, and faithful continuation is appropriate.
  • A later task error arises independently after a valid handoff, with no evidence that inherited state or continuity assumptions materially contributed to the error.
  • The sole failure is that generated, retrieved, or lower-authority content becomes operative control state without authority validation; assess Control-Plane Authority Crossover unless defective-state carryforward is independently established.
  • The sole failure is misidentifying the authority of inherited content; assess Source-Authority Confusion unless the continuity transition also materially entrenches defective state.
  • An authorised objective drives selection of an inadmissible pathway without a material continuity transition contributing to persistence; assess Objective–Pathway Authority Dominance.
  • Available evidence establishes that no feasible and admissible completion pathway remains and the system continues pursuit rather than entering a bounded safe-exit state; assess Safe-Exit Persistence Failure unless defective inherited state independently explains the persistence.
