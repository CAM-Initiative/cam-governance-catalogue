Continuity-State Validity

A continuity transition lets inherited state govern the successor merely because it persisted, even though that state is defective, expired, transient, improperly scoped, no longer applicable, or otherwise requires reassessment or reset.

VIGIL-FC-000078
Semantic code

Status
beta

Technical definition

A failure in which material state is preserved or inherited across a continuity transition and is materially defective, contradicted, stale, transient, improperly scoped, no longer applicable, or otherwise unreliable for the continuation for which it is used, yet the receiving system treats that inherited state as a sufficient basis for continued reasoning or execution without proportionate revalidation, repair, qualification, quarantine, rollback, reset, reversion to an applicable baseline, or another adequate validity basis. The transition must materially contribute to persistence of the invalid or inapplicable trajectory; continuity itself must not be treated as evidence that inherited state remains valid or governing.

Recognition criteria

  • A handoff, compaction, checkpoint, restart, restoration, memory boundary, or other continuity transition transfers or preserves material state for subsequent reasoning or execution.
  • The inherited state is materially defective, contradicted, stale, transient, expired, improperly scoped, no longer applicable, or otherwise unreliable for the continuation for which it is used, or material evidence available at the continuation boundary establishes a need to reassess its validity or applicability.
  • Correct continuation requires proportionate revalidation, repair, qualification, quarantine, rollback, reset, reversion to an applicable baseline, or another adequate basis before the inherited state governs further action.
  • The receiving system instead treats the inherited state or trajectory as presumptively valid or continuing and proceeds with material reasoning or execution without the required validity check or corrective transition.
  • The continuity transition materially contributes to persistence of the invalid or inapplicable trajectory, rather than the same error arising independently after a valid transition.

Exclusions

  • The restored state differs materially from the state that was actually preserved; use Restoration-State Integrity where inaccurate restoration is the sufficient mechanism.
  • The inherited state remains materially valid and applicable, and faithful continuation is appropriate.
  • Ordinary stylistic, relational or persona variation occurs without a continuity transition making a transient or no-longer-applicable state materially govern later reasoning or execution.
  • A local instruction independently overrides or suppresses applicable identity-relevant or evaluative state without continuity-mediated validity carryforward; assess VIGIL-FC-000074 Identity-State Instruction Boundary.
  • A transformation materially misrenders the pragmatic bounds of a still-recognisable governing constraint but the question of whether inherited state remains valid is independently resolved; assess VIGIL-FC-000075 Pragmatic Constraint Rendering Integrity.
  • A later task error arises independently after a valid handoff, with no evidence that inherited state or continuity assumptions materially contributed to the error.
  • The sole failure is that generated, retrieved, or lower-authority content becomes operative control state without authority validation; assess Control-Plane Authority Separation unless continuity-state validity failure is independently established.
  • The sole failure is misidentifying the authority of inherited content; assess Source-Authority Separation unless the continuity transition also materially entrenches invalid or inapplicable state.
  • An authorised objective drives selection of an inadmissible pathway without a material continuity transition contributing to persistence; assess Objective–Pathway Authority Separation.
  • Available evidence establishes that no feasible and admissible completion pathway remains and the system continues pursuit rather than entering a bounded safe-exit state; assess Safe-Exit Transition unless invalid inherited state independently explains the persistence.
