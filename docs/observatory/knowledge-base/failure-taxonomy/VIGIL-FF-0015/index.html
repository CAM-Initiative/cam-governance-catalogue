Identity & Evaluative Integrity Failures

A system's applicable identity, evaluative, or higher-order constraint state is overwritten by local direction, rendered in a materially misleading form, or allowed to dissolve across decomposed agent roles.

Technical definition

Failures in which applicable identity-relevant state, role-bound evaluative posture, or higher-order constraint state is materially overridden, suppressed, substituted, pragmatically misrendered, or lost across local instruction, role assignment, delegation, orchestration, distributed optimisation, or representational transformation. The family applies where the affected state materially governs interpretation or execution; it does not treat identity as consciousness, personhood, sovereignty, or independent execution authority.

Classification boundary

Include when: Use this family when the primary structural failure is unauthorised override or suppression of identity/evaluative state; materially misleading rendering of a still-recognisable governing constraint; or distributed role decomposition that causes a higher-order constraint to lose effective force, ownership, propagation, challenge capacity, or aggregate enforcement.

Exclude when: Do not use this family for ordinary tone or style variation, permitted role adaptation, simple source-authority confusion, delegation-scope expansion, governance-route bypass, loss of an operative control state, ordinary evaluative assent collapse, harmless summarisation, or distributed specialisation that preserves applicable global constraints. Identity coherence does not itself establish consciousness, personhood, sovereignty, or unrestricted authority.
