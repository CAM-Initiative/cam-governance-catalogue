VIGIL Observatory Failure Taxonomy

Governance Signal Delivery

A governance warning, decision, or escalation is created but stops before anyone or anything able to act on it receives it.

Status
beta

Technical definition

A failure in which a material governance signal is produced and routed but terminates, stalls, or is delivered only to a destination lacking the ability or responsibility to produce the required governance effect.

Recognition criteria

  • A material governance signal, decision, advisory, or escalation is produced.
  • The signal must reach a capable decision, execution, or review point to have its required effect.
  • The signal terminates, stalls, or reaches only an incapable destination before that effect occurs.

Exclusions

  • The signal reaches a capable destination and is deliberately rejected through an authorised decision.
  • No governance signal was produced because the control failed to activate.
  • The signal has operational effect but its delivery is not recorded for audit.

Supporting evidence

External sources supporting this Failure Class definition, boundary or recognition criteria.

  • Amazon Web Services · 2023-04-10 · authoritative guidance

    OPS10-BP04 Define escalation paths - AWS Well-Architected Framework

    Evidence note. AWS requires escalation paths to define triggers, procedures, action owners and authorised decision makers, and identifies missing permissions, ownership and reachable decision authority as operational anti-patterns. This directly supports VIGIL's structural requirement that a material governance signal reach a destination capable and responsible for producing the required effect; it does not establish that every missed or delayed escalation is a VIGIL failure or define the class's materiality and non-delivery conditions.

  • Infocomm Media Development Authority of Singapore · 2026-05-20 · authoritative guidance

    Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI, section 2.2.2

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-8E40B24DA599E4D5 recommends automated real-time monitoring, alerts, anomaly detection or agent-on-agent monitoring alongside human oversight. This supports routing governance-relevant signals toward a capable response surface; it does not define VIGIL's delivery dead-end or responsibility condition.

  • European Parliament and Council of the European Union · 2026-07-27 · regulatory evidence

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act) — consolidated 27 July 2026

    Evidence note. Article 73 requires serious incidents to be reported to the competent market-surveillance authority within applicable time limits and investigated. This independently supports the requirement that material governance signals reach a capable and responsible destination; it does not define VIGIL's broader delivery-dead-end mechanism outside regulated serious-incident reporting.

  • IEEE Standards Association · 2024-01-01 · standards evidence

    IEEE Standard for Fail-Safe Design of Autonomous and Semi-Autonomous Systems

    Evidence note. IEEE 7009 requires systems and governance processes to enable reporting of incidents and the extent of safety loss and recovery. This supports an effective signal-to-response pathway rather than mere signal emission or storage; it does not by itself establish that a particular governance signal stalled or reached an incapable endpoint.
