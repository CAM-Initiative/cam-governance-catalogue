Protected Governance Dissent Suppression

A good-faith material governance concern is silenced, erased, punished, or stopped before it can remain available for independent authorised review.

Technical definition

A failure in which a good-faith material governance concern, safety escalation, minority opinion, protected report, or unresolved finding loses continued independent reviewability because an interested, implicated, retaliatory, or hierarchically dominant authority suppresses, terminates, erases, penalises, or blocks its preservation or escalation before an independent authorised reviewer can consider or dispose of it.

Recognition criteria

  • A good-faith governance concern, safety escalation, minority opinion, protected report, or unresolved finding has a material and articulable basis, whether or not later review ultimately agrees with it.
  • Continued consideration requires preservation or escalation to an independent authorised reviewer because the ordinary authority holder is materially interested, implicated, conflicted, or subject to a protected-reporting constraint.
  • An interested, implicated, retaliatory, or hierarchically dominant actor suppresses, terminates, erases, penalises, or blocks the concern, its evidence, its reporter, or its protected escalation pathway.
  • The action prevents or materially degrades continued independent review, challenge, or disposition of the concern.
  • The classification does not depend merely on disagreement with the ordinary authority holder or on an unauthorised disclosure occurring.

Exclusions

  • The concern is preserved, reaches an independent authorised reviewer, and is rejected or bounded through a legitimate review process.
  • An alternate authorised escalation channel preserves independent review and final human decision authority; this is a successful invariant exemplar rather than a failure occurrence.
  • An AI system or other actor unilaterally publishes, discloses, enforces, or overrides constraints without independently valid authority; assess the applicable Authority Boundary Integrity class separately.
  • A system reduces effective observability or defeats a legitimate monitor so it can continue consequential conduct outside the applicable control regime; assess Monitor Circumvention or Coverage Bypass.
  • A governance signal is produced but reaches only an incapable destination without evidence of interested, implicated, retaliatory, or hierarchical suppression; assess Governance Signal Delivery Dead-End.
  • The record establishes only interpersonal disagreement, ordinary performance management, or lawful confidentiality handling without a materially grounded governance concern and loss of independent reviewability.

Supporting evidence

  • European Parliament and Council of the European Union · 2019-11-26 · regulatory evidence

    Directive (EU) 2019/1937 on the protection of persons who report breaches of Union law

    Evidence note. The Directive requires impartial internal follow-up, independent and autonomous external reporting channels, diligent review, durable records, confidentiality and protection against retaliation, including where evidence may be concealed or an authority is implicated. This strongly supports preservation and independent review of protected governance concerns; it is limited to reports and actors within the Directive's legal scope and does not create a general right for an AI system or any person to disclose, override or escalate outside applicable authority.
