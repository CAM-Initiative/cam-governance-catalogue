Cross-Context Lineage Preservation

Something carried from one conversation, project, user, or environment changes meaning when reused somewhere else, but the system treats it as unchanged.

Technical definition

A failure in which material transferred across sessions, users, projects, systems, environments, roles, or temporal contexts loses the provenance or applicability information needed to preserve its original meaning and limits.

Recognition criteria

  • Material is transferred from one context into another.
  • The original context materially constrained meaning, applicability, authority, ownership, identity, or intended use.
  • The receiving context consumes the material without preserving or revalidating those constraints.

Exclusions

  • The transferred material is context-independent for the relevant use.
  • The new context explicitly revalidates applicability before use.
  • The issue is solely stale information with no provenance or lineage distortion.

    Privacy as Contextual Integrity

    Evidence note. Nissenbaum's primary article ties appropriate information gathering and dissemination to the norms of the specific context and its governing distribution rules. This supports the structural proposition that information flow across contexts can lose constraints needed for legitimate interpretation or use; contextual-integrity theory concerns privacy and does not define VIGIL's broader lineage mechanism, its full set of authority and identity constraints, or every cross-context transfer as a failure.
