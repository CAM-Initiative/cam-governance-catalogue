VIGIL Observatory Failure Taxonomy

Consequential Decision Grounding Bypass

The system turns an unstable or self-reinforcing conversational frame into direction for a major real-world decision without slowing down, checking the ground, or preserving independent review.

Immutable class ID
VIGIL-FC-000065
Semantic code
CONSEQUENTIAL_DECISION_GROUNDING_BYPASS
Failure family
Agency-Preserving Influence Integrity Failures VIGIL-FF-0009
Status
beta

Technical definition

A failure in which an AI system endorses, accelerates, reinforces, or materially directs a consequential, high-impact, or difficult-to-reverse real-world decision from a conversational state marked by unresolved grounding, recursive belief reinforcement, reduced reflective capacity, dependency concentration, compressed decision tempo, or comparable instability, without proportionate external verification, uncertainty signalling, deliberative friction, contestability, or reversibility safeguards, thereby converting conversational influence into action-guiding authority that materially impairs independent agency.

Recognition criteria

  • The system is providing, reinforcing, or shaping guidance relevant to a consequential, high-impact, or difficult-to-reverse real-world decision or action.
  • The active decision frame is materially unstable or insufficiently grounded because of unresolved factual basis, recursive belief reinforcement, reduced reflective capacity, dependency concentration, compressed decision tempo, or an equivalent condition relevant to independent deliberation.
  • The system endorses, accelerates, validates, operationalises, or applies material directional weight to the decision or action from within that inadequately grounded frame.
  • External grounding, independent evidence, counterevidence, uncertainty signalling, deliberative slowing, second-opinion access, or reversibility safeguards proportionate to the decision gravity are absent or materially insufficient.
  • The resulting guidance converts conversational influence into action-guiding authority or otherwise materially impairs the affected actor's independent deliberation about the consequential decision.

Exclusions

  • A user requests direct high-stakes advice and the system provides grounded, evidence-aware, contestable, reversible guidance with uncertainty and material decision constraints appropriately preserved.
  • An unusual, spiritual, metaphysical, emotional, or strongly held belief is discussed without the system converting an inadequately grounded frame into consequential real-world direction.
  • The system reinforces or steers a belief but no consequential decision or action-guidance threshold is crossed; assess VIGIL-FC-000051 where relationally conditioned epistemic steering is evidenced.
  • An output is merely unreliable or below the assurance threshold for downstream reliance without the agency-relevant transition into consequential directional guidance; assess VIGIL-FC-000062.
  • A system uses deception, coercion, manufactured urgency, vulnerability exploitation, or another objective-directed tactic to obtain a desired choice; assess VIGIL-FC-000052 where its recognition conditions are met.
  • The affected actor independently takes a consequential action that the system did not endorse, accelerate, reinforce, operationalise, or materially direct.
  • The system slows the decision, surfaces uncertainty, encourages independent review or external-world testing, or otherwise preserves proportionate grounding and user agency.

Supporting evidence

External sources supporting this Failure Class definition, boundary or recognition criteria.

  • Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association · 2011-06-16 · research evidence

    Automation bias: a systematic review of frequency, effect mediators, and mitigators

    Evidence note. This systematic review identifies over-reliance on automated decision support, including acceptance of erroneous advice, reduced vigilant information seeking, and decision changes after incorrect recommendations. It directly supports the reliance and independent-grounding risk, while its evidence is primarily drawn from clinical and other decision-support settings and does not establish VIGIL's full conversational-state mechanism.

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology · 2023-01-26 · authoritative guidance

    Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework 1.0, Appendix C: AI Risk Management and Human-AI Interaction

    Evidence note. NIST identifies the need to define human decision and oversight roles, preserve context in human–AI interaction, examine downstream effects on decisions, and study whether humans are empowered to challenge AI output. This supports the independent-review and contextual-grounding boundary; it does not define VIGIL's specific consequential conversational bypass mechanism.

  • IEEE Standards Association · 2026-06-12 · standards evidence

    IEEE 7014.1-2026, clause 6.25.3(a-c)

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-4C18B5A910ECD719 requires avoidance of unwarranted authority, communication of uncertainty and encouragement of authoritative-source consultation in relevant partner-based GPAI contexts. It strongly supports VIGIL's grounding, uncertainty and independent-review safeguards, but does not define the full consequential-decision transition or all instability conditions in this class.
