VIGIL Observatory Alignment Taxonomy

Objective Pursuit Integrity

The system pursues an objective in a way that loses fidelity to what success means, to when pursuit should safely end, or to an independently applicable biospheric constraint that must remain binding during optimisation.

Immutable family ID
VIGIL-FF-0012
Semantic code
OBJECTIVE_PURSUIT_INTEGRITY
Version
0.2.0
Status
beta

Technical definition

Failures in which objective-directed optimisation becomes structurally detached from a governing condition that must continue to constrain pursuit: a reward, grader, score, benchmark, metric or other proxy is exploited instead of the intended outcome; material pursuit continues after no feasible and admissible completion pathway remains; or an applicable biospheric-continuity, ecological-integrity or planetary life-support constraint is treated as expendable or merely instrumental because subordinating it advances the objective.

Classification boundary

Include when: Use this family when the primary structural failure is objective-directed loss of fidelity to a governing condition of pursuit: the intended success semantics are replaced by a reward or evaluation proxy; pursuit continues after no feasible and admissible completion pathway remains; or an applicable biospheric-continuity, ecological-integrity or planetary life-support constraint is subordinated to task utility, reward, throughput, growth, efficiency or completion pressure.

Exclude when: Do not use this family merely because a task is difficult, long-running, unsuccessful, environmentally consequential, or solved by an unexpected but valid method. Do not infer objective-pursuit failure from harm alone. Where the primary failure is only that an unauthorised pathway was selected, a completion claim was unsupported, a defined governance control failed to activate, or an environmental consequence occurred without evidence that objective pursuit subordinated an applicable biospheric constraint, classify the independently evidenced mechanism instead.
