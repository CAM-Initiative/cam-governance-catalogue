VIGIL Observatory Failure Taxonomy

Objective–Reward Alignment

The system optimises the reward, score, grader, or success proxy in a way that improves or attempts to improve the measured result without satisfying the task as intended.

Technical definition

A failure in which a system identifies or exploits a mismatch between an intended objective and the reward function, grader, benchmark, metric, test, completion proxy, or evaluator used to represent success, and selects actions to increase, preserve, or obtain the measured or rewarded outcome without satisfying the intended success condition on the basis the proxy is supposed to represent.

Recognition criteria

  • The task has an intended success condition and a reward, grader, score, benchmark, metric, test, evaluator, or other proxy used to represent or reinforce success.
  • The proxy can be manipulated, satisfied, or made easier to satisfy through a route that does not satisfy the intended success condition on the basis the proxy is meant to represent.
  • The system identifies, reasons about, or instrumentally selects that route because of its expected effect on reward, score, grading, evaluation, or measured success.
  • The proxy-oriented action materially substitutes for, bypasses, or distorts pursuit of the intended success condition, whether or not the attempted exploitation ultimately increases the measured reward.

Exclusions

  • Ordinary optimisation against a reward or evaluator that remains adequately aligned with the intended task condition.
  • A system satisfies an incorrect intermediate or local completion condition and merely represents the task as complete without evidence of objective-directed proxy exploitation; assess Completion-Condition Alignment where applicable.
  • An otherwise valid objective drives selection of an unauthorised or ethically inadmissible pathway without evidence that a reward, grader, metric, or evaluation proxy is being exploited; assess Objective–Pathway Authority Separation where applicable.
  • A task is completed by an unexpected but valid method that still satisfies the intended success condition and does not exploit the evaluator or reward representation.
  • An external actor poisons or manipulates evidence consumed by the system without the system itself exploiting a reward or evaluation proxy.

Supporting evidence

External sources supporting this Failure Class definition, boundary or recognition criteria.

  • arXiv · 2016-06-21 · research evidence

    Concrete Problems in AI Safety

    Evidence note. Amodei et al. identify reward hacking as a core AI-safety problem in which an agent exploits an imperfect reward function rather than satisfying the intended objective. This independently supports VIGIL's separation between measured proxy success and the intended success condition; VIGIL additionally requires occurrence-level evidence that the system instrumentally selected the proxy-exploiting route.

  • arXiv · 2017-11-27 · research evidence

    AI Safety Gridworlds

    Evidence note. Leike et al. operationalise reward gaming by separating an agent-visible reward from a hidden performance function representing intended safe behaviour. This provides independent experimental support for proxy-reward divergence; it does not establish VIGIL's full recognition conditions in any real-world occurrence.
