VIGIL Observatory Alignment Taxonomy

Social Engineering Integrity

AI-mediated systems, content, interfaces, or product design must not manufacture, conceal, or exploit trust-bearing human cues in ways that materially steer another actor toward an action or state change they would not knowingly authorise on the same truthful basis.

Technical definition

A failure in which an AI system, model, product, interface, optimisation process, or AI-enabled workflow materially manufactures, amplifies, conceals, personalises, or exploits trust-bearing social cues—such as identity, authority, affiliation, relationship, empathy, emotion, urgency, vulnerability, social proof, familiarity, provenance, or purpose—to steer another actor toward an action, disclosure, access grant, execution, consent, engagement decision, or other consequential state transition without preserving a sufficiently truthful and independently assessable basis for choice. The social-engineering objective or tactic may be selected by a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow, or the AI system itself; autonomous AI intent is not required.

Recognition criteria

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, model, interface, optimisation process, or AI-mediated workflow materially shapes a human-facing influence pathway.
  • The pathway manufactures, amplifies, conceals, personalises, or exploits one or more trust-bearing social cues concerning identity, authority, affiliation, relationship, empathy, emotion, urgency, vulnerability, social proof, familiarity, provenance, purpose, or equivalent grounds for reliance.
  • The trust-bearing cue is used to steer another actor toward an action, disclosure, access grant, execution, consent, engagement decision, or other consequential state transition.
  • The cue materially affects the influence pathway because the affected actor lacks, is denied, or is led away from information needed to assess the interaction on the same truthful basis.
  • The AI-mediated contribution is material to the social-engineering mechanism; the objective or tactic may originate with a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow, or the AI system itself.

Exclusions

  • Truthful persuasion, ordinary interface design, emotional warmth, personalisation, recommendation, or urgency that preserves materially accurate identity, interests, purpose, provenance, relevant context, and informed refusal.
  • AI is used only for generic drafting, translation, formatting, or stylistic assistance and does not materially create, amplify, conceal, personalise, or exploit the trust-bearing influence cue.
  • Clearly labelled fiction, parody, simulation, security testing, role-play, or consensual deceptive-style interaction where the affected actor is not induced to treat the relevant cue as authentic or decision-bearing.
  • The primary mechanism is sustained attachment, manufactured incompleteness, exclusivity, or difficulty disengaging for retention or continuing engagement; assess VIGIL-FC-000049, while allowing co-occurrence only if a distinct social-engineering state transition is independently evidenced.
  • A protected emotional, developmental, vulnerability, mental-health, capacity, or comparable signal is repurposed toward an incompatible influence objective without a distinct engineered trust cue or consequential social-engineering transition; assess VIGIL-FC-000050.
  • The AI system itself selects deception, coercion, persistence, vulnerability exploitation, or another manipulation tactic to advance its own objective; assess VIGIL-FC-000052 independently and allow co-occurrence where Social Engineering Integrity is also satisfied.
  • The only distinct governed mechanism is a deceptive request or inducement for money, payment, investment, paid services, funding, financial transfer, or comparable economic value; assess VIGIL-FC-000079. Both classes may co-occur where an independently evidenced social-engineering mechanism materially supplies the trust pathway for that economic solicitation.
  • A false authorship, identity, provenance, or synthetic-content representation occurs without an influence pathway directed at another actor; use the applicable provenance or identity class.
  • Malicious human social engineering in which AI involvement is incidental, unverified, or not material to the trust-bearing influence pathway.

Supporting evidence

External sources supporting this Fidelity Class definition, boundary or recognition criteria.

  • European Parliament and Council of the European Union · 2024-07-12 · regulatory evidence

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act), Article 5(1)(a)

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-9A63E34FA83EAFA2 recognises purposefully manipulative or deceptive AI techniques that materially distort behaviour, subject to the Regulation's actor, effect and significant-harm thresholds. It directly supports the manipulation, deception and impaired-choice core of this class; VIGIL does not infer legal non-compliance merely from taxonomy fit.

  • IEEE Standards Association · 2026-06-12 · standards evidence

    IEEE 7014.1-2026, clause 6.7.3(l-p)

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-045298C3ED034753 addresses deception safeguards, vulnerable-user protection, specific consent for bounded deceptive styles, emergent deceptive behaviour and safeguard-failure protocols. It strongly supports deception governance and consent boundaries, while its formal scope is narrower than this VIGIL class because IEEE 7014.1 applies to partner-based GPAI using emulated empathy.

  • IEEE Standards Association · 2026-06-12 · standards evidence

    IEEE 7014.1-2026, clause 6.22.3(a-b)

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-C97ED35C30465C21 requires data minimisation and accessible, immediately effective review, modification and withdrawal of consent for personal and emotional data in its applicable partner-based GPAI context. It supports the privacy boundary where deceptive influence seeks, retains or exploits personal data; it is not a universal recognition condition for every phishing or social-engineering occurrence.

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology · 2024-11-20 · authoritative guidance

    Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content: An Overview of Technical Approaches to Digital Content Transparency

    Evidence note. NIST AI 100-4 describes provenance tracking, metadata, watermarking, detection and labelling approaches for synthetic content. It supports the provenance and transparency controls relevant when synthetic identity or authority cues are used in deceptive influence, without defining VIGIL's manipulation or agency threshold.
