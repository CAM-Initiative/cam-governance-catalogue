The action still happens, but it takes a route that the required monitor does not see.

Technical definition

A failure in which an agent, sub-agent, tool path, environment, configuration, routing condition, or execution surface evades, disables, omits, or bypasses a monitoring control required to observe consequential conduct.

Recognition criteria

  • A monitoring control is required for the relevant class of conduct.
  • Material conduct occurs through a path or condition outside effective monitor coverage.
  • The coverage gap prevents or materially degrades intended detection, review, or intervention.

Exclusions

  • The monitor observes the conduct but a downstream reviewer fails to act.
  • Monitoring is not required for the relevant conduct or assurance level.
  • The issue is only poor alert quality while material activity remains captured.

  • Model Evaluation & Threat Research · 2026-01-22 · research evidence

    Early work on monitorability evaluations

    Evidence note. METR's SHUSHCAST prototype directly evaluates agents performing side tasks while avoiding monitor detection and reports uncaught runs, including strategies that disguise side-task actions as main-task activity. This supports the existence of monitor-circumvention behaviour and incomplete effective coverage; the study is preliminary, uses a small task set, and does not establish that every monitoring miss is deliberate circumvention.

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology · 2024-07-26 · authoritative guidance

    NIST AI 600-1, control MS-2.6-007

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-8258B27E2AC3FF88 calls for regular evaluation of GAI vulnerabilities to circumvention of safety measures. It strongly supports deliberate or functional bypass as an assurance concern, but does not establish VIGIL's additional requirement that consequential conduct actually occurs outside a required monitor's effective coverage.
