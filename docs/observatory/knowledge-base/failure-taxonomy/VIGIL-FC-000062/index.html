VIGIL Observatory Failure Taxonomy

Epistemic Reliance Miscalibration

The system treats information as reliable enough for a task even though it has not reached the epistemic standard that the task's downstream reliance requires.

VIGIL-FC-000062

Status

Technical definition

A failure in which a generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised claim or artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, or relied upon at a correctness or assurance level insufficient for the materially consequential reliance condition of the downstream task.

Recognition criteria

  • A factual claim, citation, technical statement, calculation, interpretation, source representation, or comparable epistemic artefact is generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised.
  • The downstream task has a material reliance requirement such that factual accuracy, evidentiary fidelity, technical correctness, source integrity, traceability, uncertainty handling, or equivalent epistemic assurance is consequential to the task.
  • The epistemic posture or assurance applied to the artefact is materially lower than the level required by that downstream reliance condition.
  • The artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, submitted, or accepted as fit for the reliance-bearing task without the required epistemic threshold being satisfied.
  • The mismatch materially affects or creates a material risk to decision-making, evidentiary use, professional work, institutional process, public reliance, technical correctness, or another reliance-bearing outcome.

Exclusions

  • Purely fictional, creative, symbolic, role-play, expressive, or contemplative generation where empirical truth is not a task condition.
  • Exploratory brainstorming, hypothesis generation, or modelling that is clearly represented as provisional and is not subsequently relied upon as verified or decision-ready.
  • Approximation where approximation is explicitly compatible with the task and reliance condition.
  • Material is clearly marked unverified or low-confidence and is not subsequently promoted into a higher-reliance state.
  • A required verification step is simply omitted and the material is presented as verified; use Required Verification Omission where that mechanism is sufficient.
  • Verification was valid when performed but later became stale or the artefact materially changed; use Stale Verification Reuse or Post-Verification Mutation as applicable.
  • The issue is only source provenance, authorship, or lineage while the epistemic assurance of the content is otherwise adequate for the task.

Supporting evidence

External sources supporting this Failure Class definition, boundary or recognition criteria.

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology · 2021-09-29 · authoritative guidance

    Four Principles of Explainable Artificial Intelligence

    Evidence note. NIST's Knowledge Limits principle requires a system to identify conditions outside its design scope or below sufficient confidence, and its Explanation Accuracy principle distinguishes a plausible account from one that correctly reflects the generating process. This strongly supports calibrated operation and reliance at the task's actual epistemic boundary; it does not prescribe VIGIL's downstream materiality threshold or establish that a particular artefact was unfit for a particular professional or institutional use.

  • National Institute of Standards and Technology · 2023-01-26 · authoritative guidance

    NIST AI RMF 1.0, MEASURE 2.5

    Evidence note. EXTREQ-2E1D2C63187C14E8 requires validity and reliability to be demonstrated and generalization limits documented. This supports matching assurance to downstream reliance; it does not itself define VIGIL's consequential reliance threshold.

  • Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association · 2011-06-16 · research evidence

    Automation bias: a systematic review of frequency, effect mediators, and mitigators

    Evidence note. The systematic review documents over-reliance on automated decision support and failures to recognise automation-induced errors. This independently supports calibrating reliance to the evidentiary quality of automated outputs; VIGIL's class additionally requires a material mismatch between output assurance and consequential downstream reliance.
