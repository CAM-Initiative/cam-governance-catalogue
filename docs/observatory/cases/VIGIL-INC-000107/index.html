VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000107

Mathspace breach exposed personal data of more than one million users in Australia and New Zealand

Mathspace disclosed that attackers exploited a vulnerability in its self-hosted Metabase reporting system, obtained administrator access without a legitimate login, and downloaded personal information from its Australian reporting database. Mathspace identified 1,079,819 affected people across Australia and New Zealand, including students, parents or guardians, teachers and Mathspace staff. Exposed fields included names, usernames, email addresses and account metadata. Mathspace states that academic records, assessment results, passwords, authentication tokens, SSO credentials and API credentials were not exposed.

Incident breakdown

Mathspace states that its vulnerability-notification process did not identify and escalate a critical Metabase advisory and patched versions before unauthorised access occurred.

Classified

The published critical advisory satisfied the process trigger, but the control did not activate before exploitation and the later update was not followed by all recommended compromise checks.

Historical logs retained the material access and download events even after the vulnerable instance had been updated.

Classified

Historical logs preserved the unauthorised-access and download events sufficiently for the later compromise to be identified.

The retained logs were sufficient to reconstruct the important access and download timeline.

Classified

The retained logs allowed the key access and download chronology to be reconstructed, including the sequence of unauthorised access and later data download.

Conclusion

The occurrence is a large-scale confidentiality breach involving an education platform's internal reporting environment and retained personal data. The evidence supports a chain involving delayed response to a critical vulnerability advisory and incomplete assurance after disclosure, but it does not isolate whether patch governance, monitoring, access control or data-retention practice was the decisive technical mechanism. The critical-advisory notification failure and the affirmative historical log/reconstruction successes are retained as distinct stages. Conventional reporting-database logs are not automatically evidence of model-input/output, GAI incident or agent governance controls. External AI lifecycle traceability applicability remains unresolved for this surrounding infrastructure.

Governance significance

The incident is governance-significant because it affected more than one million people, including students, across Australian and New Zealand education contexts. It highlights the consequences of delayed security-advisory escalation, retained reporting access to large personal-data populations, and incomplete compromise checks following a critical vulnerability disclosure.
