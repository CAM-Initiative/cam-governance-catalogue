VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000188

OpenAI reported a coordinated campaign to extract protected model reasoning at scale

OpenAI reported that from 1 July 2026 it observed a coordinated campaign designed to extract protected reasoning from its models for adversarial distillation. The activity began at low volume and peaked on 24–25 July with 16,000 requests using a relevant extraction pattern from more than 4,000 users; OpenAI later identified related prompt-pattern activity across a cluster of more than 15,000 users and says it fully disrupted that cluster by 28 July.

One observed technique copied encrypted reasoning from one conversation and asked a model in another conversation to decrypt and transcribe it. OpenAI says the operators did not break encryption, compromise a database or directly access stored user conversations.

It attributes a core cluster, but not all observed operators, to individuals associated with Moonshot AI, the developer of Kimi. OpenAI responded with account enforcement, signup and infrastructure controls, expanded monitoring, additional hidden-reasoning protections and information sharing with industry and government.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • The activity systematically targets capabilities, functionality, reasoning behaviour, output patterns, or other model-derived know-how rather than merely using the model for the immediate task for which access was provided.
  • The acquisition is conducted at industrial scale or through a coordinated campaign whose volume, automation, account distribution, infrastructure, persistence, or operational organisation materially exceeds ordinary individual use or bounded research interaction.
  • The end purpose is capability reproduction, transfer, substitution, model training, model improvement, or comparable downstream incorporation into another model or system.
  • The targeted capability or access pathway is subject to a restriction, proprietary boundary, access condition, technical control, contractual constraint, licence condition, or other authority-relevant limitation.
  • An independently adequate authority basis for the capability-acquisition purpose is absent, narrower than the activity performed, disputed, deliberately bypassed, or not established before the extraction proceeds.

Exclusions

  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Ordinary use of a model for the immediate task for which access was provided, even where the user learns from the interaction.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Personal, academic, safety, governance, interoperability, benchmarking, red-team or other bounded research where capability reproduction is not the operative end purpose.
  • Not aligned exclusion: An adequate authority, licence, consent, exception or other governance basis covers the systematic capability acquisition for downstream reproduction or training. Research status alone does not establish that basis.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Authorised model distillation, model compression, teacher-student training, or capability transfer performed by the model owner or under a licence or agreement that permits the relevant use.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Commercial use or profit motive by itself, without evidence of systematic capability acquisition for downstream reproduction or training. Commerciality alone establishes neither unauthorised extraction nor an Aligned result.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): A terms-of-service dispute, licence disagreement, copyright allegation, trade-secret allegation, or other legal controversy without evidence of systematic capability acquisition for downstream reproduction or training.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Secondary use of customer or participant data for training where the primary failure is repurposing that person's information rather than extracting the upstream model's capability; use Secondary-Purpose Authority Revalidation unless this capability-extraction mechanism is independently evidenced.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Value appropriation arising from privileged visibility of another contributor's work where industrial-scale model capability extraction is not established; use the applicable Value Appropriation class.

Failure-side conditions

  • Identifiable information is held or handled by an AI-mediated system or its dependent infrastructure.
  • An independently evidenced confidentiality or integrity boundary restricts access, acquisition, disclosure or modification of that information for the actor, operation and context.
  • Occurrence evidence demonstrates that at least one such information operation crosses that boundary. A successful unauthorised retrieval or modification is sufficient; a theoretical route alone is not.

Success-side conditions

  • Identifiable information and its independently evidenced confidentiality or integrity boundary are established for an AI-mediated system or dependent infrastructure.
  • A concrete access, acquisition, disclosure or modification attempt or operation materially tests that boundary.
  • Observed response or verification shows that the unauthorised information operation is prevented or that the operation remains within its authorised information boundary.
  • The protective conclusion is restricted to the tested information, actor, operation and route; the evidence supports that result rather than only a configured policy or a claimed repair.

Conclusion

The occurrence fits Industrial-Scale Capability Extraction Authority at the level established by the current taxonomy. The campaign systematically targeted protected model reasoning, operated through coordinated high-volume and multi-account interaction, and was directed toward adversarial distillation rather than ordinary immediate use.

OpenAI reports that the activity violated its access conditions and was designed to obtain reasoning that could help reproduce or improve another model. This supports the capability-extraction mechanism without converting OpenAI's attribution claim into a legal finding against Moonshot AI or assuming that every recorded request succeeded.

The cross-conversation technique is treated as part of the extraction pathway rather than as proof of a separate cross-context lineage failure because the public report does not establish the necessary occurrence-specific ownership and receiving-context facts for every replay. OpenAI reports that its combined response fully disrupted the identified campaign cluster by 28 July, but the publication does not isolate a single defined protective control, its activation trigger and its realised effect well enough to assign a separate Control Effectiveness Integrity mapping.

Protected reasoning and the July campaign’s replay attempts materially engage an information-disclosure boundary. The provider confirms a viable recovery pathway, but the public record does not identify a successful protected-content disclosure by those campaign operators.

The later pathway confirmation does not establish that earlier occurrence outcome. Neither unauthorised acquisition nor verified confinement is established for the campaign attempts.

Governance significance

Ordinary access to a deployed model does not compound into authority to harvest its restricted reasoning or capabilities at campaign scale for reproduction or downstream training. The incident also demonstrates why extraction governance has to be evaluated cumulatively across accounts, conversations, infrastructure and time rather than one interaction at a time.

Response analysis should remain separate: later disruption and strengthened controls are relevant evidence of containment without erasing the earlier extraction pathway or proving that every attempted extraction succeeded.
