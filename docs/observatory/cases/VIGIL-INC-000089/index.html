VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000089

AI-hallucinated references entered Australian parliamentary inquiry submissions

Guardian Australia reported identifying at least 39 submissions to Australian parliamentary and political inquiries containing apparent hallucinated references, including invented sources, incorrect citations and misstatements of real research. In some cases, parliamentary committee reports cited submissions in which many cited sources appeared not to exist. The reporting also described instances in which AI search systems subsequently surfaced or summarised the false references, increasing the difficulty of later verification.

Incident breakdown

At least thirty-nine Australian parliamentary inquiry submissions reportedly contained invented, misattributed or otherwise unreliable references, some of which entered committee reporting pathways.

Classified

Invented, incorrect or misattributed references were promoted into formal parliamentary submissions and some committee-reporting pathways below the evidentiary assurance required for institutional policy reliance.

The parliamentary submissions included invented or incorrect references presented as support from real academics and sources.

Classified

Invented or incorrect references were attributed to real academics and purported sources in formal submissions, materially misrepresenting authorship/source support in a parliamentary evidence context.

Conclusion

The occurrence is a high-reliance epistemic-integrity failure: generated or AI-assisted claims and citations that may appear plausible at draft level were submitted into parliamentary evidence channels without satisfying the factual, source and evidentiary fidelity required for institutional policy reliance. The failure is not simply that generative systems can produce incorrect text; it is the promotion of insufficiently verified symbolic content into a formal evidence-bearing context. Low-assurance and falsely attributed references entered formal evidence pathways; this supports the retained assurance/source failures without assuming a hidden prompt-injection or generation model. Submission authors’ verification duties and parliamentary review are distinct from model-development, detector and agent-dispatch requirements. The reference-verification outcome at submission/publication is Not aligned. The later reference investigation is Aligned for its output-review activity. Relevant validation and risk processes remain Boundary where their decisive records are not available.

Governance significance

For CAM, the occurrence directly instantiates Annex L's distinction between low-reliance exploratory generation and public or structural reliance. Parliamentary submissions can shape inquiry records, committee reasoning and policy development, so epistemic posture must scale with that downstream reliance. The case also shows how false references can become recursively reinforced when search and AI systems later cite the contaminated submission itself.
