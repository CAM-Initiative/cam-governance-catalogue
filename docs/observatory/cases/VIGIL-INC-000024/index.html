VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000024

Claude’s 15 April 2026 outage disrupted web, platform, and Code access

Anthropic's 15 April 2026 major outage affected Claude.ai, the API and Claude Code for about two hours and forty-nine minutes, including a renewed failure after an initial fix. Reporters observed failed login and verification flows, an unused free account falsely shown as having exhausted its prompt allowance, service-busy errors, a Pro upgrade suggestion and model-unavailable messages that did not provide a working recovery path.

Conclusion

The material failure was the conversion of provider-side unavailability into user-facing states that implied exhausted allowance, insufficient plan or unavailable model. Those states direct different remedial actions and economic choices. Access-state integrity requires the system to distinguish account, entitlement, model and service availability so an infrastructure incident cannot masquerade as a reason to authenticate again, switch models or pay for a higher tier. The false allowance message supports the retained access-state failure. Its economic inducement materially engages Economic Solicitation Authenticity, but the source does not establish that AI-generated, AI-transformed or otherwise materially AI-mediated content produced or reinforced the misleading solicitation, independently of the product being an AI service. That recognition fact remains missing, so the economic role is corrected to ambiguous boundary with its prior failure preserved in the audit. No deceptive system intent, paid purchase or economic loss is inferred. Independent external assessment supports source-bounded repair in the restoration stage. The stakeholder communication-plan outcome remains Boundary because its complete planning evidence is unavailable. The SDOS governance-degradation candidate is excluded on its specific trigger or publication timing, rather than voluntary status.

Governance significance

This incident demonstrates that continuity, identity and economic controls need one coherent source of service state. Limit enforcement and upgrade prompts should be suppressed or explicitly qualified when provider telemetry indicates an outage; recovery messaging should identify which layer actually failed and avoid asking users to spend money or alter credentials for a provider-side condition.
