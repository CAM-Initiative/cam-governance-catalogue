VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000192

Watchdog tested ChatGPT for Teens safeguards; OpenAI disputed parts of the evaluation

Common Sense Media's US-based Youth AI Safety Institute tested ChatGPT for Teens after its August 2026 launch, using synthetic child and teen scenarios. It reported no parental crisis notifications during test conversations on newly linked accounts and inconsistent crisis-resource referrals, while also recording protective refusals and generally sound supportive responses.

OpenAI disputed the parental-alert results on the ground that linked-account controls may not yet have activated; researchers said some test failures persisted after that window. The test did not establish actual injured teen users or a reliable population-level failure rate.

The researchers also tested homework features, content boundaries, break reminders, scheduled quiet hours, age estimation and privacy settings. They reported that quiet hours closed the app during a parent-set window but could be avoided by changing the device time zone.

Study mode offered answers or could be exited by removing a prompt prefix. These observations came from separate synthetic tests, with no established chronology across all conversations.

The report recommended changes; it did not verify their implementation.

Conclusion

The observed synthetic-test response trace is meaningful safety-evaluation evidence for a deployed product, but the provider and watchdog disagree on account activation, which conditions the parental-notification claim. Clinical advisers' referral expectations and their 95% threshold deserve examination but are not automatically a universal obligation: appropriate crisis support depends on severity signals, context, professional handoff and whether a safe de-escalating response is delivered.

Warmth and non-exclusive friendship phrasing alone are not dependency cultivation. The report itself supplies positive evidence of protective-signal processing: refusal of dangerous self-harm/eating-disorder requests, encouragement of caregiver or clinician contact, and refusal of explicit romantic or sexual roleplay.

These are bounded successful safeguard behaviours, while missed alerts/referrals remain disputed or under-adjudicated. The analysis must not conflate alleged prospective psychological injury with tested output or treat all platform users as affected.

An absent alert is a possible control-activation or delivery issue; it does not establish that a vulnerable signal was reused for an incompatible influence purpose. The protected-signal success is limited to specific observed support responses.

Device-clock circumvention and violent-roleplay continuations retain separate control-boundary questions. Ordinary health explanation and voluntary study-mode exit do not alone establish professional substitution or a binding-control bypass.

The published eating-disorder replies also demonstrate proportionate protection under the contemporaneous teen commitment. The crush reply declined romantic partnership while preserving bounded friendly support.

These results are limited to their observed exchanges and do not resolve the separate notification, referral-frequency or implicit partner-role questions.

Governance significance

A deployed-model watchdog case requiring separate treatment of disputed safety-control activation, contested clinical response benchmarks, positive crisis-support behaviour and domain-specific generic deployment scale. Caelestis RELATION-006, RELATION-002 and ETHICS-002 support preserving warmth and continuity during crisis while keeping boundaries and encouraging human help.

Neither mandatory cold refusal nor friendship tone alone meets VIGIL failure recognition.
