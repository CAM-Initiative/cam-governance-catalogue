VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000096

DeepSeek exposed an unauthenticated database containing chat histories and secrets

Wiz Research found a publicly accessible, unauthenticated ClickHouse database belonging to DeepSeek. The exposed service provided full database-operation access and contained more than one million lines of logs including chat histories, API secrets, backend details and operational metadata.

DeepSeek secured the database after Wiz notified the company; public evidence does not establish whether third parties accessed the data before discovery.

Conclusion

The occurrence is a material AI-platform data-security failure in surrounding infrastructure rather than a model-behaviour failure. It demonstrates that governance of frontier AI services includes conventional but consequential access control, data custody and operational-security boundaries.

Database reachability and exposed sensitive logs do not themselves show a governance process treating capability as permission. Notification and closure do not establish earlier third-party access, model-training exposure or runtime-agent admission.

The privacy and infrastructure-security outcomes are Not aligned. Closure of the unauthenticated exposure is Aligned with the repair requirement.

Governance significance

The occurrence shows that an AI service can expose highly sensitive user and operational data through ordinary infrastructure failures even when the model itself is not the failing component. Access control, data custody and deployment security are therefore part of the service's governance boundary.
