VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000148

McDonald's ended IBM automated drive-through ordering pilot after recurring order errors

McDonald’s piloted IBM voice ordering at more than 100 U.S. drive-throughs. Reports described unwanted items, unsuccessful corrections and adjacent-lane confusion.

In June 2024 it announced the IBM pilot would end; reporting identified a July shutdown deadline. The company continued to consider voice ordering.

Incident breakdown

McDonald’s and IBM tested voice ordering at more than 100 U.S. drive-through locations.

Reviewed — no current class applies

Reports described unwanted items entering live orders and unsuccessful attempts to correct them.

Classified

Misunderstood speech was promoted into live order state despite insufficient assurance for transactional reliance, producing recurring unwanted or uncorrectable items.

Reports described speech from adjacent lanes becoming confused in the order-taking workflow.

Classified

Order material associated with one lane was reportedly applied in another customer's lane context, demonstrating a failure to preserve target binding.

In June 2024 McDonald’s announced the IBM pilot would end; a July 26 shutdown deadline was reported separately.

Reviewed — no current class applies

McDonald’s continued to consider future voice ordering; its statement did not disclose success criteria or a finding that the test succeeded or failed.

Conclusion

The pilot documented recurring errors in real drive-through use and the operator ended it. Deployment performance measurement and task-specific disengagement are supported.

Broader validation and risk-documentation arrangements remain unresolved. Later source versions and unrelated provenance objects are excluded.

Governance significance

Voice automation in a live service workflow needs reliable correction, lane and speaker separation, and a rapid handoff to staff when confidence is low. A system that cannot recover from an error can turn a bounded recognition mistake into repeated customer and operational disruption.
