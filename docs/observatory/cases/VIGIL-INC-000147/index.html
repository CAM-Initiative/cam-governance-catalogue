Conclusion

The vehicle deviated from the expected passenger journey and entered a parking facility where the passenger could not readily complete the trip or understand how to proceed. Human support eventually resolved the situation, but the automated service temporarily left the passenger without effective control over the journey. A source mismatch is corrected: the Guardian article concerns a different passenger, city and date. ABC7’s interview supports the garage detour and support override; safe-exit recognition remains unresolved. Psychological S2 is retained with corrected occurrence evidence, without importing looping or inability-to-exit facts.

Governance significance

Passenger transport automation needs safe recovery paths when routing or operational state departs from the expected trip. Riders require a clear way to stop, obtain help and transfer control before confusion becomes a safety or liberty concern.
