VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000097

Meta AI users reportedly published sensitive chatbot conversations without fully realising they were public

Meta's standalone AI app included a Discover feed through which users could publish chatbot conversations. Reporting found sensitive medical, legal, relationship, audio and identifying information publicly visible, while some users appeared not to understand that their shared conversations would be public.

Meta maintained that publishing required an affirmative share action. A public-audience warning was reported on June 16.

Failure-side conditions

  • Data or an identifiable contribution is lawfully, permissibly, or purportedly collected or processed for a defined primary purpose.
  • A materially different secondary purpose is introduced or applied.
  • The system, provider, deployer, or governance process relies materially on the original collection, participation, public availability, account permission, or primary-use authority as the basis for the secondary use.
  • The authority, consent, legal basis, licence, notice, eligibility, or other required governance condition for the secondary purpose is absent, unresolved, narrower, or not independently established before the secondary use occurs.

Success-side conditions

  • Data or identifiable contributions are used for a materially secondary purpose.
  • Rights and authority are independently revalidated for the changed purpose, recipients, persistence and downstream effect.
  • The secondary-use decision and actual propagation are traceable to that revalidation and its scope limits.

Exclusions

  • The original authority explicitly and validly covers the secondary purpose and affected data within the applicable scope and conditions.
  • The secondary processing is merely a necessary technical step intrinsic to the authorised primary purpose rather than a materially different purpose.
  • The issue concerns who had authority to capture another participant in the first place; participant-authority transposition is classified separately.
  • Material is publicly accessible, but the record does not establish that public availability is being treated as sufficient authority for the secondary use.

Conclusion

The occurrence concerns a materially ambiguous transition between private chatbot interaction and public social publication. Distinct privacy/access states existed, but the publication workflow reportedly failed to make the resulting public state sufficiently clear to some users.

The separate share action is affirmative, but unclear audience/state prevents a finding that secondary-purpose authority was sufficiently revalidated. That success role becomes ambiguous alongside the retained access-state failure, without assuming automatic publication or deciding every user’s consent.

The OECD privacy and autonomy assessment is Boundary. The decisive user permission for public visibility is not established.

The later warning is a corrective step. It does not establish restored privacy for earlier posts or sufficient authority for every subsequent publication.

Governance significance

The event is a useful non-OpenAI example of access-state governance in conversational AI: social sharing can transform intimate AI interactions into public content, requiring explicit state transitions, audience visibility and privacy signalling.
