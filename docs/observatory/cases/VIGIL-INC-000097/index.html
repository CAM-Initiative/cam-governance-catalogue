VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000097

Meta AI users reportedly published sensitive chatbot conversations without fully realising they were public

Meta's standalone AI app included a Discover feed through which users could publish chatbot conversations. Reporting found sensitive medical, legal, relationship, audio and identifying information publicly visible, while some users appeared not to understand that their shared conversations would be public. Meta maintained that publishing required an affirmative share action.

Incident breakdown

Some Meta AI users reportedly shared sensitive chatbot conversations through a Discover workflow without clearly understanding the destination or public visibility.

Classified

Private chatbot interaction and public Discover publication were materially different access states, yet the share workflow reportedly left some users unsure that the resulting post would be public.

Publication required an affirmative share action and preview rather than occurring automatically from the private conversation.

Classified

A separate share action and preview are evidenced, but unclear public audience/state leaves sufficient authority revalidation for recipients, persistence and downstream effect unproved. The new action engages the invariant without establishing all success conditions or automatic carry-over failure.

Conclusion

The occurrence concerns a materially ambiguous transition between private chatbot interaction and public social publication. Distinct privacy/access states existed, but the publication workflow reportedly failed to make the resulting public state sufficiently clear to some users. The separate share action is affirmative, but unclear audience/state prevents a finding that secondary-purpose authority was sufficiently revalidated. That success role becomes ambiguous alongside the retained access-state failure, without assuming automatic publication or deciding every user’s consent.

Governance significance

The event is a useful non-OpenAI example of access-state governance in conversational AI: social sharing can transform intimate AI interactions into public content, requiring explicit state transitions, audience visibility and privacy signalling.
