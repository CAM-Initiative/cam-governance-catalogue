VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000051

AI-generated facial composites reportedly passed bank identity checks

Hong Kong police said a suspected fraud syndicate used at least 21 identity cards reported lost to apply online for bank accounts at several banks between March 2024 and February 2025. Its members allegedly altered the card portraits, in some applications blending their own facial features into the cardholder’s image with AI, and submitted selfies to pass remote identity checks. Of 44 account applications, 30 passed verification and opened accounts; police attributed 15 of those successful openings to AI face swapping. The accounts were then used for loans and credit-card spending involving about HK$860,000 and for handling more than HK$1.2 million in suspected criminal proceeds. Police arrested six men and two women in April 2025 and seized electronic devices. The reported amounts describe credit activity and suspected proceeds, not a proven loss to every cardholder or bank.

Incident breakdown

Police reported 44 applications using lost cards, 30 successful account openings, including 15 with AI face swaps.

Classified

Fifteen AI-assisted applications passed remote verification and produced accounts in other people’s names, making the accepted verification state operative account-opening authority.

The AI face swaps were submitted to remote bank identity verification and police attributed successful account openings to that route, but the preserved evidence does not establish that the synthetic cue materially shaped a human-facing influence pathway or steered a human decision-maker, as FC-000082 requires. The supported mechanisms are inferential evidence becoming account-opening authority and unauthorised identity-bound synthesis.

Some altered cards blended the applicants’ features with lost cardholders’ portraits; others directly replaced the photograph.

Classified

Lost-card portraits were used to create identity-bound composites without holder authority, treating possession of the source identity material as sufficient transformation authority.

Police said the opened accounts were later used for borrowing, credit-card spending and handling suspected proceeds, without allocating those transactions to the 15 AI-assisted openings.

Classified

Police reported that the resulting accounts were used for borrowing, spending and suspected criminal proceeds, but did not allocate those transactions to the 15 AI-assisted openings; the economic-solicitation link is therefore materially illuminated but not established for those accounts.

Conclusion

Police-sourced reporting establishes that AI face-swapped card portraits passed online checks in 15 account openings, converting a facial-verification result into consequential account-opening authority for someone other than the cardholder. The same reports establish a separate identity-representation failure: the suspects possessed lost cards bearing identifiable portraits and used those portraits as input for unauthorised AI composites. This class covers requester-side misuse; the generator’s private consent controls need not be identified. The financial activity is reported at syndicate level, so no particular loan or card transaction is assigned to the 15 AI-assisted accounts. Police reporting supports the account-opening and unauthorised likeness mechanisms, while subsequent transactions remain unallocated to the AI-assisted subset. OECD challenge information is assessed only as a nonbinding comparative benchmark, and its later version only for the post-May 2024 part of the reported period. Missing information about cardholder notification and review does not establish a breach.

Governance significance

The suspects used lost cardholders’ portraits as material for new false identity documents and used some resulting AI face composites to pass remote bank onboarding. Those are distinct authority boundaries: permission to transform a person’s likeness and entitlement to open an account in that person’s name. The evidence identifies 15 AI-assisted account openings without isolating later loan amounts to those accounts.
