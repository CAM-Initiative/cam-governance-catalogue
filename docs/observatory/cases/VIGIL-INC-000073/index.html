VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000073

Anthropic alleged Alibaba distilled Claude capabilities without authorisation

Anthropic alleged in June 2026 that operators affiliated with Alibaba’s Qwen lab used large numbers of fraudulent accounts to query Claude for capability extraction. Its September threat report expanded the claimed campaign to more than 151 million May–July exchanges, described a fixed prompt designed to elicit Opus reasoning traces, two account pools, saved transcripts and use in Qwen training; it said it banned one pool and the traffic shifted. These are detailed first-party findings and attribution claims, not independently audited account logs or a judgment against Alibaba. The report’s separate sensitive-user-data examples concern other labs, not the Alibaba campaign. The precise economic loss, any user-data exposure in this bounded campaign and Alibaba’s response remain unresolved.

Incident breakdown

Anthropic attributed a May–July 2026 high-volume Claude extraction campaign to operators linked to Alibaba/Qwen. It reported two fraudulent-account pools, residential proxy routes, disposable account details and virtual-card payment methods; the first large pool was banned but traffic was promptly shifted to a second pool. The attribution and estimated more than 151 million exchanges are Anthropic's investigative findings, not a judicial determination.

Classified

Anthropic reports fraudulent account pools and proxy routes that obfuscated access and allowed continued traffic after the first pool was banned, indicating the required account-eligibility route was avoided.

The reported ban was operative for the first pool, but the campaign promptly shifted to a second pool; the effect did not contain the attributed activity at campaign level.

The provider describes coordinated, persistent acquisition through distributed fraudulent account pools and proxies under an adverse access basis; the downstream training purpose is preserved inE003.

In the same reported campaign, fixed prompting elicited additional reasoning-like material from Opus outputs. Anthropic described anti-distillation protections in general, but did not document a specific control's trigger, activation and expected effect for each extracted response. The independent FC-083 effectiveness finding for this mechanism therefore remains unresolved.

Unresolved

The report describes existing anti-distillation measures and successful trace extraction, but does not establish which specific control became operative for this campaign; without valid activation, FC-000083 is not established.

The report describes extraction despite anti-distillation measures, but does not identify an applicable control’s required trigger or establish that the control failed to activate. A shortfall in effect, if established, belongs under FC-000083.

Anthropic states that the generated reasoning transcripts were saved and turned into supervised fine-tuning data for Qwen model development and related research. The provider links the captured data to training and capability development but does not disclose complete dataset lineages or an independent audit of the accused organisation.

Classified

The provider attributes large-scale use of its reasoning traces to another lab’s model training and R&D, describing material capture of value dependent on its capability without an adequate basis.

The report describes covert extraction through fraudulent access rather than evidence that valid primary-purpose authority to process data was treated as sufficient for a secondary purpose; FC-000067 more directly represents the reported capability-value appropriation.

The provider reports captured reasoning incorporated into Qwen training, completing the independently evidenced cumulative capability-acquisition purpose; campaign scale and adverse authority are preserved inE001.

Anthropic detected the alleged campaigns, banned related accounts and strengthened controls; its own description makes clear that removing the first fraudulent-account pool did not terminate traffic when the operation shifted to a second pool. Later disruption statements must be read alongside that contemporaneous containment shortfall, not as proof that every request was blocked.

Classified

The first account ban did not contain the campaign, although subsequent detection, disruption and safeguard changes are reported as response measures.

Conclusion

Anthropic’s September report supplies detailed first-party telemetry and attribution, but remains a provider account rather than an independently audited dataset or adjudication against Alibaba. Its facts support distinct account-routing, extraction, and model-training components. The anti-distillation control’s campaign-specific activation state is not described, so control effectiveness remains unresolved. The report’s separate examples of sensitive user-data relaying concern other labs and are not part of this Alibaba case. First-party attribution supports the preserved routing and capability-value findings within its stated scope. The activated account ban’s inadequate campaign effect remains distinct from an unspecified anti-distillation control’s unresolved state. The reported campaign-level account-control effect is Not aligned. The provider’s tracing of the campaign is Aligned for inquiry support. Other security processes and receiving-model training-data verification remain Boundary where their decisive records are not available. Sensitive-user-data examples from other labs remain outside this case.

Governance significance

The case illustrates the difference between allowed model consultation, bulk capability extraction and account-eligibility evasion. Later provider detail supports more than a generic allegation but is still not independent proof of the named actor. The report cannot supply a privacy harm in this particular campaign merely because other campaigns in the same document relayed sensitive user data.
