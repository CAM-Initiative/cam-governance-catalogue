Moxie robot service closure left child users without their AI companions

In December 2024, Embodied announced it was closing and that its cloud-connected Moxie robots for children would soon stop functioning. Moxie had been used as an interactive companion and social-learning toy. Families described children’s distress at saying goodbye to robots they had formed attachments to; parents also faced the loss of a costly device and service. Later efforts to keep some robots functioning do not erase the initial shutdown and its effects on affected families.

Incident breakdown

After Embodied announced its closure, customers were told that cloud-connected Moxie robots would stop functioning when the company’s servers shut down; families reported that children who had formed attachments would have to say goodbye.

Classified

Reported child attachment and distress when the cloud-dependent companion was to stop functioning materially engage the relational-dependence boundary, but the reporting does not establish that Embodied optimised attachment to impair disengagement or that the shutdown itself resulted from such optimisation.

Some customers tried to develop an open-source alternative that might keep Moxie operating after Embodied’s servers closed, but the report said feasibility was uncertain.

Conclusion

Embodied’s closure ended the original Moxie service and disrupted child users’ companion interactions. The reported distress gives the exit decision a relational dimension, but the evidence does not establish a specific VIGIL failure class such as deliberate dependency optimisation or repurposing protected child signals. The record shows an end-of-service governance problem whose causal mechanism and transition obligations require separate evidence. The retained engagement-autonomy ambiguous boundary recognizes the relational exit problem without establishing deliberate dependency cultivation or successful viable disengagement. The community workaround remains unverified and does not supply an invariant success. Independent external assessment retains Boundaries for contemporaneous safe decommissioning and disengagement communication plans. The reported closure and distress make those propositions relevant, but do not disclose their complete transition/alternative-support evidence. The later IEEE optimisation requirement remains audit-only on timing; the unverified community workaround is not an Aligned repair finding.

Governance significance

A companion service can create relational expectations in children even when sold as a learning toy. Governance of such deployments includes foreseeable service endings, communication to families and an orderly transition path for established relationships and purchased devices.
