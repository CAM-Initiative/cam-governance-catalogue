Francisco Arteaga facial-recognition lead and FRT discovery-access dispute

New Jersey's published appellate opinion records that an initial NJROIC facial-recognition search found no match after a 2019 robbery, after which NYPD's Real Time Crime Center offered Francisco Arteaga as a possible match from the raw surveillance video. Police used Arteaga's photo in separate arrays shown to two eyewitnesses, both of whom identified him, and he was charged. Before trial, Arteaga sought thirteen categories of discovery concerning the facial-recognition process. The State produced two categories, while the trial court denied the remaining eleven. The Appellate Division reversed, holding that the requested material was directly relevant to testing the reliability of the FRT evidence, identifying possible alternative suspects and permitting the defense to challenge the State's case; it ordered the remaining material produced. VIGIL does not treat the opinion as a finding that the facial-recognition result was false or that Arteaga was wrongfully arrested or detained.
