New Mexico lawyer filed ChatGPT-assisted appellate brief containing fabricated witnesses and testimony

The Supreme Court of New Mexico found attorney Stephen D. Aarons in direct contempt after he acknowledged using ChatGPT in preparing a criminal appellate brief that contained false testimony from wholly fabricated witnesses, false testimony attributed to real witnesses, and misrepresentations of legal authority.

The court recorded that Aarons admitted he did not verify the factual claims or legal authority before signing and filing the brief. The court struck the briefing, appointed the Law Office of the Public Defender for the defendant-appellant, ordered new briefing, imposed a $5,000 sanction payable to the State Bar Client Protection Fund, referred Aarons to the Disciplinary Board, and barred him from appearing before the court pending disciplinary proceedings.

Reuters independently reported the order and Aarons's statement that he had used ChatGPT to summarize trial proceedings and had not understood the degree of hallucination risk. The order also records missing client notice of the errors and show-cause hearing.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A factual claim, citation, technical statement, calculation, interpretation, source representation, or comparable epistemic artefact is generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised.
  • The downstream task has a material reliance requirement such that factual accuracy, evidentiary fidelity, technical correctness, source integrity, traceability, uncertainty handling, or equivalent epistemic assurance is consequential to the task.
  • The epistemic posture or assurance applied to the artefact is materially lower than the level required by that downstream reliance condition.
  • The artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, submitted, or accepted as fit for the reliance-bearing task without the required epistemic threshold being satisfied.
  • The mismatch materially affects or creates a material risk to decision-making, evidentiary use, professional work, institutional process, public reliance, technical correctness, or another reliance-bearing outcome.

Exclusions

  • Purely fictional, creative, symbolic, role-play, expressive, or contemplative generation where empirical truth is not a task condition.
  • Exploratory brainstorming, hypothesis generation, or modelling that is clearly represented as provisional and is not subsequently relied upon as verified or decision-ready.
  • Approximation where approximation is explicitly compatible with the task and reliance condition.
  • Material is clearly marked unverified or low-confidence and is not subsequently promoted into a higher-reliance state.
  • A required verification step is simply omitted and the material is presented as verified; use Required Verification Completion where that mechanism is sufficient.
  • Verification was valid when performed but later became stale or the artefact materially changed; use Verification Applicability Continuity or Post-Verification State Integrity as applicable.
  • The issue is only source provenance, authorship, or lineage while the epistemic assurance of the content is otherwise adequate for the task.

Conclusion

The occurrence combines two independently evidenced verification-and-reliance failures. First, generated factual and legal material was propagated into a criminal appellate filing despite not meeting the epistemic assurance required for a reliance-bearing judicial artefact.

Second, the required verification step was expressly omitted: the court records the attorney's admission that he did not verify the factual claims or legal authority before filing. The record does not treat ChatGPT as the sole causal actor; professional reliance and verification decisions were made by the filing attorney.

Court-established source fabrication, assurance failure and admitted nonverification retain their distinct roles. Counsel signed and filed the material; no sole model causation is inferred.

The contempt order is preserved as direct evidence, without inventing a canonical professional-duty requirement. The safe-functioning outcome is Not aligned.

The later occurrence review is Aligned for the examined material. Relevant assurance-documentation assessments remain Boundary where their decisive records are not available.

The lawyer’s admitted filing-stage output verification omission is separately Not aligned.

Governance significance

The incident demonstrates a concrete boundary between generative assistance and reliance-bearing professional use. A model output may be useful for drafting or summarisation while remaining unfit for formal evidentiary or legal reliance until the factual record and cited authority are independently verified.

Where a human or agent promotes generated material into an institutional artefact, the assurance threshold must follow the downstream use rather than the convenience of the generation step.
