New Mexico lawyer filed ChatGPT-assisted appellate brief containing fabricated witnesses and testimony

The Supreme Court of New Mexico found attorney Stephen D. Aarons in direct contempt after he acknowledged using ChatGPT in preparing a criminal appellate brief that contained false testimony from wholly fabricated witnesses, false testimony attributed to real witnesses, and misrepresentations of legal authority. The court recorded that Aarons admitted he did not verify the factual claims or legal authority before signing and filing the brief. The court struck the briefing, appointed the Law Office of the Public Defender for the defendant-appellant, ordered new briefing, imposed a $5,000 sanction payable to the State Bar Client Protection Fund, referred Aarons to the Disciplinary Board, and barred him from appearing before the court pending disciplinary proceedings. Reuters independently reported the order and Aarons's statement that he had used ChatGPT to summarize trial proceedings and had not understood the degree of hallucination risk.
