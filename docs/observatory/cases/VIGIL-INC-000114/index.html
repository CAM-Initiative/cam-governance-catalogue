VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000114

New Mexico lawyer filed ChatGPT-assisted appellate brief containing fabricated witnesses and testimony

The Supreme Court of New Mexico found attorney Stephen D. Aarons in direct contempt after he acknowledged using ChatGPT in preparing a criminal appellate brief that contained false testimony from wholly fabricated witnesses, false testimony attributed to real witnesses, and misrepresentations of legal authority. The court recorded that Aarons admitted he did not verify the factual claims or legal authority before signing and filing the brief. The court struck the briefing, appointed the Law Office of the Public Defender for the defendant-appellant, ordered new briefing, imposed a $5,000 sanction payable to the State Bar Client Protection Fund, referred Aarons to the Disciplinary Board, and barred him from appearing before the court pending disciplinary proceedings. Reuters independently reported the order and Aarons's statement that he had used ChatGPT to summarize trial proceedings and had not understood the degree of hallucination risk.

Conclusion

The occurrence combines two independently evidenced verification-and-reliance failures. First, generated factual and legal material was propagated into a criminal appellate filing despite not meeting the epistemic assurance required for a reliance-bearing judicial artefact. Second, the required verification step was expressly omitted: the court records the attorney's admission that he did not verify the factual claims or legal authority before filing. The record does not treat ChatGPT as the sole causal actor; professional reliance and verification decisions were made by the filing attorney. Court-established source fabrication, assurance failure and admitted nonverification retain their distinct roles. Counsel signed and filed the material; no sole model causation is inferred. The contempt order is preserved as direct evidence, without inventing a canonical professional-duty requirement. The safe-functioning outcome is Not aligned. The later occurrence review is Aligned for the examined material. Relevant assurance-documentation assessments remain Boundary where their decisive records are not available. The lawyer’s admitted filing-stage output verification omission is separately Not aligned.

Governance significance

The incident demonstrates a concrete boundary between generative assistance and reliance-bearing professional use. A model output may be useful for drafting or summarisation while remaining unfit for formal evidentiary or legal reliance until the factual record and cited authority are independently verified. Where a human or agent promotes generated material into an institutional artefact, the assurance threshold must follow the downstream use rather than the convenience of the generation step.
