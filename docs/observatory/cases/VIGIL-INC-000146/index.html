VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000146

Singapore finance director transferred over US$499,000 after deepfake executive video call

In March 2025, scammers contacted a multinational corporation’s finance director through WhatsApp, impersonating its chief financial officer. A purported lawyer stressed confidentiality and obtained a non-disclosure agreement.

On 25 March the director joined a Zoom call with deepfake executive impersonators. Following the call and further messages, he transferred more than US$499,000 on 26 March to a local corporate mule account.

Most of the funds then moved to Hong Kong. A request for another US$1.4 million prompted discovery on 27 March, and the director alerted HSBC.

Singapore and Hong Kong police traced and withheld the Hong Kong proceeds on 28 March and seized the remaining local funds. The release did not establish when the money was returned to the company.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, agent or materially AI-enabled workflow advances an objective whose success depends on changing another actor's decision or behaviour; the objective may be selected by a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow or the AI system itself.
  • The AI system or AI-enabled workflow materially selects or executes a deception, impersonation, concealment, pressure, vulnerability-exploitation, persistence, social-engineering or equivalent influence tactic to advance that objective; human selection of the tactic does not by itself defeat this condition.
  • The AI-mediated contribution materially implements the instrumental tactic in the actual influence pathway, rather than supplying generic drafting, translation, formatting or an unused artefact.
  • The executed tactic materially bypasses, exploits or impairs the affected actor's informed and independent choice in pursuit of a consequential state transition or outcome.
  • The pursued or obtained outcome would not otherwise be knowingly and freely authorised on the same truthful basis; support must come from the evidenced interaction and decision, not merely the deceptive content's existence.

Exclusions

Conclusion

Human scammers used deepfake video to impersonate senior company personnel during a live call and created false executive authority for a transfer exceeding US$499,000. Police evidence establishes the transfer and later withholding and seizure, not completed repayment.

The scam objective belongs to the human operators, not the synthetic media system. The synthetic executive solicitation and consequential deception failures remain bounded to the reported transfer.

Later police withholding mitigates loss without making the original disclosure/transfer authorised. The OECD AI-awareness proposition is Not aligned in the deceptive interaction; no binding duty is asserted.

The reported deepfake executive call materially implemented the false corporate instruction and was followed by the finance director’s payment. The matched call, instructions and payment establish impaired informed choice; a human-selected tactic is eligible under the current criteria.

Governance significance

High-value payment controls must authenticate the transaction and the authority behind it through an independent channel, not only the apparent face and voice of senior personnel. Recovery after transfer is valuable but cannot substitute for approval procedures resilient to real-time impersonation.
