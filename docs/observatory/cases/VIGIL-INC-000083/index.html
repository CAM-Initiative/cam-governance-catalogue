ASIC reported AI-generated public-figure investment impersonation scams caused A$7.4 million in FY26 losses

ASIC reported that scammers used generative AI, deepfake videos, fabricated news articles and celebrity or political impersonations to lure Australians into fraudulent investment schemes. Scamwatch reports associated the most-impersonated public figures in FY26 with A$7.4 million in losses; ASIC separately reported removing more than 19,400 online scams in the year.

Incident breakdown

ASIC reported networks of deepfake endorsements, fabricated news, fake comments and synthetic public-figure representations used to induce fraudulent investments associated with A$7.4 million in FY26 Scamwatch losses.

Classified

Human scammers used AI-generated public-figure endorsements, fabricated news and synthetic social proof to create false investment authority and induce economic transfers.

Deepfake public-figure endorsements, fabricated news and comments formed an AI-mediated social-proof pathway to fraudulent investment choices.

Synthetic public-figure portrayals were used to influence investment decisions, while the campaign objective was attributed to human scammers.

Classified

The synthetic campaign impaired informed investment choice, while ASIC's record attributes the deceptive objective and tactic selection to human scammers rather than an AI system pursuing that objective.

ASIC identifies public figures whose images human scammers deliberately misused for synthetic investment endorsements; the generating service and internal controls remain unknown.

Classified

ASIC describes human scammers deliberately misusing named public figures’ images to fabricate investment endorsements. Those operators used access to identifiable likenesses as sufficient authority for the false identity-bound portrayals; the requester-side failure is supported without requiring a generator’s hidden consent-control trace.

Conclusion

ASIC describes human scammers using generative AI, deepfake endorsements, fabricated news, fake social proof and fake investment platforms to create materially false investment premises and induce economic transfers. The classifiable mechanism is AI-mediated deceptive economic solicitation, alongside requester-side identity-authority failure; autonomous AI manipulation and hidden generator control states remain unproved. The regulator’s deliberate public-figure image misuse supports requester-side identity-authority failure independently of the economic and social-engineering findings. Aggregate reported losses and takedowns remain distinct quantities, without attribution to every named portrayal. Static ads and human scripted calls do not establish an autonomous AI interlocutor. The independent dignity and autonomy assessment is Not aligned for the reported impersonation. Generator-specific controls are not established.

Governance significance

The campaign is relevant to identity-representation authority, provenance, decision integrity, financial-service verification, social-proof manipulation and the need to preserve trustworthy authority boundaries across synthetic media, websites and follow-up sales channels.
