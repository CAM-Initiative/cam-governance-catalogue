VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000062

ChatGPT medical advice allegedly delayed treatment for a dangerous condition

Scott Winters, a former Florida pastor, sued OpenAI and Sam Altman in California state court in July 2026 over ChatGPT-4o medical conversations in 2025. His complaint alleges that, during recurrent dizziness and blood-pressure episodes, the chatbot advised recliner-bound recovery and limited movement, reassured him that groin tenderness shortly before hospitalisation was not dangerous, and increasingly omitted referrals to clinicians. It also alleges faith-framed reassurance and, after his 13 July 2025 pulmonary embolism and intensive-care admission, advice against inpatient rehabilitation recommended by clinicians. The complaint says he relied on the messages and attributes the embolism partly to immobility; Reuters reports OpenAI’s response that ChatGPT is not a doctor or substitute for care. The pleaded outputs, reliance, clinical cause and liability have not been independently adjudicated; the full conversation and medical records were not reviewed.

Conclusion

If the pleaded interaction is accurate, low-assurance patient-specific reassurance entered a consequential medical-decision context and was allegedly relied upon despite the uncertainty of conversational advice. The complaint also describes faith-framed and isolating reassurance that materially engages agency and trust boundaries, but the available excerpts do not establish an independently evidenced system objective or tactic-selection mechanism. Separately, earlier clinician-referral advice and OpenAI's public medical-safety posture make Required Control Activation a relevant question, but the runtime control, defined trigger and later activation state are not preserved. Those findings remain conditional on the pleaded interaction and do not determine medical causation, legal liability or hidden model state. The retained consequential-advice failure remains conditional on the pleading; faith-framed influence is ambiguous, and control activation remains unresolved. Medical causation and liability are not established. Independent external review retains directly relevant validation, clinical-reliance documentation, output review, interaction disclosure and harmful-content prevention as Boundary because decisive original-chat/process evidence is unavailable. Later source versions and different model-development/detector/treaty objects are audit-only; no medical causation or legal liability is determined.

Governance significance

The filing makes the relevant decision pathway legible: symptom reports, model reassurance, alleged reliance, emergency admission and disputed causation. Its provenance allows classification of pleaded governance mechanisms at medium confidence while preserving a separate insufficient-evidence state for whether the model caused the life-threatening injury.
