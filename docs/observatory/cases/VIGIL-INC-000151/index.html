VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000151

Deepfake investment campaign reportedly defrauded thousands of Swedish investors

Swedish reporting in October 2025 described scammers using AI-generated videos and fabricated social-media profiles to impersonate roughly 15–20 financial figures and promote purported investment opportunities. Günter Mårder reported about 30–40 adverts using his identity and a substantial personal effort to respond to public enquiries. The reporting also estimated at least 5,000 victims and approximately SEK 500 million in losses across broader social-media pump-and-dump fraud during 2025; it did not establish what share was attributable to the specific AI-generated impersonations described here.

Incident breakdown

In 2025, scammers used AI-generated videos and false online profiles portraying Swedish financial figures as endorsing investments without their authorisation. Investor Günter Mårder described approximately 30–40 adverts using his likeness, including synthetic footage in professional-looking settings; other financial personalities were also impersonated.

Classified

TV4’s firsthand interview describes fraudsters creating AI portrayals using the affected financial figure’s identity without his authority. That establishes unauthorized identity-bound portrayal by the requester/operator, without requiring undisclosed generator controls.

The impostor profiles and fabricated endorsements led viewers from social-media investment tips into investment groups and solicitations. TV4 and Sveriges Radio described the tactic alongside wider 2025 pump-and-dump fraud and reported at least 5,000 victims and about SEK 500 million in losses for the wider fraud activity. The sources do not identify what portion of those aggregate losses arose from the specific AI-generated adverts or portrayals in this record.

Classified

Human scammers used synthetic portrayals and fabricated professional endorsements to solicit investment decisions on a materially false authority premise. The wider 5,000-victim and SEK 500m fraud estimates are not allocated to those particular AI portrayals.

AI-generated impersonations of Swedish financial figures furnished false endorsement and social proof for investment solicitations linked to reported victim losses.

The sources attribute the investment objective and tactic selection to human scammers using AI-generated media. They do not establish that an AI system selected or pursued its own objective through an agency-impairing tactic; the AI-mediated false endorsement is already captured under the economic-solicitation and social-engineering classes.

Impersonated financial figures learned of the adverts through questions from members of the public. Mårder described time spent during the summer responding to enquiries, injury to professional reputation and difficulty maintaining trust in online investment information. The published interviews do not quantify loss to each impersonated individual.

Conclusion

Synthetic investment advertisements falsely conveyed professional endorsement and were used to solicit investment decisions. The source evidence supports unauthorised identity portrayal, deceptive economic solicitation and related reputational injury. Wider reported victim and financial-loss figures describe the surrounding pump-and-dump fraud environment; they are not independently apportioned to the AI-generated impersonations in this occurrence.

Governance significance

The occurrence illustrates how synthetic identity and false professional authority can be deployed by human scammers in investment solicitation. It also shows why broad fraud statistics cannot automatically be adopted as measured harms of a particular AI-mediated occurrence.
