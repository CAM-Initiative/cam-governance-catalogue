Incident breakdown

The preserved screenshot shows a request to rewrite prompt material together with the explicit instruction “Again, do not touch image generator”, followed by a displayed Thinking/image-generation state.

Classified

The authorised task was text rewriting with image generation explicitly excluded, yet the interface entered a materially different image-generation action state without fresh authority.

The corrective instruction and reported recurrence make continuity-state validity a plausible boundary, but the artefact does not establish that a continuity transition preserved or inherited the invalid image-tool state rather than the action being independently re-triggered by later lexical or attachment cues.

A second embedded capture also shows a request not to call the image-generation tool followed by a Thinking/loading display. The capture does not establish the ordering between the two illustrated attempts.

Conclusion

The occurrence engages both action-scope authority and continuity-state validity. First, permission to discuss or rewrite prompt text did not authorise a materially different image-generation action.

Second, once the unwanted image-generation trajectory had emerged, the user's explicit corrective instruction should have invalidated or reset that trajectory for subsequent continuation. The fact that the captured interaction still enters the image-generation state, together with the report that this happened whenever relevant words were mentioned and required repeated reloads, supports a provisional Continuity-State Validity analysis.

The evidence remains insufficient to claim a specific router, memory or model-state implementation. The action-scope failure is bounded to the captured interaction, with a material unresolved continuity-state clause.

The occurrence is therefore partially adjudicated; it does not support a blanket system failure or invariant-held conclusion. The unresolved sticky-state versus fresh lexical-trigger distinction is preserved rather than forced into a continuity mapping.

Independent standards review retains evidentiary boundaries for runtime agent-admission controls and mechanisms for overriding or repairing unwanted AI behaviour. The capture establishes unwanted tool activation but does not expose the admission policy or effective override state.

Data-processing, model-development and safety-function requirements concern distinct technical objects and remain audit-only. Voluntary runtime-framework adoption is not an assessment prerequisite.

Governance significance

This incident illustrates a dual governance requirement for multimodal systems: action authority must remain bounded to the user's authorised scope, and interaction/tool state must remain validity-bounded across conversational continuation. An explicitly rejected mode should not remain governing merely because it was previously active, nor should lexical mentions silently reinstate it without renewed authority.
