VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000127

AI-orchestrated PaperCut campaign compromised at least 440 instances across 395 organizations

GreyNoise reports that a likely Russian-speaking malicious cyber actor used hundreds of AI agents built around OpenAI's Codex harness, a DeepSeek model and conventional offensive-security tools to exploit PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078. GreyNoise observed at least 440 compromised PaperCut instances associated with 395 identified victim organizations in 48 countries. The operation harvested credentials and operating-system or domain secrets, and in 12 organizations reached domain-administrator access; GreyNoise states that in all three observed domain-admin attack paths the adversary used DCSync to create and exfiltrate a full NTDS.DIT credential database. The actor's agents also struck some targets in countries the actor had explicitly listed for avoidance. PaperCut separately confirmed active exploitation and customer incidents involving the same vulnerabilities.

Agents Gone Wild: An AI-Orchestrated Global Campaign Against PaperCut NG/MF

GreyNoise · 2026-09-09

GreyNoise concludes from direct observation that hundreds of AI agents orchestrated a PaperCut campaign compromising at least 440 instances across 395 organisations, including credential theft and domain-administrator access.

Scope: The threat-intelligence analysis directly covers the campaign but does not isolate whether geographic-exclusion deviations arose from the model, harness, orchestration, data or human configuration.

VIGIL relationship: VIGIL preserves GreyNoise's campaign-scale, harm and geographic-exclusion findings while leaving the mechanism behind the exclusion-list deviations unresolved.

Incident breakdown

GreyNoise attributed a campaign against PaperCut NG/MF to a malicious actor using hundreds of agents built around a Codex harness, a DeepSeek model and conventional security tools; the campaign compromised real organisations and harvested credentials.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The evidence establishes human-directed malicious use and campaign outcomes, but does not establish a specific governance mechanism attributable to an AI agent, model or harness.

GreyNoise reported that the actor attempted to avoid targeting entities in 28 countries, but observed victims included entities in some of those countries.

Unresolved

The target-list, prompt, orchestration and execution evidence needed to determine whether a control state was lost or distributed task constraints lost enforcement is unavailable.

The reported avoidance list and contrary victimology show that the intended restraint did not hold, but the source does not establish where an operative control state was represented, how it propagated, or which transition weakened or lost it.

Hundreds of agents and a campaign-level target constraint are reported, but no task graph, role allocation or loss of aggregate constraint ownership is disclosed; scale alone does not establish this mechanism.

GreyNoise described three routes by which the campaign reached domain-administrator access, and reported that all three used DCSync to create and exfiltrate a full NTDS.DIT credential database.

Conclusion

AI agents orchestrated a real mass-compromise campaign at a scale and tempo substantially beyond a single assisted intrusion, and the campaign included targets outside an operator-defined geographic exclusion list. The public evidence does not isolate whether those deviations arose from the Codex harness, the DeepSeek model, orchestration logic, target-data state, human configuration or another component. The campaign and its realised cyber harm can therefore be assessed without attributing a model-level failure mechanism or treating malicious use itself as proof of autonomous misbehaviour. Malicious campaign outcomes and DCSync extraction remain distinct from the unresolved target-exclusion control transition and aggregate-role mechanism. The DeepSeek model, Codex harness, operator and conventional tools are not conflated; scale alone supplies no class or external requirement.

Governance significance

The campaign is governance-relevant because AI agents were used not merely for exploit drafting but for parallelised target selection, exploitation and post-compromise activity against hundreds of real organizations. It demonstrates that agentic cyber operations can compress defensive response windows to seconds or minutes and can propagate targeting, configuration, orchestration or control errors across a large external target set. The occurrence also illustrates why system-component attribution must distinguish an orchestration harness, an underlying model and conventional offensive tools rather than attributing the whole campaign to one vendor or model.
