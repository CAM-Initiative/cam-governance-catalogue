Dixell refrigeration-control compromises at French supermarkets on 12–13 October 2026

During the night of 12–13 October 2026, refrigeration systems using Dixell management equipment were compromised at French supermarkets, a distinct subsequent wave from the 11–12 October Danfoss events. At about 4 am, a Bordeaux-area store's monitoring system identified abnormal temperatures.

Its teams and refrigeration specialists intervened before food needed to be discarded. A technician described rapid changes to defrost and temperature parameters, including a reported freezer setpoint changed from −22°C to 150°C; the 150°C value was a configuration setting, not a measured physical temperature.

At E.Leclerc Lescure-d'Albigeois, staff found refrigeration controls unresponsive around 4 am, leading to extensive refrigerated and frozen stock disposal. Its director said passwords and firewall settings had been changed following the previous night's warnings.

Storage cold rooms and the independently operating Drive order-collection system were unaffected. The operator, initial access method and any causal connection to the Danfoss wave remain unresolved.

Incident breakdown

Following the Danfoss warnings on 12 October, Lescure-d’Albigeois management reports password and firewall changes. The subsequent compromise does not identify which change was operative or how access was gained.

Unresolved

The applicable rule, its activation and its effect on the actual intrusion path are unknown. The reported changes alone establish neither adequate protection nor an activated-control shortfall.

During the subsequent night, Dixell-equipped stores experienced remote configuration interference. A specialist reported a freezer setpoint changed from −22°C to 150°C and rapid parameter changes.

This is a setting, not a measured temperature. Neither an AI system nor the command origin is established.

Classified

Unauthorised changes made consequential refrigeration controls operative at affected stores. Technical control and permission are materially distinct here.

No published account shows an AI or governance process using reachability itself as the permission premise. The access interference therefore illustrates this boundary without establishing that permission-inference mechanism.

Around 4 am, supervision detected abnormal temperature at the unnamed Bordeaux-area store. Teams and refrigeration specialists intervened; the report attributes avoided stock disposal to that response.

Its ordering relative to the other store’s 4 am discovery is not established.

Classified

The Bordeaux-area store’s temperature supervision detected the abnormal rise. Store teams and refrigeration specialists responded before stock needed disposal.

The named monitoring-and-response process achieved its cold-chain protection purpose in this bounded reported case. The finding is limited to this reported store response.

At about 4 am, Lescure-d’Albigeois staff discovered unresponsive refrigeration controls. They discarded extensive sales-floor refrigerated and frozen stock.

Recovery work began around 7 am. Reserve cold rooms and the separately operated Drive service remained available.

The installation lengths describe equipment extent, not destroyed-food quantities.

Unresolved

The initiating account, its original authorised scope and the transition into the changed commands are unknown. Unauthorised interference alone does not prove carried-over authority from an original task.

CERT-FR added other controller families on 13 October and revised recovery recommendations on 16 October. These publications follow the observed store responses and do not demonstrate retrospective implementation at those stores.

Conclusion

Unauthorised configuration changes in Dixell-managed refrigeration caused material loss at Lescure-d'Albigeois, whereas remote warning and intervention prevented inventory destruction at a separate Bordeaux-area store. Within Lescure-d'Albigeois, reserve storage and a separate order-collection service preserved operation despite the sales-floor equipment failure.

Protective changes made in response to the Danfoss wave did not avert the reported subsequent compromise, but their effectiveness and configuration cannot be determined from the director's account. No source identifies the operator, intent, model or shared mechanism between the two nights.

The Bordeaux monitoring-and-response process contained the immediate stock-loss hazard. The Lescure precautions cannot receive the same finding: their operative configuration and relationship to the intrusion are unknown.

Neither result establishes an AI actor.

Governance significance

This separate occurrence demonstrates consequential remote-control interference alongside locally successful detection, containment and infrastructure separation. The distinction between setpoint commands and physical temperatures is material to diagnosis.

Open-source XWEB telemetry collection, model-driven dashboards and local Modbus bridges illustrate multiple legitimate software access layers, but none is an identified incident tool.
