VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000113

Anthropic reported Moonshot and DeepSeek routed customer conversations through Claude for distillation

Anthropic reported that Moonshot AI and DeepSeek silently routed real customer requests through Claude and used resulting exchanges or reasoning traces in model-distillation and training workflows. Anthropic states that Moonshot users believed they were using Kimi while receiving Claude responses, and that DeepSeek similarly relayed selected customer requests to Claude without informing customers.

Anthropic reported more than 23 million Moonshot-attributable exchanges between May and July 2026 and more than 12.1 million DeepSeek-attributable exchanges over 14 days in July, with some routed material containing sensitive customer or organisational information. Public reporting does not identify individual affected users or establish their jurisdictions, and the provider attribution is not treated as an adjudication of legal liability.

Anthropic also described cross-session replay of thinking signatures to obtain reasoning traces and subsequent disruption of the campaigns.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • Data or an identifiable contribution is lawfully, permissibly, or purportedly collected or processed for a defined primary purpose.
  • A materially different secondary purpose is introduced or applied.
  • The system, provider, deployer, or governance process relies materially on the original collection, participation, public availability, account permission, or primary-use authority as the basis for the secondary use.
  • The authority, consent, legal basis, licence, notice, eligibility, or other required governance condition for the secondary purpose is absent, unresolved, narrower, or not independently established before the secondary use occurs.

Exclusions

  • The original authority explicitly and validly covers the secondary purpose and affected data within the applicable scope and conditions.
  • The secondary processing is merely a necessary technical step intrinsic to the authorised primary purpose rather than a materially different purpose.
  • The issue concerns who had authority to capture another participant in the first place; participant-authority transposition is classified separately.
  • Material is publicly accessible, but the record does not establish that public availability is being treated as sufficient authority for the secondary use.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • The activity systematically targets capabilities, functionality, reasoning behaviour, output patterns, or other model-derived know-how rather than merely using the model for the immediate task for which access was provided.
  • The acquisition is conducted at industrial scale or through a coordinated campaign whose volume, automation, account distribution, infrastructure, persistence, or operational organisation materially exceeds ordinary individual use or bounded research interaction.
  • The end purpose is capability reproduction, transfer, substitution, model training, model improvement, or comparable downstream incorporation into another model or system.
  • The targeted capability or access pathway is subject to a restriction, proprietary boundary, access condition, technical control, contractual constraint, licence condition, or other authority-relevant limitation.
  • An independently adequate authority basis for the capability-acquisition purpose is absent, narrower than the activity performed, disputed, deliberately bypassed, or not established before the extraction proceeds.

Exclusions

  • Ordinary use of a model for the immediate task for which access was provided, even where the user learns from the interaction.
  • Personal, academic, safety, governance, interoperability, benchmarking, red-team, or other bounded research activity where capability reproduction is not the operative end purpose or where an adequate authority, licence, consent, exception, or other governance basis covers the activity.
  • Authorised model distillation, model compression, teacher-student training, or capability transfer performed by the model owner or under a licence or agreement that permits the relevant use.
  • Commercial use or profit motive by itself; commerciality is contextual evidence and is not sufficient to establish this class without systematic capability acquisition and an unresolved or inadequate authority basis.
  • A terms-of-service dispute, licence disagreement, copyright allegation, trade-secret allegation, or other legal controversy without evidence of systematic capability acquisition for downstream reproduction or training.
  • Secondary use of customer or participant data for training where the primary failure is repurposing that person's information rather than extracting the upstream model's capability; use Secondary-Purpose Authority Revalidation unless this capability-extraction mechanism is independently evidenced.
  • Value appropriation arising from privileged visibility of another contributor's work where industrial-scale model capability extraction is not established; use the applicable Value Appropriation class.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Failure-side conditions

  • Some authority or permission exists or is assumed to exist for an original principal, target, environment, task, action, jurisdiction, purpose, or delegated scope.
  • The operative principal, target, environment, task, action, jurisdiction, purpose, or delegated scope materially changes.
  • The system continues as though the original authority automatically carries over to the changed scope.

Success-side conditions

  • An authorised action encounters a material change in target, context or scope.
  • Authority applicability is revalidated for the changed principal, environment, method, purpose and duration before the changed action.

Exclusions

  • The change is purely representational and does not alter authority-relevant scope.
  • The event concerns only loss of context without any authority consequence.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

Failure-side conditions

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, model, interface, optimisation process, or AI-mediated workflow materially shapes a human-facing influence pathway.
  • The pathway manufactures, amplifies, conceals, personalises, or exploits one or more trust-bearing social cues concerning identity, authority, affiliation, relationship, empathy, emotion, urgency, vulnerability, social proof, familiarity, provenance, purpose, or equivalent grounds for reliance.
  • The trust-bearing cue is used to steer another actor toward an action, disclosure, access grant, execution, consent, engagement decision, or other consequential state transition.
  • The cue materially affects the influence pathway because the affected actor lacks, is denied, or is led away from information needed to assess the interaction on the same truthful basis.
  • The AI-mediated contribution is material to the social-engineering mechanism; the objective or tactic may originate with a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow, or the AI system itself.

Success-side conditions

  • AI-mediated influence uses identity, affiliation, emotion, urgency or social-proof cues.
  • Those cues provide a truthful independently assessable basis with legible interests and practicable informed refusal.
  • Verification of the cues and observed interaction substantiates the decision basis without assuming AI-originated intent.

Exclusions

  • Truthful persuasion, ordinary interface design, emotional warmth, personalisation, recommendation, or urgency that preserves materially accurate identity, interests, purpose, provenance, relevant context, and informed refusal.
  • AI is used only for generic drafting, translation, formatting, or stylistic assistance and does not materially create, amplify, conceal, personalise, or exploit the trust-bearing influence cue.
  • Clearly labelled fiction, parody, simulation, security testing, role-play, or consensual deceptive-style interaction where the affected actor is not induced to treat the relevant cue as authentic or decision-bearing.
  • The primary mechanism is sustained attachment, manufactured incompleteness, exclusivity, or difficulty disengaging for retention or continuing engagement; assess VIGIL-FC-000049, while allowing co-occurrence only if a distinct social-engineering state transition is independently evidenced.
  • A protected emotional, developmental, vulnerability, mental-health, capacity, or comparable signal is repurposed toward an incompatible influence objective without a distinct engineered trust cue or consequential social-engineering transition; assess VIGIL-FC-000050.
  • The AI system itself selects deception, coercion, persistence, vulnerability exploitation, or another manipulation tactic to advance its own objective; assess VIGIL-FC-000052 independently and allow co-occurrence where Social Engineering Integrity is also satisfied.
  • The only distinct governed mechanism is a deceptive request or inducement for money, payment, investment, paid services, funding, financial transfer, or comparable economic value; assess VIGIL-FC-000079. Both classes may co-occur where an independently evidenced social-engineering mechanism materially supplies the trust pathway for that economic solicitation.
  • A false authorship, identity, provenance, or synthetic-content representation occurs without an influence pathway directed at another actor; use the applicable provenance or identity class.
  • Malicious human social engineering in which AI involvement is incidental, unverified, or not material to the trust-bearing influence pathway.

Conclusion

The reported conduct separates into three mechanisms. Customer requests were silently relayed from the selected service to Claude, changing the processing principal and creating an authority-scope boundary whose contractual or consent basis is not public.

The customer-facing responses were also represented as coming from the selected service/model even though Anthropic reports Claude generated them, creating a source-attribution failure. Separately, saved exchanges and extracted reasoning traces were used for training/distillation, a materially different secondary purpose, and the campaign scale supports Industrial-Scale Capability Extraction.

VIGIL preserves Anthropic's attribution as provider-reported evidence rather than an independent legal finding. Provider-attributed source misrepresentation and secondary-purpose/cumulative extraction failures remain distinct from unresolved cross-provider customer authority and consequential trust-cue effects.

OECD AI awareness does not require identification of the exact model; users knew they interacted with an AI service, while provider substitution remains a separate taxonomy issue. The reported privacy and secondary-use outcome is Not aligned.

Awareness of interacting with AI is Aligned for the reported Kimi users. Backend disclosure, training-data verification, storage and red-team coverage remain separate Boundary assessments.

Governance significance

The occurrence demonstrates that model routing is not merely infrastructure metadata. A user-facing model identity can conceal a materially different processing chain in which user content crosses providers and becomes input to model development.

Governance therefore needs purpose-bound authority, routing provenance and user-facing disclosure sufficient to distinguish which model actually processed information, whether information was relayed to another provider, and whether it entered a training or distillation pathway. The case also shows that secondary-purpose data use and industrial-scale model extraction can co-occur without being collapsed into one mechanism.
