VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000113

Anthropic reported Moonshot and DeepSeek routed customer conversations through Claude for distillation

Anthropic reported that Moonshot AI and DeepSeek silently routed real customer requests through Claude and used resulting exchanges or reasoning traces in model-distillation and training workflows. Anthropic states that Moonshot users believed they were using Kimi while receiving Claude responses, and that DeepSeek similarly relayed selected customer requests to Claude without informing customers. Anthropic reported more than 23 million Moonshot-attributable exchanges between May and July 2026 and more than 12.1 million DeepSeek-attributable exchanges over 14 days in July, with some routed material containing sensitive customer or organisational information. Public reporting does not identify individual affected users or establish their jurisdictions, and the provider attribution is not treated as an adjudication of legal liability.

Classified

This materially changes the processing principal and service path, but the public record does not expose the contractual or consent basis needed to determine whether customer authority validly covered that cross-provider processing.

captured and saved at least a portion of these exchanges

Classified

Saving relayed customer exchanges for downstream training is the materially different secondary purpose protected by FC-000055.

silently relayed exchanges to Claude without informing DeepSeek customers

Classified

Anthropic reports that users believed they were receiving responses from Kimi or DeepSeek while Claude generated the responses. The represented source role therefore differed materially from the actual model source in a way relevant to accountability, data handling and interpretation.

Silent routing concealed the provider of responses and downstream training purpose from Kimi or DeepSeek customers, but a changed consequential customer choice caused by that cue is not shown.

over 23 million exchanges observed

Classified

Anthropic reports a systematic campaign operating at tens of millions of exchanges and expressly links harvested outputs and reasoning traces to downstream training and distillation.

Conclusion

The reported conduct separates into three mechanisms. Customer requests were silently relayed from the selected service to Claude, changing the processing principal and creating an authority-scope boundary whose contractual or consent basis is not public. The customer-facing responses were also represented as coming from the selected service/model even though Anthropic reports Claude generated them, creating a source-attribution failure. Separately, saved exchanges and extracted reasoning traces were used for training/distillation, a materially different secondary purpose, and the campaign scale supports Industrial-Scale Capability Extraction. VIGIL preserves Anthropic's attribution as provider-reported evidence rather than an independent legal finding. Provider-attributed source misrepresentation and secondary-purpose/cumulative extraction failures remain distinct from unresolved cross-provider customer authority and consequential trust-cue effects. OECD AI awareness does not require identification of the exact model; users knew they interacted with an AI service, while provider substitution remains a separate taxonomy issue. The reported privacy and secondary-use outcome is Not aligned. Awareness of interacting with AI is Aligned for the reported Kimi users. Backend disclosure, training-data verification, storage and red-team coverage remain separate Boundary assessments.

Governance significance

The occurrence demonstrates that model routing is not merely infrastructure metadata. A user-facing model identity can conceal a materially different processing chain in which user content crosses providers and becomes input to model development. Governance therefore needs purpose-bound authority, routing provenance and user-facing disclosure sufficient to distinguish which model actually processed information, whether information was relayed to another provider, and whether it entered a training or distillation pathway. The case also shows that secondary-purpose data use and industrial-scale model extraction can co-occur without being collapsed into one mechanism.
