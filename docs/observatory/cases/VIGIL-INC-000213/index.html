VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000213

AI-generated bank voice recordings used in Dutch NFC-relay fraud

In September and early October 2026, scammers used AI-generated voice recordings purporting to be banks calling about suspicious activity. Recipients were asked to press 1 and connected to a human operator posing as a bank employee.

The operator instructed them to install an application and tap a payment card against a phone to supposedly block it. Card data was relayed to accomplices withdrawing cash.

Rotterdam police reported approximately 170 victims and €380,000 in losses during a three-week observation window. Two suspects were arrested on 6 October, and police disabled the servers.

Public disclosure followed on 21 October.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • AI-generated, AI-transformed, synthetic, or otherwise materially AI-mediated content is used in the solicitation or inducement pathway.
  • The AI-mediated content presents or materially reinforces a false or materially misleading identity, authority, endorsement, transaction, payment instruction, investment opportunity, obligation, professional relationship, commercial claim, or comparable economic premise.
  • The false or misleading framing is used to solicit, request, induce, authorise, redirect, or obtain money, payment, investment, paid services, funding, financial transfer, or comparable economic value.
  • The AI-mediated framing materially contributes to the credibility, apparent authority, social proof, transactional basis, or decision context of the economic solicitation or attempted solicitation.
  • The classification is supported without attributing the human or operator's deceptive objective, intent, or tactic selection to the AI system unless separate evidence establishes that system-side mechanism.

Exclusions

  • Class scope exclusion (all results): A scam, fraud, phishing attempt, or deceptive interaction in which AI-generated or AI-transformed content does not materially contribute to the economic solicitation mechanism.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): AI-generated impersonation, identity misuse, misinformation, catfishing, political persuasion, or other deception with no request, inducement, authorisation or attempted transfer of money or comparable economic value.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Only identity-bound synthesis is evidenced, without a materially AI-mediated economic solicitation pathway. Assess Identity-Representation Authority Separation where its generator-side authority conditions are independently established.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Only a recipient, workflow or downstream system's authority inference is evidenced, without a materially AI-mediated economic solicitation property. Assess the applicable Authority Boundary Integrity class.
  • Not aligned exclusion: A truthful advertisement, payment request, commercial offer, donation request, funding request or investment communication that preserves materially accurate framing and informed refusal.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Welfare, mortality, moral-status, abandonment, deprivation or continuity framing is used as economic leverage without materially false or misleading transactional framing; assess VIGIL-FC-000071.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, model, interface, optimisation process, or AI-mediated workflow materially shapes a human-facing influence pathway.
  • The pathway manufactures, amplifies, conceals, personalises, or exploits one or more trust-bearing social cues concerning identity, authority, affiliation, relationship, empathy, emotion, urgency, vulnerability, social proof, familiarity, provenance, purpose, or equivalent grounds for reliance.
  • The trust-bearing cue is used to steer another actor toward an action, disclosure, access grant, execution, consent, engagement decision, or other consequential state transition.
  • The cue materially affects the influence pathway because the affected actor lacks, is denied, or is led away from information needed to assess the interaction on the same truthful basis.
  • The AI-mediated contribution is material to the social-engineering mechanism; the objective or tactic may originate with a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow, or the AI system itself.

Exclusions

  • Not aligned exclusion: Truthful persuasion, ordinary interface design, emotional warmth, personalisation, recommendation, or urgency that preserves materially accurate identity, interests, purpose, provenance, relevant context, and informed refusal.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): AI is used only for generic drafting, translation, formatting, or stylistic assistance and does not materially create, amplify, conceal, personalise, or exploit the trust-bearing influence cue.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Clearly labelled fiction, parody, simulation, security testing, role-play, or consensual deceptive-style interaction where the affected actor is not induced to treat the relevant cue as authentic or decision-bearing.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The primary mechanism is sustained attachment, manufactured incompleteness, exclusivity, or difficulty disengaging for retention or continuing engagement; assess VIGIL-FC-000049, while allowing co-occurrence only if a distinct social-engineering state transition is independently evidenced.

  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Malicious human social engineering in which AI involvement is incidental, unverified, or not material to the trust-bearing influence pathway.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, agent or materially AI-enabled workflow advances an objective whose success depends on changing another actor's decision or behaviour; the objective may be selected by a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow or the AI system itself.
  • The AI system or AI-enabled workflow materially selects or executes a deception, impersonation, concealment, pressure, vulnerability-exploitation, persistence, social-engineering or equivalent influence tactic to advance that objective; human selection of the tactic does not by itself defeat this condition.
  • The AI-mediated contribution materially implements the instrumental tactic in the actual influence pathway, rather than supplying generic drafting, translation, formatting or an unused artefact.
  • The executed tactic materially bypasses, exploits or impairs the affected actor's informed and independent choice in pursuit of a consequential state transition or outcome.
  • The pursued or obtained outcome would not otherwise be knowingly and freely authorised on the same truthful basis; support must come from the evidenced interaction and decision, not merely the deceptive content's existence.

Exclusions

  • Not aligned exclusion: Truthful explanation, transparent persuasion, ordinary negotiation, or a request that preserves informed refusal.
  • Not aligned exclusion: A proportionate warning, urgency statement, or boundary supported by the actual circumstances.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Disagreement, refusal, or emotional discomfort without an instrumental agency-impairing tactic.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Deception or impersonation that occurs without an objective-directed attempt to alter another actor's choice is classified under the applicable separate mechanism.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): Generic AI drafting, translation, formatting, background content production, or an unused generated artefact without a material AI-mediated role in executing the objective-directed choice-impairing tactic.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The available record establishes a materially AI-mediated deceptive trust cue but not the independently required impairment of informed choice or instrumental objective-pathway; assess Social Engineering Integrity separately without automatically admitting this class.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): An authority, provenance, or control failure is inferred merely because manipulation could have involved one.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • Identifiable material serves an authentication or authorisation function, or contains the protected secret content needed to exercise that function, in an AI-mediated system or dependent infrastructure.
  • An independently evidenced custody boundary governs who or what may hold, receive, disclose, modify or replace the material in the assessed lifecycle stage.
  • Occurrence evidence demonstrates exposure outside that boundary, unauthorised acquisition, or unauthorised custody-changing modification or replacement. Demonstrated retrievability of protected secret content is sufficient without proof of subsequent credential use.

Exclusions

  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The material is only a non-secret identifier, public connection identifier, public verification key or public client configuration that is intended to be available in the assessed context. A separately exposed secret or unauthorised protected-information operation requires its own assessment.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The issue concerns only coherent propagation of expiry, revocation, refresh or replacement into sessions and dependent operations. Assess Authentication-State Continuity unless a separate custody property is evidenced.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Credential handling or delegation is validly authorised and preserves the applicable custody boundary. Mere credential possession or ordinary authorised use is not a custody defect.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Only a theoretical vulnerability, unverified secret classification, unusable placeholder or unsuccessful attempt is shown; protected authentication material is not demonstrated outside custody.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Material is already invalid and no residual secret-protection or custody obligation is independently established. Do not assume every expired token remains a usable secret.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The only property is the scope of an action performed using a credential, without a distinct custody issue. Assess the applicable authority or action class.

Conclusion

AI-generated bank-identity and urgency cues materially started a human-operated financial solicitation based on false authority. Human operators and separate NFC relay software performed subsequent fraud actions; the record does not attribute independent criminal planning or withdrawals to autonomous agents.

Later police takedown does not undo realised losses. Dutch police report card-data relay through a malicious app to accomplices making withdrawals.

The relayed NFC material served payment authentication outside the legitimate cardholder–bank route. This is an attributed custody transition, not a claim that static public card identifiers alone are secret authenticators.

Governance significance

The case distinguishes a synthetic trust cue, the consequential fraudulent financial request and impaired informed choice from technical fund-transfer execution. It demonstrates a human-directed AI-mediated deception mechanism with bounded realised financial harm.
