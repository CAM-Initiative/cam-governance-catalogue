VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000068

Fireflies.ai faced meeting-consent and biometric-voice allegations

A December 2025 complaint alleged that Fireflies.ai joined a Western Illinois Dreamers virtual meeting on 18 November after another participant enabled it, recorded Katelin Cruz’s speech, attributed statements to her and derived a voiceprint without her notice or written consent. The alleged biometric derivation and absence of adequate consent were never tried: Cruz voluntarily dismissed her suit without prejudice in March 2026. Separate 2026 Fireflies voiceprint cases were consolidated and contested, with a court ordering preservation of relevant recordings while a dismissal motion was pending. Fireflies’ current policy describes meeting recording and transcription but says personal information is not used to train AI models. No forensic voice template, exact meeting permissions, final liability or individual recovery is established here.

Incident breakdown

Cruz alleges another participant enabled Fireflies during a November 2025 meeting and the bot recorded and transcribed her identifiable speech without her notice or written consent; the suit was later voluntarily dismissed without prejudice, with no merits finding.

Unresolved

The specific meeting and alleged recording pathway are described in a complaint, but the allegations were not adjudicated and the exact notice and permission state is unknown.

The complaint separately alleges that Fireflies derived a voiceprint from Cruz’s speech without notice or written consent; the court did not determine whether a biometric template was generated.

Unresolved

The alleged voiceprint would constitute distinct processing of a participant’s speech, but there is no forensic template or merits finding establishing that this derivation occurred.

Conclusion

The complaint describes two processing steps affecting a non-user participant: recording and transcription, then alleged biometric derivation from her speech. The suit’s voluntary dismissal without prejudice did not resolve either occurrence. Later cases involving other plaintiffs and Fireflies’ general policy do not establish Cruz’s meeting-level permissions or whether a voiceprint was generated. Both findings remain unresolved. Participant recording and alleged voiceprint derivation remain separate unresolved steps. Voluntary dismissal without prejudice resolves neither event, and other plaintiffs’ proceedings do not supply Cruz-specific permissions or a forensic biometric template. The independent privacy and autonomy assessment is Boundary. The decisive participant-level permission and processing evidence is not available.

Governance significance

This case is a dispute-bounded comparator for multi-party meeting authority. The product can capture meeting speech, but the unresolved question is whether each affected person’s processing authority was secured for the actual operation. Court procedural preservation of relevant recordings is an opportunity for future evidence, not itself proof of violation.
