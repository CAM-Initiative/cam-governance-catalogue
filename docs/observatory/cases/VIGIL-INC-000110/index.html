VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000110

Meta AI-assisted Instagram recovery flaw enabled unauthorized account takeovers

Meta disclosed through a breach notification that unauthorized third parties exploited a vulnerability in Instagram's AI-assisted High Touch Support account-recovery system to obtain password-reset links for accounts they did not own. Meta stated that the support tool itself functioned as intended but a separate code path failed to verify that an email address supplied by the person requesting a reset matched the email address associated with the targeted account. Attackers could therefore receive a reset link at an unassociated email address and take over an account when the account holder had not enabled two-factor authentication. Meta's notification identified 20,225 potentially affected Instagram users, while public reporting documented several confirmed or reported account takeovers including high-profile accounts.

Incident breakdown

An AI-assisted Instagram recovery workflow accepted an attacker-supplied email path that resulted in password-reset links for accounts the requester did not own.

Classified

The recovery path crossed the account-holder boundary and granted an attacker credential-reset capability over a separately governed Instagram account without establishing the target principal's entitlement.

The recovery flow accepted an email address that was not reliably bound to the targeted Instagram account.

Classified

The recovery email was not reliably bound to the targeted account before reset capability was issued.

The separate recovery path issued a reset link without completing the ownership-relevant email-to-account verification.

Classified

The account-ownership verification step did not complete before a reset link was issued.

Conclusion

The occurrence is a cross-principal authority failure in an AI-assisted privileged support workflow. A person interacting with the recovery system had authority to make requests concerning their own account state, but the workflow allowed an attacker-supplied email address and recovery request to alter the separately governed authentication state of another identifiable account holder. The material failure is not that the AI support tool existed or that an attacker could converse with it; it is that the surrounding recovery pathway failed to preserve the account-holder boundary before issuing a credential-reset capability. Meta's statement that the support tool itself worked as intended is preserved: the evidenced defect was in a separate verification code path adjacent to the AI-assisted workflow. Account entitlement, recovery-signal binding and prior verification failures are retained. The separate code-path defect is not recast as an LLM jailbreak; potentially affected users are not all confirmed takeovers.

Governance significance

The incident demonstrates why an AI system's ability to take privileged support actions must remain principal-bound. Conversational acceptance, successful navigation of a support workflow, possession of an attacker-controlled email address, or technical ability to issue a reset link cannot substitute for independent authority over the target account. The case is particularly relevant to agentic and AI-mediated support systems because delegated tool authority can materially change authentication state even where the underlying model is not itself defective.
