Granola allegedly transcribed a meeting participant and used data for model improvement

In a July 2026 proposed class complaint, Florida resident Tarra Chamberlain alleged that another participant manually ran Granola during at least one Microsoft Teams or Zoom meeting, causing her speech to be captured and transcribed without her knowledge or consent. She further alleged, on information and belief, that Granola used the meeting information to train its models. Granola describes a bot-free app that transcribes meeting audio, stores transcripts rather than recordings, and uses anonymised data for its own model improvement unless an account user opts out; Enterprise training is off by default. A non-user participant lacks that account setting. The filing and product posture are established, but the particular meeting’s permissions, actual inclusion of her data in training, and lawsuit outcome are not.

Conclusion

The complaint describes two distinct questions: whether another attendee’s use of Granola authorised transcription of Chamberlain’s contribution, and whether any captured material was reused for model improvement. Granola’s policy and product design make both pathways relevant, but the complaint does not prove participant-level notice or consent, account-plan or opt-out state, or inclusion of Chamberlain’s data in training. Both findings remain unresolved. Transcription and possible model-improvement reuse remain distinct unresolved claims. Plan, notice, opt-out and actual training inclusion are unknown; a non-user’s lack of an account setting does not prove the secondary processing event. The independent privacy and autonomy assessment is Boundary. The decisive participant-level permission and processing evidence is not available.

Governance significance

Unlike bot-joining systems, this application can capture meeting audio from a participant’s device without joining the call. The governance question still concerns all affected speakers, not the visual presence of a bot. Provider policy supports a secondary training-purpose posture, while the actual plaintiff-specific use requires further evidence.
