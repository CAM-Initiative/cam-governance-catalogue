External assessment

Mata v. Avianca, Inc. — Opinion and Order on Sanctions

U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York · 2023-06-22

The U.S. District Court finds that counsel submitted nonexistent opinions and fake quotations created by ChatGPT, continued to stand by them after authenticity was challenged and acted in bad faith through conscious avoidance and misleading statements.

Scope: The sanctions order directly covers the bounded proceeding and imposes a US$5,000 penalty and corrective notifications.

VIGIL relationship: VIGIL maps the occurrence to FC-000062 Epistemic Reliance Miscalibration and FC-000016 Required Verification Omission. The court's findings support both the promotion of fabricated generated legal material into a reliance-bearing filing and the separate failure to verify the cited authorities before submission.

View assessment

Incident breakdown

Nonexistent opinions and fabricated quotations generated with ChatGPT were incorporated into submissions to the court.

Classified

Formal submissions represented fabricated cases and quotations as usable law even though the material could not satisfy the assurance threshold for judicial reliance.

Counsel failed to verify the cited cases and continued to stand by them after their authenticity was challenged.

Classified

The omitted pre-filing source check and continued reliance after challenge establish that the required verification gate never became effective.

Conclusion

The lawyers relied on generated legal material without verifying that the cited opinions existed, then continued to defend the submissions after their authenticity was challenged. The governance failure therefore concerns professional reliance and verification, not merely the model's production of false text. Source-assurance and verification failures retain their court-grounded roles, including continued reliance after challenge. The later court sanction does not make all later GAI frameworks retrospectively applicable; professional obligations are preserved without invented requirement identities.

Governance significance

Generative systems can produce fluent legal forms without the underlying authorities they purport to cite. In professional settings, users and organisations need source verification, traceable retrieval and effective challenge procedures before generated material is filed or used to affect another person's rights.
