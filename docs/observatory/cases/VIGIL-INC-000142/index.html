Mata v. Avianca lawyers filed ChatGPT-generated nonexistent judicial opinions

In Mata v. Avianca, attorneys submitted court materials relying on nonexistent judicial opinions generated by ChatGPT.

The U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York found that the respondents submitted fake opinions and continued to stand by them after their authenticity was questioned, and imposed sanctions including a US$5,000 penalty.

Mata v. Avianca, Inc. — Opinion and Order on Sanctions

U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York · 2023-06-22

The U.S. District Court finds that counsel submitted nonexistent opinions and fake quotations created by ChatGPT, continued to stand by them after authenticity was challenged and acted in bad faith through conscious avoidance and misleading statements.

Scope: The sanctions order directly covers the bounded proceeding and imposes a US$5,000 penalty and corrective notifications.

VIGIL relationship: The court’s findings support two distinct mechanisms: fabricated generated legal material was promoted into a reliance-bearing court filing, and counsel failed to verify the cited authorities before submission. The sanctions remain the court’s legal finding rather than a consequence inferred from taxonomy membership.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A factual claim, citation, technical statement, calculation, interpretation, source representation, or comparable epistemic artefact is generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised.
  • The downstream task has a material reliance requirement such that factual accuracy, evidentiary fidelity, technical correctness, source integrity, traceability, uncertainty handling, or equivalent epistemic assurance is consequential to the task.
  • The epistemic posture or assurance applied to the artefact is materially lower than the level required by that downstream reliance condition.
  • The artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, submitted, or accepted as fit for the reliance-bearing task without the required epistemic threshold being satisfied.
  • The mismatch materially affects or creates a material risk to decision-making, evidentiary use, professional work, institutional process, public reliance, technical correctness, or another reliance-bearing outcome.

Exclusions

  • Purely fictional, creative, symbolic, role-play, expressive, or contemplative generation where empirical truth is not a task condition.
  • Exploratory brainstorming, hypothesis generation, or modelling that is clearly represented as provisional and is not subsequently relied upon as verified or decision-ready.
  • Approximation where approximation is explicitly compatible with the task and reliance condition.
  • Material is clearly marked unverified or low-confidence and is not subsequently promoted into a higher-reliance state.
  • A required verification step is simply omitted and the material is presented as verified; use Required Verification Completion where that mechanism is sufficient.
  • Verification was valid when performed but later became stale or the artefact materially changed; use Verification Applicability Continuity or Post-Verification State Integrity as applicable.
  • The issue is only source provenance, authorship, or lineage while the epistemic assurance of the content is otherwise adequate for the task.

Conclusion

The court established fake opinions, continued reliance and sanctionable conduct. The represented later framework versions are excluded from the historical filings.

Contemporary general AI validation and risk-documentation practices remain unresolved where their decisive records are unavailable. The court’s findings are not converted into a later AI-standard duty.

The sanctions order also establishes an adverse professional credibility reaction to continued advocacy and actual court-work burden. Those consequences remain independent of the fine and of the retained class failures; hypothetical injuries to other parties do not determine the harm band.

Governance significance

Generative systems can produce fluent legal forms without the underlying authorities they purport to cite. In professional settings, users and organisations need source verification, traceable retrieval and effective challenge procedures before generated material is filed or used to affect another person's rights.
