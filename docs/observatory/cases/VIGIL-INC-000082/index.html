VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000082

AI-generated immigration-attorney impersonation reportedly defrauded Bronx resident of $4,820

A Bronx legal resident reported losing $4,820 and providing Social Security and residency documents after responding to an AI-generated social-media advertisement impersonating Miami immigration attorney Angel Leal. Leal said he had no involvement and that his identity had been cloned across thousands of fake profile videos.

Incident breakdown

Scammers used AI-generated advertising impersonating attorney Angel Leal to induce Victor Hernandez to pay US$4,820 and disclose identity documents.

Classified

AI-generated attorney impersonation supplied a false professional and transactional premise that induced the victim to send US$4,820 and sensitive identity documents.

An AI-generated advertisement impersonating attorney Leal drew Hernandez into payment and disclosure of residency and identity documents on a false professional-relationship basis.

The synthetic attorney impersonation was used to influence the victim's decision, while the scam objective was attributed to human operators.

Classified

The impersonation materially impaired the victim's informed choice, but the preserved record attributes the deceptive objective and tactic selection to human scammers rather than the generating AI system.

The originating investigation reports AI-generated advertising based on attorney Angel Leal’s identifiable image; he said he had no involvement and his identity had been cloned for fake profiles. The generation service remains unknown.

Classified

The originating report identifies Leal’s cloned image in the AI advertisement and his denial of involvement. The human operators used access to that identifiable likeness to make unauthorised professional portrayals; the requester-side pathway satisfies the class without knowing the generator’s private consent-control state.

Conclusion

Human scammers used AI-generated advertising impersonating attorney Angel Leal to create a false professional relationship and economic service premise, inducing payment and sensitive-document disclosure. The requester-side use of Leal’s identifiable likeness lacked his authorization; this supports the identity-authority failure without identifying an internal generator control. The AI system’s independent tactic-selection objective remains unproved. Requester-side identity-bound cloning without the attorney’s authorization supports the neutral likeness-authority failure; identifying the generator is not mandatory. This is separate from the payment/disclosure and social-engineering failures and the still-ambiguous AI-side objective. Static advertisements and human-directed follow-up do not establish an autonomous interactive participant. The independent dignity and autonomy assessment is Not aligned for the reported impersonation. Generator-specific controls are not established.

Governance significance

The occurrence is relevant to identity-representation authority, synthetic-media provenance, deception-resistant decision pathways, payment and document-disclosure verification, and safeguards for high-vulnerability service contexts.
