AI-generated immigration-attorney impersonation reportedly defrauded Bronx resident of $4,820

A Bronx legal resident reported losing $4,820 and providing Social Security and residency documents after responding to an AI-generated social-media advertisement impersonating Miami immigration attorney Angel Leal. Leal said he had no involvement and that his identity had been cloned across thousands of fake profile videos.

His wife questioned the requests and a friend helped identify the fake. Hernandez said later demands sought more money, and he reported the case to police and federal authorities.

He described rent and electricity arrears and concern about misuse of his documents.

Conclusion

Human scammers used AI-generated advertising impersonating attorney Angel Leal to create a false professional relationship and economic service premise, inducing payment and sensitive-document disclosure. The requester-side use of Leal’s identifiable likeness lacked his authorization; this supports the identity-authority failure without identifying an internal generator control.

Independent AI tactic selection remains unproved, but the materially AI-enabled human-directed impersonation workflow is the governed unit and human selection is not an exclusion. Requester-side identity-bound cloning without the attorney’s authorization supports the neutral likeness-authority failure; identifying the generator is not mandatory.

This is separate from the payment/disclosure and social-engineering failures and the separately supported instrumental choice-impairment mechanism. Static advertisements and human-directed follow-up do not establish an autonomous interactive participant.

The independent dignity and autonomy assessment is Not aligned for the reported impersonation. Generator-specific controls are not established.

Harm Impact: Hernandez reports sending Social Security and residency documents to the impersonators. Sensitive identity-document disclosure supports meaningful bounded privacy harm.

Later identity misuse and containment remain unknown. The cloned professional identity induced an actual victim to rely on a false attorney relationship and send money and documents.

That materially adverse third-party reliance supports bounded impersonation harm. Wider video takedown counts are not attributed to this victim occurrence.

Governance significance

The occurrence is relevant to identity-representation authority, synthetic-media provenance, deception-resistant decision pathways, payment and document-disclosure verification, and safeguards for high-vulnerability service contexts.
