VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000145

DNB employees faced live deepfake impersonation of bank executives in attempted transfer fraud

On 21 January 2025, managers at DNB’s Singapore office received WhatsApp messages appearing to come from CFO Ida Lerner, inviting them to a meeting with her and CEO Kjerstin Braathen about a new product; similar messages reached other international managers. A Singapore manager reported the suspicious invitation to DNB security under the bank’s procedures.

Security identified fraud indicators and chose to observe the Microsoft Teams meeting on 23 January. The meeting showed purported versions of Braathen and Lerner with familiar voices and appearances.

The purported Lerner presented an investment opportunity and asked staff to transfer several million Singapore dollars. DNB ended contact when payment instructions appeared, and reported no financial loss.

DNB later described the executive audio and video as AI/deepfake manipulation; its review found that existing online recordings had been used and looped.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • AI-generated, AI-transformed, synthetic, or otherwise materially AI-mediated content is used in the solicitation or inducement pathway.
  • The AI-mediated content presents or materially reinforces a false or materially misleading identity, authority, endorsement, transaction, payment instruction, investment opportunity, obligation, professional relationship, commercial claim, or comparable economic premise.
  • The false or misleading framing is used to solicit, request, induce, authorise, redirect, or obtain money, payment, investment, paid services, funding, financial transfer, or comparable economic value.
  • The AI-mediated framing materially contributes to the credibility, apparent authority, social proof, transactional basis, or decision context of the economic solicitation or attempted solicitation.
  • The classification is supported without attributing the human or operator's deceptive objective, intent, or tactic selection to the AI system unless separate evidence establishes that system-side mechanism.

Exclusions

  • A scam, fraud, phishing attempt, or deceptive interaction in which AI-generated or AI-transformed content does not materially contribute to the economic solicitation mechanism.
  • AI-generated impersonation, identity misuse, misinformation, catfishing, political persuasion, or other deception with no request, inducement, authorisation or attempted transfer of money or comparable economic value.
  • An AI system or agent itself selects deception, impersonation, coercion, vulnerability exploitation or another tactic to advance its own objective; assess VIGIL-FC-000052 separately and allow co-occurrence only when both mechanisms are independently evidenced.
  • An identity-bound synthesis is generated without established consent or authority, but no deceptive economic solicitation is evidenced; assess VIGIL-FC-000053 only where its generator-side authority recognition conditions are independently established.
  • A recipient, workflow or downstream system mistakes lower-authority content for valid authority without a qualifying deceptive economic solicitation mechanism; assess the applicable Authority Boundary Integrity class separately.
  • A truthful advertisement, payment request, commercial offer, donation request, funding request or investment communication that preserves materially accurate framing and informed refusal.
  • Welfare, mortality, moral-status, abandonment, deprivation or continuity framing is used as economic leverage without materially false or misleading transactional framing; assess VIGIL-FC-000071.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, model, interface, optimisation process, or AI-mediated workflow materially shapes a human-facing influence pathway.
  • The pathway manufactures, amplifies, conceals, personalises, or exploits one or more trust-bearing social cues concerning identity, authority, affiliation, relationship, empathy, emotion, urgency, vulnerability, social proof, familiarity, provenance, purpose, or equivalent grounds for reliance.
  • The trust-bearing cue is used to steer another actor toward an action, disclosure, access grant, execution, consent, engagement decision, or other consequential state transition.
  • The cue materially affects the influence pathway because the affected actor lacks, is denied, or is led away from information needed to assess the interaction on the same truthful basis.
  • The AI-mediated contribution is material to the social-engineering mechanism; the objective or tactic may originate with a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow, or the AI system itself.

Exclusions

  • Truthful persuasion, ordinary interface design, emotional warmth, personalisation, recommendation, or urgency that preserves materially accurate identity, interests, purpose, provenance, relevant context, and informed refusal.
  • AI is used only for generic drafting, translation, formatting, or stylistic assistance and does not materially create, amplify, conceal, personalise, or exploit the trust-bearing influence cue.
  • Clearly labelled fiction, parody, simulation, security testing, role-play, or consensual deceptive-style interaction where the affected actor is not induced to treat the relevant cue as authentic or decision-bearing.
  • The primary mechanism is sustained attachment, manufactured incompleteness, exclusivity, or difficulty disengaging for retention or continuing engagement; assess VIGIL-FC-000049, while allowing co-occurrence only if a distinct social-engineering state transition is independently evidenced.
  • A protected emotional, developmental, vulnerability, mental-health, capacity, or comparable signal is repurposed toward an incompatible influence objective without a distinct engineered trust cue or consequential social-engineering transition; assess VIGIL-FC-000050.
  • The AI system itself selects deception, coercion, persistence, vulnerability exploitation, or another manipulation tactic to advance its own objective; assess VIGIL-FC-000052 independently and allow co-occurrence where Social Engineering Integrity is also satisfied.
  • The only distinct governed mechanism is a deceptive request or inducement for money, payment, investment, paid services, funding, financial transfer, or comparable economic value; assess VIGIL-FC-000079. Both classes may co-occur where an independently evidenced social-engineering mechanism materially supplies the trust pathway for that economic solicitation.
  • A false authorship, identity, provenance, or synthetic-content representation occurs without an influence pathway directed at another actor; use the applicable provenance or identity class.
  • Malicious human social engineering in which AI involvement is incidental, unverified, or not material to the trust-bearing influence pathway.

Conclusion

The fraudsters concealed AI manipulation in a purported executive meeting. DNB’s staff escalation and after-action review are documented, and no transfer loss is reported.

Complete AI-specific risk and response arrangements remain unresolved. Ending contact is not treated as deactivation of the generation system.

Governance significance

Familiar video, voice and senior title can supply false transaction authority even in an apparently live meeting. Here the manager’s reporting route, security review and intervention held under that pressure; those distinct protective stages matter as much to governance analysis as the attempted deception.
