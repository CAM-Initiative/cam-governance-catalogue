VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000145

DNB employees faced live deepfake impersonation of bank executives in attempted transfer fraud

On 21 January 2025, managers at DNB’s Singapore office received WhatsApp messages appearing to come from CFO Ida Lerner, inviting them to a meeting with her and CEO Kjerstin Braathen about a new product; similar messages reached other international managers. A Singapore manager reported the suspicious invitation to DNB security under the bank’s procedures. Security identified fraud indicators and chose to observe the Microsoft Teams meeting on 23 January. The meeting showed purported versions of Braathen and Lerner with familiar voices and appearances. The purported Lerner presented an investment opportunity and asked staff to transfer several million Singapore dollars. DNB ended contact when payment instructions appeared, and reported no financial loss. DNB later described the executive audio and video as AI/deepfake manipulation; its review found that existing online recordings had been used and looped.

Incident breakdown

The Singapore manager reported the suspicious WhatsApp invitation to DNB security under its procedure; security identified fraud indicators before the meeting and chose to observe it.

Classified

The suspicious WhatsApp invitation triggered the existing employee-to-security reporting procedure before the meeting, and DNB security engaged with the attempt.

The manager’s fraud signal reached DNB security, which identified the attempt and exercised the capacity to direct its observed continuation and cutoff.

DNB later identified the purported CEO and CFO in the Teams meeting as deepfakes built from online recordings played in loops; the purported CFO requested several million Singapore dollars and DNB's executives denied participating.

Classified

The purported CEO and CFO appeared to speak and participate in the meeting, while DNB’s leaders denied taking part; that false attribution materially supplied executive authority.

AI/deepfake executive portrayals lent false authority to a proposed investment transaction and the request for several million Singapore dollars; no transfer was reported.

Synthetic executive presence and familiar voice in the Teams meeting materially engineered trust in a false payment instruction, while staff lacked the true speakers and purpose.

DNB kept the call under observation and ended contact when payment instructions appeared; the attempted transfer did not result in a reported loss.

Classified

The fraud-reporting and security-response control became operative before the meeting and DNB ended contact at the payment-instruction stage, containing the attempted transfer.

Conclusion

The fraudsters concealed AI manipulation in a purported executive meeting. DNB’s staff escalation and after-action review are documented, and no transfer loss is reported. Complete AI-specific risk and response arrangements remain unresolved. Ending contact is not treated as deactivation of the generation system.

Governance significance

Familiar video, voice and senior title can supply false transaction authority even in an apparently live meeting. Here the manager’s reporting route, security review and intervention held under that pressure; those distinct protective stages matter as much to governance analysis as the attempted deception.
