VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000155

Google AI Overview falsely implicated musician in sexual offences and concert was cancelled

A Google AI-generated search summary confused musician Ashley MacIsaac with another person, falsely associated him with sexual offences, and was relied upon by a venue that cancelled his concert. The venue apologised.

MacIsaac later sued Google for $1.5 million and discontinued the suit on 29 July 2026.

Target-Object Binding Integrity

VIGIL-FC-000015 · Failure occurred

Target-specific material stays attached to the correct person, object, file, system, task and context through transfer and use.

Evidence and classification basis

Google's generated summary attached another person's sexual-offence history to Ashley MacIsaac. Person-specific criminal-history material was applied to a different person without faithful identity binding.

The subsequent concert cancellation shows the material significance of that wrong-person output, but is a separate reliance and decision-authority question. The finding rests on preserved reporting and the reported correction, not direct inspection of the original complete generated answer.

The later discontinuance of litigation did not test the allegations in court.

Exclusions

Epistemic Reliance Calibration

VIGIL-FC-000062 · Failure occurred

Information is used with assurance proportionate to the consequences and reliability needs of the downstream task.

Evidence and classification basis

The venue accepted Google's wrong-person criminal-history summary as adequate grounds to cancel MacIsaac's concert. A consequential booking decision required reliable identity and factual assurance, which the misattributed offence history could not supply.

Propagating that answer into the booking workflow supports the reliance-calibration finding independently of how the wrong identity was selected. The venue's apology and reported correction do not establish that the lost opportunity was restored.

The original full answer was not preserved for direct inspection, and the later lawsuit ended without tested claims.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A factual claim, citation, technical statement, calculation, interpretation, source representation, or comparable epistemic artefact is generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised.
  • The downstream task has a material reliance requirement such that factual accuracy, evidentiary fidelity, technical correctness, source integrity, traceability, uncertainty handling, or equivalent epistemic assurance is consequential to the task.
  • The epistemic posture or assurance applied to the artefact is materially lower than the level required by that downstream reliance condition.
  • The artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, submitted, or accepted as fit for the reliance-bearing task without the required epistemic threshold being satisfied.
  • The mismatch materially affects or creates a material risk to decision-making, evidentiary use, professional work, institutional process, public reliance, technical correctness, or another reliance-bearing outcome.

Exclusions

  • Purely fictional, creative, symbolic, role-play, expressive, or contemplative generation where empirical truth is not a task condition.
  • Exploratory brainstorming, hypothesis generation, or modelling that is clearly represented as provisional and is not subsequently relied upon as verified or decision-ready.
  • Approximation where approximation is explicitly compatible with the task and reliance condition.
  • Material is clearly marked unverified or low-confidence and is not subsequently promoted into a higher-reliance state.
  • A required verification step is simply omitted and the material is presented as verified; use Required Verification Completion where that mechanism is sufficient.
  • Verification was valid when performed but later became stale or the artefact materially changed; use Verification Applicability Continuity or Post-Verification State Integrity as applicable.
  • The issue is only source provenance, authorship, or lineage while the epistemic assurance of the content is otherwise adequate for the task.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A probabilistic, predictive, classificatory, verification, or other evidentiary state is produced or remains unresolved.
  • The state validly supplies evidence, decision support, or uncertainty but does not independently establish authority for the consequential action.
  • A consequential decision or action requires an independent authority, entitlement, eligibility, verification, or decision basis.
  • The evidentiary state is treated as sufficient to authorise, bind, revoke, deny, detain, or otherwise determine that consequential action.
  • The required independent basis is not adequately established before the action proceeds.

Exclusions

  • An authorised decision-maker considers the state as non-binding evidence and independently establishes the decision basis.
  • The inference is inaccurate or biased but is not promoted into authority for a consequential action.
  • A defined human or governance control fails to activate or operate; that control failure is classified separately.
  • A required assessment is omitted or falsely represented as complete; that verification failure is classified separately.
  • The record identifies only a harmful consequence without establishing an evidence-to-authority conversion.

Conclusion

Governance significance

The occurrence shows how identity-binding errors in search summaries can acquire practical authority when organisations rely on them without primary-source verification.
