Conclusion

The evidenced boundary is service availability, not authority state. A login symptom does not establish that expired, suspended, restricted, unavailable or revoked states were conflated; that an authentication transition failed to propagate; or that an otherwise valid entitlement depended on a verifier that could not complete.

The provider's distinct investigating, monitoring and resolved updates preserved a usable operational recovery state, while the undisclosed cause prevents a more specific mechanism adjudication. The material episodes retain resolved-no-mapping dispositions.

The occurrence is fully adjudicated but unclassified, which does not assert that every invariant held. Clear operational status updates and recovery do not establish all recognition conditions of an access-state success.

No reviewed FC-derived candidate or independently source-identified admitted external requirement is established here; no external compliance finding is implied. Independent occurrence-level external assessment finds affirmative, source-bounded repair in the reported restoration stage.

Relevant communication/incident-process requirements remain Boundary where the decisive plan or implementation record is not public. Requirements addressing a different technical object, lifecycle or source date remain audit-only; voluntary adoption is not a gate.

Governance significance

This incident shows why VIGIL separates occurrence evidence from mechanism inference. Status reporting can establish affected surfaces, duration and recovery while remaining insufficient to determine which internal governance boundary failed.

CAM analysis should preserve that uncertainty and require more detailed causal evidence before assigning access-state, session-continuity or verification-dependency controls.
