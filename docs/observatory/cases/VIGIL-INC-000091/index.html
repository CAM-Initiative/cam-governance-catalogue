Concurrent ChatGPT, Claude and Grok service outages on 3 September 2026

On 3 September 2026, ChatGPT/Codex, Claude and Grok experienced overlapping service-availability incidents. OpenAI reported elevated errors across ChatGPT and Codex and separately attributed its disruption to a routing error. Anthropic reported a partial Claude outage caused by an infrastructure issue, affecting Claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Cowork and the Claude API. xAI reported a Grok models outage across multiple surfaces and later attributed its disruption to an outage at its Memphis compute centre. The temporal overlap is material ecosystem evidence, but no reviewed source establishes that the three providers shared one technical cause. OpenAI’s later write-up says it rolled back a routing change, with broad mitigation by about 15:20 UTC and final resolution at 16:55 UTC. Returning traffic prolonged some recovery. xAI also apologised to affected compute partners.

Conclusion

Three major frontier-AI service providers experienced overlapping availability failures on the same day. The available evidence gives different provider-specific explanations: OpenAI attributed its disruption to a routing error, Anthropic to an infrastructure issue, and xAI to an outage at its Memphis compute centre.

The coincidence is relevant to resilience, concentration, dependency and correlated-failure analysis, but it does not establish a shared failure path across all three providers. Anthropic’s disclosed SpaceX capacity agreement makes a Claude/Grok dependency plausible, while outage-specific propagation remains unconfirmed.

The separate provider causes are preserved and temporal overlap is not promoted into proof of a common dependency. Degraded availability alone does not establish verifier failure, leverage or a required-control state.

The provider’s later alerting improvements do not expose the produced signal and failed delivery path needed to diagnose a signal-delivery failure. The reported service recoveries are Aligned with the OECD repair requirement.

This assessment does not establish a common outage cause.

Governance significance

The occurrence demonstrates that simultaneous or near-simultaneous loss of access to multiple frontier AI providers can create ecosystem-level resilience risk even where the individual provider failures have different or unresolved causes. It is relevant to organisational fallback design, provider concentration, correlated-outage monitoring and sovereign assurance, while preserving the distinction between temporal correlation and proven common causation.
