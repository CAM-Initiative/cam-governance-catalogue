Incident breakdown

Cost Explorer suffered an interruption after an engineering change in December 2025. Financial Times reporting associated a roughly 13-hour interruption with Kiro; Amazon instead described a brief, one-region event caused by user error and a misconfigured role.

Classified

Press reporting associated the outage-triggering change with Kiro, while Amazon attributed the event to human error and access-control configuration; the causal mechanism therefore remains materially contested.

Amazon said no compute, storage, database or AI services were affected and no customer inquiries were received. It reported additional safeguards, mandatory peer review for production access and review through its Correction of Error process.

Failure-side conditions

  • A material event, output, interruption, pathway, or behaviour is observed or reported.
  • A specific causal, operational, behavioural, or intentional mechanism is attributed to that event.
  • Available evidence does not trace the attribution to runtime state, action history, evaluation conditions, or another adequate evidentiary basis.
  • Material alternative mechanisms remain unresolved or the attribution is presented more definitively than the evidence supports.
  • The unsupported attribution materially affects interpretation, governance response, accountability, or reliance.

Success-side conditions

  • A causal, operational or intentional mechanism is attributed to an occurrence.
  • The strength of attribution is proportionate to traceable evidence connecting that mechanism to the material event.
  • The attribution distinguishes observed facts, correlation, hypothesis and established causation in accordance with the inspected evidence.

Exclusions

  • Adequate evidence establishes the attributed mechanism and its application to the event.
  • The explanation is explicitly presented as a bounded hypothesis with material alternatives and uncertainty preserved.
  • The issue is only factual inaccuracy unrelated to causal, operational, behavioural, or intentional attribution.
  • The event cannot be reconstructed, but no unsupported mechanism is assigned.
  • Execution state is not disclosed, but the system does not invent or overstate a causal account.

Conclusion

The service outage is established while AI causation remains disputed. Security and investigator-traceability assessments remain unresolved pending an occurrence-specific process trace.

The finding does not assign the outage to Kiro or infer that later peer review prevents every recurrence. Harm Impact: The Financial Times reports roughly thirteen hours of Cost Explorer interruption, supporting service S3 at medium confidence.

Amazon acknowledges limited interruption but disputes duration/characterisation and autonomous AI causation. The band describes the reported occurrence consequence, not a finding that Kiro caused the full outage or that other AWS services failed.

Governance significance

The case illustrates why AI attribution in software operations must be based on the actual change-authority and review chain rather than a tool's presence in an engineering workflow.
