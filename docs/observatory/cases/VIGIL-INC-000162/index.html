Kiro coding tool reportedly implicated in 13-hour AWS Cost Explorer outage

AWS Cost Explorer experienced a reported multi-hour interruption after an engineering change associated in press reporting with Amazon's Kiro coding tool; Amazon disputes autonomous-AI causation and attributes the event to human error and misconfigured access controls.

Incident breakdown

Amazon described a brief interruption limited to AWS Cost Explorer in one of its 39 geographic regions, attributed it to user error involving a misconfigured role, and said compute, storage, database and other services were not affected.

Reviewed — no current class applies

This is Amazon's attributed account, not an independently established technical postmortem; it does not resolve the competing mechanism account.

Amazon said it implemented additional safeguards, including mandatory peer review for production access, and reviewed the event through its Correction of Error process.

Reviewed — no current class applies

This clause records the safeguards Amazon said it implemented; it does not independently establish their effectiveness.

Financial Times reporting linked the triggering change to Kiro, while Amazon attributed the interruption to human error and misconfigured access controls rather than autonomous AI action.

Classified

Press reporting associated the outage-triggering change with Kiro, while Amazon attributed the event to human error and access-control configuration; the causal mechanism therefore remains materially contested.

Compliance

Conclusion

A bounded AWS service outage occurred, but the available public accounts conflict about whether Kiro had a causal role. VIGIL therefore assesses the materialised service consequence separately from the disputed mechanism and retains an ambiguous mechanism-attribution relationship. The service consequence and disputed Kiro mechanism remain separate. The retained ambiguous mechanism-attribution relationship is a substantive boundary result, not a concluded AI-causation failure or proof that subsequent peer review prevented every recurrence.

Governance significance

The case illustrates why AI attribution in software operations must be based on the actual change-authority and review chain rather than a tool's presence in an engineering workflow.
