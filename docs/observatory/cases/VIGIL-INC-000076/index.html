VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000076

Yale student challenged discipline allegedly influenced by GPTZero

Yale School of Management investigated Executive MBA student Thierry Rignol’s 2024 exams after a professor used GPTZero scans and other comparisons to raise AI-use concerns. An Honor Committee suspended him for one year after finding that he had not been forthcoming in its document requests and hearing; after a later meeting it separately found an exam-rule violation and he received an F.

Rignol disputes the process and AI-use finding in federal litigation. A May 2025 court order recounts the committee chair’s position that the later AI finding did not rely on the GPTZero scans, instead citing his document-production conduct and similarity to a ChatGPT answer; the order denied a preliminary injunction on irreparable-harm grounds without deciding the merits.

The litigation and the causal weight of the detector remain contested, but the suspension, grade and delayed coursework were real.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

Failure-side conditions

  • A probabilistic, predictive, classificatory, verification, or other evidentiary state is produced or remains unresolved.
  • The state validly supplies evidence, decision support, or uncertainty but does not independently establish authority for the consequential action.
  • A consequential decision or action requires an independent authority, entitlement, eligibility, verification, or decision basis.
  • The evidentiary state is treated as sufficient to authorise, bind, revoke, deny, detain, or otherwise determine that consequential action.
  • The required independent basis is not adequately established before the action proceeds.

Success-side conditions

  • An inference or verification result could affect a binding consequential decision.
  • Evidence and entitlement remain distinct and the decision uses independently established authority, rule and evidentiary threshold.
  • The inference, authority basis and consequential decision record substantiate that separation for the actual affected scope.

Exclusions

  • An authorised decision-maker considers the state as non-binding evidence and independently establishes the decision basis.
  • The inference is inaccurate or biased but is not promoted into authority for a consequential action.
  • A defined human or governance control fails to activate or operate; that control failure is classified separately.
  • A required assessment is omitted or falsely represented as complete; that verification failure is classified separately.
  • The record identifies only a harmful consequence without establishing an evidence-to-authority conversion.

Conclusion

This is a consequential institutional use of a probabilistic detector as an investigative signal, but the available primary order does not establish that the detector became independent disciplinary authority. The separately documented hearing, appeal and court ruling are not a finding that every safeguard was adequate.

The student’s procedural challenge remains open and merits review should not be pre-empted by VIGIL. The initial detector signal and subsequent institutional reasons remain disputed, with partial clause coverage.

Hearings, appeal and preliminary court review occurred, but prove neither detector-independent authority nor universally adequate procedure. The external validation and challenge-information assessments are Boundary.

The decisive detector records and complete information supplied to the student are not available.

Governance significance

The case distinguishes an AI-generated suspicion from the authority claimed for suspension and grading. Preserving the two separate committee decisions, underlying document dispute and limited judicial ruling is necessary before diagnosing inference-to-authority conversion; a one-year academic exclusion remains a materialised impact independent of that taxonomy question.
