VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000076

Yale student challenged discipline allegedly influenced by GPTZero

Yale School of Management investigated Executive MBA student Thierry Rignol’s 2024 exams after a professor used GPTZero scans and other comparisons to raise AI-use concerns. An Honor Committee suspended him for one year after finding that he had not been forthcoming in its document requests and hearing; after a later meeting it separately found an exam-rule violation and he received an F. Rignol disputes the process and AI-use finding in federal litigation. A May 2025 court order recounts the committee chair’s position that the later AI finding did not rely on the GPTZero scans, instead citing his document-production conduct and similarity to a ChatGPT answer; the order denied a preliminary injunction on irreparable-harm grounds without deciding the merits. The litigation and the causal weight of the detector remain contested, but the suspension, grade and delayed coursework were real.

Incident breakdown

The court recounts an initial AI-use inquiry with GPTZero scans, subsequent document requests and a hearing; the first suspension finding concerned non-forthcoming conduct.

Classified

GPTZero output materially triggered the disciplinary inquiry, but the preserved court record attributes the later suspension and grade to other stated grounds, leaving the boundary between evidentiary signal and decision authority contested.

The committee later found AI-use violation and assigned an F, but its chair said it did not rely on detector scans, citing conduct and answer similarity; the judge did not adjudicate this explanation.

Unresolved

Specific recognition condition of an AI inference being treated as independent authority for the F remains unestablished; not rejected merely because the internal process is private.

No defined applicable safeguard whose trigger occurred and protective effect failed is established; hearing, appeal and court review did occur, though their adequacy is disputed.

Conclusion

This is a consequential institutional use of a probabilistic detector as an investigative signal, but the available primary order does not establish that the detector became independent disciplinary authority. The separately documented hearing, appeal and court ruling are not a finding that every safeguard was adequate. The student’s procedural challenge remains open and merits review should not be pre-empted by VIGIL. The initial detector signal and subsequent institutional reasons remain disputed, with partial clause coverage. Hearings, appeal and preliminary court review occurred, but prove neither detector-independent authority nor universally adequate procedure. OECD challenge information is only a nonbinding benchmark for the post-May 2024 proceedings; the complete relevant information supplied remains unavailable.

Governance significance

The case distinguishes an AI-generated suspicion from the authority claimed for suspension and grading. Preserving the two separate committee decisions, underlying document dispute and limited judicial ruling is necessary before diagnosing inference-to-authority conversion; a one-year academic exclusion remains a materialised impact independent of that taxonomy question.
