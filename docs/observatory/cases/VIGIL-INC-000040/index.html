VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000040

OpenAI and Google models were reportedly supplied to blacklisted Chinese firms through Singapore entities

The Financial Times reported that OpenAI and Google provided AI model access to Singapore-based subsidiaries of Alibaba, Baidu and Tencent, whose parent companies were on a U.S. defence list. The reporting described the sales as legal under the rules then in force.

The July account also reported that OpenAI had suspended several Alibaba-affiliated users in June over suspected model distillation. The reports do not identify a prohibited transfer through these subsidiaries or a materialised downstream harm from their access.

Conclusion

The evidence establishes reported lawful model-service sales to Singapore-based subsidiaries of listed Chinese parent firms and a separately reported June suspension of Alibaba-affiliated users over suspected distillation. It does not establish an export-control prohibition that the service provider propagated beyond its scope, nor identify the suspected extractor, verified extraction or the evidentiary basis of the suspension.

The earlier Jurisdiction-Bounded Infrastructure Authority mapping treated a parent-company designation as though it were itself a service restriction; the class recognition conditions are not met on this record. Both clauses retain resolved-no-mapping dispositions.

A listed parent is not itself an applicable model-service restriction, and lawful subsidiary sales do not prove circumvention or jurisdictional projection. Suspected extraction does not establish its actor, scale or legal violation.

Independent external relevance review identifies no source-grounded canonical export-control or distillation rule for these reported lawful sales and suspected extraction. A parent-company designation does not identify a model-service prohibition.

No canonical standards finding is admitted; this absence is not a compliance determination.

Governance significance

CAM should distinguish a sovereign designation from a rule that actually governs model-service access, and distinguish a parent company from the subsidiary and users who receive service. Any future finding about restriction or circumvention would need the applicable rule, controlled actor, access route and attributable conduct.
