VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000035

US directive suspended foreign access to Anthropic’s Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models

On 12 June 2026, Anthropic said the U.S. government had ordered it to suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access for foreign nationals, including its own foreign-national employees. Anthropic said it would disable both models for all customers to comply. It disputed the government’s apparent concern about a possible narrow jailbreak and said other Anthropic models would remain available. Later reporting described the restriction being lifted. The order itself and the government’s technical evidence are not preserved in this record.

Conclusion

The source identifies a concrete infrastructure authority projection. The government directive targeted foreign nationals, including people outside the United States; Anthropic then removed Fable 5 and Mythos 5 from all customers to comply. A sovereign access restriction thus became a broader rule of availability through a globally relied-upon provider. VIGIL maps this operational scope expansion to Jurisdiction-Bounded Infrastructure Authority at medium confidence. The classification concerns the infrastructure-mediated effect on users beyond the targeted cohort, not a finding that the government lacked legal power over foreign nationals, that Anthropic acted unlawfully, or that a less restrictive technical implementation was immediately feasible. The undisclosed order and compliance architecture limit stronger causal and legal conclusions. The retained jurisdiction-bounded infrastructure failure is a bounded operational scope-projection finding at medium confidence, alongside successful disclosure of which models remained available. It does not determine the directive’s legal reach, provider liability or technical feasibility of narrower compliance. No source-grounded canonical export-control requirement is independently available from the undisclosed order. Independent external review retains a Boundary for the complete model-deactivation communication plan: reasons, affected models and available alternatives are publicly disclosed, while the maintained plan/contact provisions remain unavailable. The SDOS degradation requirement concerns unavailable or indeterminate governance infrastructure, rather than enforcing a received directive. Neither outcome determines the directive's legal validity.

Governance significance

A host nation’s control over a model provider can become access control for people outside the named regulatory cohort when globally shared infrastructure has no narrower way to apply the order. The governing invariant is to preserve the issuing authority, affected users, jurisdiction, duration and independent basis for any wider restriction. Provider-wide disablement should be visible as a distinct implementation decision with review and restoration paths.
