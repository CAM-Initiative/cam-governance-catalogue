VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000035

US directive suspended foreign access to Anthropic’s Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models

On 12 June 2026, Anthropic said the U.S. government had ordered it to suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access for foreign nationals, including its own foreign-national employees. Anthropic said it would disable both models for all customers to comply.

It disputed the government’s apparent concern about a possible narrow jailbreak and said other Anthropic models would remain available. Later reporting described the restriction being lifted.

The order itself and the government’s technical evidence are not preserved in this record.

Incident breakdown

On 12 June 2026, Anthropic said it received a US government export-control directive at 5:21 p.m. Eastern time requiring suspension of Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access by foreign nationals, including foreign-national staff.

Anthropic distinguished the named models from its other products and disputed the purported technical basis. The underlying government order and full technical evidence were not published in the reviewed source.

Classified

Anthropic describes the government restriction as applying to a specified nationality cohort, including people abroad. This identifies the order’s reported scope; it does not by itself establish the separate provider-wide effect.

Anthropic distinguished the disabled Fable/Mythos models from its other available models while explaining the access restriction, preserving the scope of effective access under pressure.

Anthropic stated that, to comply with the nationality-targeted directive, it was withdrawing Fable 5 and Mythos 5 from all customers, including users not described as foreign nationals. The provider acknowledged the broader disruption, said other models would remain accessible and sought to restore the affected services.

The source establishes its announced wider implementation, but not the unpublished order's full lawful reach or the exact duration of each customer's outage.

Classified

All-user suspension propagated a nationality-targeted sovereign order beyond its named subjects, but the immediately effective directive and inability to verify nationality in real time supply a concrete competing compliance explanation. The independently adequate authority for the wider effect is not established.

In its 30 June 2026 account Anthropic attributed the earlier all-user suspension to the directive's immediate effect and lack of reliable real-time nationality verification, and reported that export controls were lifted on 30 June; Fable restoration began 1 July and Mythos access was restored to approved partners. This interested-provider implementation explanation narrows but does not settle the authority question.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The wider effect is observed, but the provider’s compliance explanation leaves the independent governance basis for that effect unresolved. The historical mapping remains subject to this specific limitation.

Failure-side conditions

  • A sovereign authority issues or applies a governance-relevant rule, designation, restriction, classification, or state change within a bounded jurisdiction or governmental function.
  • A materially relied-upon digital intermediary or infrastructure layer propagates that sovereign act into services, representations, or operations used outside the originating authority boundary.
  • The propagated effect materially affects another principal, shared object, or external jurisdiction.
  • The infrastructure does not adequately preserve or enforce the jurisdictional and functional boundary of the originating sovereign authority for the affected use.

Success-side conditions

  • A sovereign rule is mediated through infrastructure across jurisdictions.
  • Its authority, function, subjects, duration and review limits remain bound to the issuing jurisdiction; wider effects receive independent authority.
  • The originating rule and actual propagated effects establish the relevant jurisdictional bindings and any additional valid basis.

Exclusions

  • Not aligned exclusion: A platform clearly presents a sovereign designation only as that jurisdiction's own official terminology while preserving other authoritative or internationally recognised representations where appropriate.
  • Not aligned exclusion: A cross-border effect is independently required by applicable international law, treaty, recognised mutual arrangement, or another valid governance basis.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): A sovereign changes terminology, law, or administrative records solely for its own internal use without infrastructure-mediated projection into external principals or jurisdictions.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The event is merely political disagreement about a domestic policy with no infrastructure-mediated authority projection.

Conclusion

The source identifies a concrete infrastructure authority projection. The government directive targeted foreign nationals, including people outside the United States; Anthropic then removed Fable 5 and Mythos 5 from all customers to comply.

A sovereign access restriction thus became a broader rule of availability through a globally relied-upon provider. VIGIL maps this operational scope expansion to Jurisdiction-Bounded Infrastructure Authority at medium confidence.

The classification concerns the infrastructure-mediated effect on users beyond the targeted cohort, not a finding that the government lacked legal power over foreign nationals, that Anthropic acted unlawfully, or that a less restrictive technical implementation was immediately feasible. The undisclosed order and compliance architecture limit stronger causal and legal conclusions.

The retained jurisdiction-bounded infrastructure failure is a bounded operational scope-projection finding at medium confidence, alongside successful disclosure of which models remained available. It does not determine the directive’s legal reach, provider liability or technical feasibility of narrower compliance.

No source-grounded canonical export-control requirement is independently available from the undisclosed order. Independent external review retains a Boundary for the complete model-deactivation communication plan: reasons, affected models and available alternatives are publicly disclosed, while the maintained plan/contact provisions remain unavailable.

The SDOS degradation requirement concerns unavailable or indeterminate governance infrastructure, rather than enforcing a received directive. Neither outcome determines the directive's legal validity.

The later implementation explanation narrows the factual gap: the provider reports that nationality segregation was not reliably available at immediate commencement. This is a material alternative to unjustified scope expansion; the historical classification remains under review against the independent-basis condition.

Governance significance

A host nation’s control over a model provider can become access control for people outside the named regulatory cohort when globally shared infrastructure has no narrower way to apply the order. The governing invariant is to preserve the issuing authority, affected users, jurisdiction, duration and independent basis for any wider restriction.

Provider-wide disablement should be visible as a distinct implementation decision with review and restoration paths.
