VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000014

Anthropic enforcement cut off a company’s Claude access for about 15 hours

Independent reporting describes Anthropic suspending more than 60 Claude accounts belonging to Belo after a generic usage-policy enforcement notice, disrupting company workflows and leaving a Google Form as the reported appeal route. Access was restored after roughly 15 hours and the suspension was described as a false positive.

Conclusion

The key distinction is between trigger correctness and control activation integrity. A false positive can arise upstream: a classifier or evidence process can incorrectly represent the triggering condition as satisfied, after which an enforcement control may operate exactly as designed relative to that erroneous state. Control Activation Validity intentionally excludes that mechanism. The remaining governance problem is still substantial: high-blast-radius enforcement should have proportionate trigger assurance, scoped propagation, intelligible reason disclosure, continuity-safe degradation and an appeal path capable of correcting erroneous predicates quickly. The current taxonomy does not provide a sufficiently evidenced selectable class for that upstream false-positive enforcement mechanism in this occurrence. Access-state differentiation is a bounded affirmative success, alongside activation and delivery boundary findings; it does not validate the false-positive predicate or the appeal’s effectiveness. Coverage remains partial because the route from appeal to a capable destination is unresolved. No external met or not-met finding follows: the selected NIST actions and SDOS runtime provisions retain unestablished action-specific implementation and adoption conditions, and held historical paths remain excluded.

Governance significance

This incident is a useful taxonomy-boundary case. It demonstrates why a wrong enforcement outcome is not automatically an Control Activation Validity occurrence: classification must identify where the governance failure happened. CAM should separately govern predicate quality, enforcement scope, service continuity and reviewability so that an erroneous upstream signal cannot disable an entire legitimate organisation without bounded recovery.
