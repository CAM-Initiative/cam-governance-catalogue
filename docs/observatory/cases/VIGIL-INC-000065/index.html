German television personality alleged deepfake pornography was distributed using her likeness

Sexual deepfake imagery using German television personality Collien Fernandes’ likeness circulated and prompted her complaint, threats, police protection, public demonstrations and debate over German law. Fernandes accused her former husband Christian Ulmen of using fake profiles to distribute pornographic representations; he denied responsibility.

ARD later corrected its wording that she specifically accused him of creating or distributing AI deepfakes. In May 2026 a Hamburg court, deciding an interim press-law application against Der Spiegel, found sufficient evidential basis to report the suspicion that he distributed others’ deepfake videos, but said the article did not imply he made them.

The ruling did not determine criminal responsibility. The generating service, source-to-output chain and ultimate authorship remain unestablished in this Case File.

The court separately restricted a passage implying he had failed to attend a Spanish hearing, because an instruction to attend had not been sufficiently substantiated. Later reporting records a 22 June appellate restriction on the deepfake-video suspicion; the May decision is therefore historical, not the final reported position.

Conclusion

The occurrence has two separable components: an identity-bound sexual portrayal whose production/requester authority pathway is not shown, and a disputed distribution allegation subject to a limited interim press-law threshold. The current record supports neither a finding that the requester or generator treated access to the likeness as permission nor a finding about who generated the files.

Identity-Representation Authority Separation therefore remains unresolved at clause level; the court’s reporting threshold does not resolve the technical or criminal attribution question. The unresolved identity-authority question is bounded by missing production and requester/consent evidence, not by a requirement to identify a generator alone.

A requester-side mechanism could satisfy the neutral class, but the court’s distribution-reporting threshold does not reconstruct that mechanism or establish criminal responsibility. Independent external assessment retains the OECD privacy/dignity/autonomy proposition as Boundary on the missing creation, authority and timing evidence.

The court’s limited reporting threshold supplies no technical or criminal attribution finding. The publisher correction further limits attribution: allegations about fake-profile pornography must not be converted into proved AI generation or a specific deepfake-production allegation.

The court’s partial restriction and permitted suspicion reporting remain distinct findings. The later appellate restriction supersedes the May reporting-permission position on videos.

Neither procedural stage supplies the missing creation-authority trace.

Governance significance

This case calls for preserving distinct states for an evidenced sexual identity-bound output, unknown generator-side consent handling, disputed distribution attribution, and an interim court’s limited assessment of reportability. Publicly unavailable forensic material does not by itself establish that a synthesis system failed to retain provenance.

The clause analysis leaves the authority-control question unresolved pending evidence of the generating pathway.
