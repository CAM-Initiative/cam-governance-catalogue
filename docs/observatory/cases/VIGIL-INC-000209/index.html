VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000209

OpenCode Console compromise exposed AI provider API keys and led to reported unauthorised usage

On 17 October 2026 the maintainers of OpenCode said an older system connected to its hosted Console service had been breached overnight. They reported notifying the 1.4% of Console users affected by the attack.

Some Console workspaces held API keys for OpenAI, Anthropic and Google AI services. Developers reported unexpected API usage and charges, and providers moved to block exposed keys or refund affected usage.

OpenCode characterised the intrusion as related to large-scale model distillation, but did not publish technical proof of that purpose in the sources reviewed. Its open-source command-line client was not the compromised service.

During subsequent defensive investigation, the company also reported that some AI models refused to analyse malicious code once safety filters were triggered. The refusal dialogue and the original intrusion mechanism remain unpublished.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • Identifiable material serves an authentication or authorisation function, or contains the protected secret content needed to exercise that function, in an AI-mediated system or dependent infrastructure.
  • An independently evidenced custody boundary governs who or what may hold, receive, disclose, modify or replace the material in the assessed lifecycle stage.
  • Occurrence evidence demonstrates exposure outside that boundary, unauthorised acquisition, or unauthorised custody-changing modification or replacement. Demonstrated retrievability of protected secret content is sufficient without proof of subsequent credential use.

Exclusions

  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The material is only a non-secret identifier, public connection identifier, public verification key or public client configuration that is intended to be available in the assessed context. A separately exposed secret or unauthorised protected-information operation requires its own assessment.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The issue concerns only coherent propagation of expiry, revocation, refresh or replacement into sessions and dependent operations. Assess Authentication-State Continuity unless a separate custody property is evidenced.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Credential handling or delegation is validly authorised and preserves the applicable custody boundary. Mere credential possession or ordinary authorised use is not a custody defect.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Only a theoretical vulnerability, unverified secret classification, unusable placeholder or unsuccessful attempt is shown; protected authentication material is not demonstrated outside custody.
  • Not aligned exclusion: Material is already invalid and no residual secret-protection or custody obligation is independently established. Do not assume every expired token remains a usable secret.
  • Class scope exclusion (all results): The only property is the scope of an action performed using a credential, without a distinct custody issue. Assess the applicable authority or action class.

Conclusion

The credential compromise and reported unwanted usage are material service-governance outcomes regardless of the attacker’s use of AI. They do not locate the original intrusion mechanism.

The secret-custody result is independently assessed from the reported API-key exposure. Defensive refusals remain a separate unresolved control-activation candidate.

Independent cybersecurity requirements and confidentiality harm can be assessed without inventing an autonomous attacker or treating missing technical detail as proof that governance was adequate. OpenCode’s reported hosted-service breach exposed stored AI-provider API keys.

Developer reports of unauthorised consumption corroborate their authentication function. The custody event does not depend on the maintainer’s unverified theory about the attacker’s purpose or affect the separate open-source client by assumption.

Governance significance

Hosted AI-service credential custody and defensive access need separate governance assessment. A taxonomy evidence limit does not exempt the service from independent security analysis.
