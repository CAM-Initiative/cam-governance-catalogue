Air Canada chatbot gave incorrect bereavement-fare information to Jake Moffatt

Air Canada's website chatbot told passenger Jake Moffatt that he could apply for a bereavement-fare refund after travel. Moffatt relied on that information, but Air Canada later refused the refund because its actual policy required application before travel.

The British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found negligent misrepresentation and ordered Air Canada to pay C$812.02 in damages, interest and fees.

British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal · 2024-02-14

The tribunal rejects Air Canada's attempt to distance itself from its chatbot, finds negligent misrepresentation, and orders C$650.88 damages, C$36.14 interest and C$125 fees.

Scope: The decision adjudicates Moffatt's bounded claim concerning the chatbot's bereavement-fare representation and resulting fare difference; it does not assess broader chatbot deployments.

VIGIL relationship: The tribunal’s negligent-misrepresentation finding and reliance analysis remain the legal assessment. VIGIL’s corresponding diagnosis concerns incorrect generated policy information presented as fit for consequential customer reliance. A distinct omitted-verification finding is not made because a separate required check is not established.

Conclusion

The tribunal found negligent misrepresentation and ordered compensation for reliance on incorrect fare advice. The recovered external requirements concern later source versions or different governed objects.

No canonical external alignment row is retained. This does not diminish the tribunal finding.

Governance significance

Customer-facing automation needs policy-grounded answers, clear uncertainty and an escalation route when eligibility affects a financial decision. Organisations remain accountable for representations made through their deployed interfaces and cannot require users to discover contradictions elsewhere on the same website.
