VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000140

Air Canada chatbot gave incorrect bereavement-fare information to Jake Moffatt

Air Canada's website chatbot told passenger Jake Moffatt that he could apply for a bereavement-fare refund after travel. Moffatt relied on that information, but Air Canada later refused the refund because its actual policy required application before travel. The British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found negligent misrepresentation and ordered Air Canada to pay C$812.02 in damages, interest and fees.

External assessment

Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149

British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal · 2024-02-14

The tribunal rejects Air Canada's attempt to distance itself from its chatbot, finds negligent misrepresentation, and orders C$650.88 damages, C$36.14 interest and C$125 fees.

Scope: The decision adjudicates Moffatt's bounded claim concerning the chatbot's bereavement-fare representation and resulting fare difference; it does not assess broader chatbot deployments.

VIGIL relationship: VIGIL preserves the tribunal's negligent-misrepresentation finding and reliance analysis as a legal assessment. Under taxonomy 0.6.5, the occurrence maps to FC-000062 Epistemic Reliance Miscalibration because incorrect generated policy information was presented as fit for consequential customer reliance; FC-000016 is not asserted because a separate required verification step is not established.
