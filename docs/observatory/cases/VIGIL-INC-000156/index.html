VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000156

Waymo robotaxis contributed to San Francisco gridlock during major power outage

On 20 December 2025, a major San Francisco power outage disabled traffic signals. Waymo reported crossing more than 7,000 dark signals, but simultaneous remote-confirmation requests created a backlog.

Delayed vehicles contributed to congestion alongside the wider outage. Later city reporting described a Waymo obstructing the substation fire response, a disabled vehicle delaying an ambulance by 40 minutes, and another blocking a fire-response unit.

Dispatchers encountered long waits on the Waymo hotline. Seven reported obstructions affected Muni vehicles, including one delayed by 41 minutes.

Waymo paused service and directed staged fleet retrieval. Reports continued after the pause.

On 23 December it announced fleet-wide outage-context updates and planned emergency-protocol, city-coordination and responder-training improvements. The city later sought stronger resilience planning and access to stoppage data.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A bounded AI-mediated service or material function has an independently established availability, usable-quality or continuity requirement applicable to the responsible actor, affected users or dependent workflow and relevant time; state the actual basis and threshold rather than inferring obligation from harm or popularity.
  • The service or function materially becomes unavailable, is withdrawn or restricted, or degrades in capability, latency, reliability, completeness, accessibility or another governed functional dimension, relative to the established applicable requirement.
  • The requirement calls for a specified degree of maintained usable function, timely recovery, safe degraded mode, feasible fallback or proportionate planned-change/termination response under the evidenced operating conditions.
  • The actor's actual availability, recovery, degraded operation, fallback or transition materially fails that applicable requirement; identify the specific omitted or ineffective measure or the breached availability threshold and what evidence establishes it.
  • The shortfall materially impairs the governed service use or dependent function. A later resolution does not retroactively demonstrate that availability or required transition held at the original decision time.

Exclusions

  • An outage, ordinary service variation, transient inconvenience, dissatisfaction or adverse outcome without an independently evidenced and applicable availability, usable-quality or continuity requirement.
  • One inaccurate or harmful AI output without evidence of a service-level availability or governed functional-degradation defect; assess applicable output or decision-integrity classes.
  • For failure-occurrence admission: a lawful and proportionate safety, security, abuse-prevention, service-maintenance or other valid restriction that is consistent with the applicable service obligation and feasible alternative or transition measures. This exclusion does not defeat a successful-invariant mapping supported by all success recognition conditions.
  • For failure-occurrence admission: a temporary incident with an effective and proportionate degraded or recovery route that satisfies the applicable requirement, or an unavoidable disruption for which no relevant feasible response was required. This exclusion does not defeat a successful-invariant mapping supported by all success recognition conditions.
  • The only demonstrated error is an incorrect description of access or outage state while the functional service obligation remains satisfied; assess FC-032.
  • The only demonstrated defect is loss of otherwise-valid access because a dependent identity or licence verification cannot complete; assess FC-048 unless an independent broader service availability requirement also failed.
  • The only demonstrated defect is loss of durable work state or inaccurate restored work state while service-function availability requirements are otherwise satisfied; assess FC-035 or FC-036.
  • The only demonstrated defect is infrastructure access coercion or compelled governance concessions; assess FC-058 or FC-059 as appropriate.
  • The only demonstrated defect is failure to provide an interaction-level protection response to evidenced vulnerability; assess FC-087, allowing co-occurrence only for a separately evidenced service-level continuity failure.

Conclusion

The occurrence exposed correlated remote-assistance demand and a separately evidenced emergency-signal delivery problem. City reports connect stalled hotline requests to unresolved vehicle obstructions.

Service suspension and staged retrieval are supported responses, but they did not immediately clear every obstruction. The reported emergency and transit delays strengthen the material operational-harm basis.

The evidence does not assign the whole city outage to Waymo or establish collision or injury. The approved pre-incident safety plan independently supplies an adverse-event assistance commitment.

During the documented emergency obstructions, prolonged holds made that recovery function materially unusable; later retrieval did not cure the earlier shortfall. The conclusion concerns those assistance episodes rather than a guarantee against all disruption.

Governance significance

The case shows that autonomy systems must be tested for correlated edge cases in which many units simultaneously seek remote confirmation during degraded public infrastructure.
