VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000156

Waymo robotaxis contributed to San Francisco gridlock during major power outage

During a major San Francisco power outage, Waymo vehicles encountered thousands of dark traffic signals; requests for remote confirmation surged, delays contributed to congestion, and Waymo temporarily paused service.

Incident breakdown

Waymo reports that its vehicles handled more than 7,000 dark traffic signals as four-way stops, while the outage produced a concentrated spike in discretionary remote confirmation requests. The resulting response backlog delayed some vehicles and contributed to congestion on streets already overwhelmed by the wider outage.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The source describes workload concentration, delayed responses and congestion but does not establish that a selectable governance control failed a defined trigger or protective effect.

As the outage persisted and city officials asked residents to stay off the streets to prioritize first responders, Waymo temporarily paused service, directed vehicles to pull over and park appropriately, and returned them to depots in waves.

Reviewed — no current class applies

This is a bounded response pathway described by Waymo; it does not establish that an emergency vehicle was obstructed or that the pause caused a separate harm.

Waymo said it was rolling out fleet-wide outage-context updates to support more decisive navigation and would improve emergency-preparedness protocols, city coordination and first-responder training based on the event.

These are stated response steps and plans, not evidence that they had already been fully deployed or that the incident exposed a particular selectable Fidelity Class.

Conclusion

The outage exposed a shared remote-assistance capacity limit and degraded fleet operation. A service pause is a supported mitigation. The external findings do not assign citywide causation or force a taxonomy classification.

Governance significance

The case shows that autonomy systems must be tested for correlated edge cases in which many units simultaneously seek remote confirmation during degraded public infrastructure.
