UnitedHealth algorithm allegedly supported Medicare Advantage care denials

A putative class action filed in Minnesota alleges that UnitedHealthcare and naviHealth used the nH Predict care-management model to curtail Medicare Advantage post-acute coverage for older patients before their treating clinicians considered them ready for discharge. The complaint describes nH Predict as generating expected care durations and alleges that employees were pressured to keep skilled-nursing stays close to those projections.

Named plaintiffs alleged that coverage was terminated despite treating-clinician objections, leaving some families to pay substantial costs or forgo further care. In February 2025, a federal judge allowed breach-of-contract and implied-covenant claims to proceed while dismissing several other claims; the surviving contract theory turns on whether UnitedHealthcare complied with plan language stating that clinical staff and physicians make coverage decisions when plaintiffs allege AI was used instead.

As of March 2026, that merits question remained unresolved and in discovery.

Conclusion

The evidence supports a provisional two-part governance interpretation. First, the complaint describes a population-derived prediction being treated as practically determinative of an individual patient’s post-acute coverage despite contrary clinical information; if established, that is an evidence-to-authority conversion rather than ordinary decision support.

Second, the surviving contract claims and later discovery orders identify a distinct route question: whether a process represented as requiring clinical staff and physician decision-making was allegedly displaced by nH Predict. Both mechanisms remain allegation-bounded because the court has not determined that nH Predict actually dictated the named plaintiffs’ coverage decisions.

The record therefore preserves the mappings at medium confidence rather than treating the litigation posture as proof of liability or of every pleaded factual claim. Both retained failure roles remain provisional on the explicitly pleaded mechanisms, without treating surviving claims or discovery orders as merits proof.

The coverage-contract representation is a separate source-grounded process duty but has no matching admitted canonical EXTREQ; it is not relabelled as a later standards duty. Independent external assessment retains a Boundary for the RMF validity/reliability proposition where publication overlaps the occurrence range and validation, generalisation limits and determinative-use facts remain unresolved.

Later source versions stay audit-only; the source-grounded coverage contract is not relabelled as a canonical EXTREQ or a medical/merits finding.

Governance significance

The occurrence separates two governance questions that should not be collapsed. A predictive care-duration estimate may be a legitimate decision-support input, but it must not become individual entitlement authority beyond what the estimate can establish.

Separately, where the governing coverage process represents consequential decisions as being made by clinical staff and physicians, an alternate automated pathway must not displace that required review route without an authorised equivalent. The litigation remains important precisely because those alleged mechanisms are testable without assuming the plaintiffs have already proved them.
