UnitedHealth algorithm allegedly supported Medicare Advantage care denials

A putative class action filed in Minnesota alleges that UnitedHealthcare and naviHealth used the nH Predict care-management model to curtail Medicare Advantage post-acute coverage for older patients before their treating clinicians considered them ready for discharge. The complaint describes nH Predict as generating expected care durations and alleges that employees were pressured to keep skilled-nursing stays close to those projections. Named plaintiffs alleged that coverage was terminated despite treating-clinician objections, leaving some families to pay substantial costs or forgo further care. In February 2025, a federal judge allowed breach-of-contract and implied-covenant claims to proceed while dismissing several other claims; the surviving contract theory turns on whether UnitedHealthcare complied with plan language stating that clinical staff and physicians make coverage decisions when plaintiffs allege AI was used instead. As of March 2026, that merits question remained unresolved and in discovery.

Refusal of Recovery: How Medicare Advantage Insurers Have Denied Patients Access to Post-Acute Care

U.S. Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations · 2024-10-17

The investigation reports increased denials of post-acute care and examines insurer use of predictive tools and internal processes that restricted beneficiaries' access to medically necessary care.

Scope: The Senate investigation assesses a broader Medicare Advantage denial pattern that includes UnitedHealth and algorithm-supported post-acute-care decisions; it does not adjudicate every individual denial in this Incident cluster.

VIGIL relationship: The Senate assessment evaluates insurer practices and access consequences. VIGIL separately bands the materialised essential-care deprivation under VIGIL-HIM and does not treat the external assessment as controlling.
