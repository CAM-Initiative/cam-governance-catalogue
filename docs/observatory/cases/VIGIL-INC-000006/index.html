VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000006

Porcha Woodruff arrest following facial-recognition misidentification

A Detroit police facial-recognition search produced 73 candidates and an investigator narrowed them to Porcha Woodruff as an investigative lead, followed by internal review and a separately prepared photo lineup in which the victim selected Woodruff. A warrant followed; Woodruff, then eight months pregnant, spent about ten hours in detention, experienced contractions, and received hospital treatment after release. Police later determined she was not the perpetrator and the charges were dropped. In 2025 a federal court granted summary judgment to the remaining officer, finding that Woodruff had not shown a lack of probable cause.

Incident breakdown

The facial-recognition search produced 73 candidates and an investigator narrowed them to Woodruff as the investigative lead; the selection was peer reviewed.

Classified

The facial-recognition nomination materially structured the identity pathway, but peer review and a separately prepared witness lineup supplied additional evidence before the warrant. Because the preserved record does not establish that the biometric inference itself became sufficient authority for arrest, the occurrence materially engages the inferential-evidence boundary without satisfying a failure-occurrence finding.

A separately prepared photo lineup included Woodruff, and the victim selected her while stating that the choice was based on seeing the suspect in person for multiple hours.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The separately prepared lineup and victim identification are material evidence independent of the machine score itself, limiting any claim that the biometric inference alone acquired coercive authority; the occurrence remains informative to the class boundary because the machine nomination determined whose identity entered the downstream verification pathway.

Woodruff was eight months pregnant when arrested, and police later determined that she was not the perpetrator.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The advanced-pregnancy contradiction is material to whether the overall identity pathway remained adequately defeasible, but it does not by itself establish that a probabilistic machine inference was promoted into independent authority for arrest.

Peer review and a separate photo lineup were completed, but the mistaken identification still progressed.

Classified

Peer review and a separately prepared photo lineup were performed, but their independence and sufficiency did not prevent mistaken identity from reaching arrest; this materially distinguishes inadequate verification from omission of verification.

A facial-recognition nomination became part of the pathway leading to arrest.

Classified

The facial-recognition nomination entered a reliance-bearing arrest pathway, but the specific mechanism is inferential identity evidence acquiring coercive authority, materially distinguishing it from a generic assurance-to-reliance mismatch.

Conclusion

This occurrence is not accurately described as 'algorithm makes match, police automatically arrest'. The machine output was an investigative lead; human narrowing, peer review, a witness lineup, warrant preparation, prosecutorial review and judicial approval followed. The governance question is therefore narrower: the originating biometric inference selected the identity around which later verification was organised, but the preserved record also contains additional evidence that prevents treating the machine inference itself as the demonstrated authority basis for arrest. The advanced-pregnancy mismatch remains important because it was an occurrence-specific contradiction that should have prompted renewed identity assurance before coercive action. Inferential Evidence–Authority Separation is therefore retained as an ambiguous-boundary finding rather than a failure occurrence: the event tests how machine-generated identity evidence should remain defeasible through downstream review without overstating what the preserved evidence establishes. The completed peer review and witness lineup affirmatively distinguish this pathway from omitted verification, but the mistaken identification and unresolved sufficiency do not establish a successful invariant. All retained canonical relationships remain ambiguous boundaries. The summary-judgment outcome and the court’s express refusal to decide the amicus theory prevent a blanket illegality conclusion. External review yields no met or not-met finding: later versions are temporally excluded, while applicable-framework undertaking and source-defined validation conditions remain unresolved for the earlier guidance.

Governance significance

This incident demonstrates why 'human in the loop' is not equivalent to independent assurance. Consequential identity workflows should preserve the provenance of machine-generated nominations, distinguish review of the same inference from genuinely independent corroboration, and require contradictions capable of falsifying the candidate to be actively resolved before detention or comparable coercive effects. The later Detroit policy—requiring additional independent reliable evidence beyond a facial-recognition lead and a lineup—illustrates the control shape that this occurrence made necessary without being treated as proof that the later policy was already legally required at the time.
