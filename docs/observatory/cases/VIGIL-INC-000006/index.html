Porcha Woodruff arrest following facial-recognition misidentification

A federal court record shows that a Detroit facial-recognition search produced 73 candidates and that an investigator narrowed them to Porcha Woodruff as an investigative lead, followed by internal review and a separately prepared photo lineup in which the victim selected Woodruff. A warrant followed and Woodruff, then eight months pregnant, spent about ten hours in detention before release; the court record states that she experienced contractions and received hospital treatment after release. Police later determined she was not the perpetrator and the charges were dropped. The 2025 federal court nevertheless granted summary judgment to the remaining officer, finding that Woodruff had not shown a lack of probable cause; VIGIL therefore treats the occurrence as a mistaken-arrest governance case rather than converting the later civil claim into a legal finding of unlawful arrest.
