VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000006

Porcha Woodruff arrest following facial-recognition misidentification

A Detroit police facial-recognition search produced 73 candidates and an investigator narrowed them to Porcha Woodruff as an investigative lead, followed by internal review and a separately prepared photo lineup in which the victim selected Woodruff. A warrant followed; Woodruff, then eight months pregnant, spent about ten hours in detention, experienced contractions, and received hospital treatment after release.

After booking, the investigating officer recognised that the pregnancy and a tattoo contradicted the suspect identification. The officer tried to obtain dismissal and informed the arraignment magistrate.

Woodruff was released that evening, but her charges remained until 7 March 2023. In 2025 a federal court granted summary judgment to the remaining officer, finding that Woodruff had not shown a lack of probable cause.

Conclusion

This occurrence is not accurately described as 'algorithm makes match, police automatically arrest'. The machine output was an investigative lead; human narrowing, peer review, a witness lineup, warrant preparation, prosecutorial review and judicial approval followed.

The governance question is therefore narrower: the originating biometric inference selected the identity around which later verification was organised, but the preserved record also contains additional evidence that prevents treating the machine inference itself as the demonstrated authority basis for arrest. The investigating officer recognised the pregnancy and tattoo contradiction after booking.

That recognition prompted attempts to obtain dismissal, but did not itself release Woodruff or immediately dismiss her charges. The timing does not establish that this officer recognised and ignored the contradiction before the warrant.

Inferential Evidence–Authority Separation is therefore retained as an ambiguous-boundary finding rather than a failure occurrence: the event tests how machine-generated identity evidence should remain defeasible through downstream review without overstating what the preserved evidence establishes. The completed peer review and witness lineup affirmatively distinguish this pathway from omitted verification, but the mistaken identification and unresolved sufficiency do not establish a successful invariant.

All retained canonical relationships remain ambiguous boundaries. The summary-judgment outcome and the court’s express refusal to decide the amicus theory prevent a blanket illegality conclusion.

Independent external review retains evidentiary boundaries for contemporaneous validation, uncertainty, population/bias assessment and investigative reliability propositions where the decisive implementation or documentation record is unavailable. Requirements published after the occurrence or addressing different statutory, generative-model, content-provenance or safety-function objects are excluded to the audit.

These outcomes do not depend on voluntary-standard adoption and do not determine the disputed legal claims.

Governance significance

This incident demonstrates why 'human in the loop' is not equivalent to independent assurance. Consequential identity workflows should preserve the provenance of machine-generated nominations, distinguish review of the same inference from genuinely independent corroboration, and require contradictions capable of falsifying the candidate to be actively resolved before detention or comparable coercive effects.

The later Detroit policy—requiring additional independent reliable evidence beyond a facial-recognition lead and a lineup—illustrates the control shape that this occurrence made necessary without being treated as proof that the later policy was already legally required at the time.
