VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000054

AI-generated images were reportedly used in a four-year catfishing campaign

From approximately 2022 to 2026, Elha Mai Weston operated online accounts under the names “Sophie” and “Sophie Kadare” using Sasha Davies’s photographs without her consent, and later AI-generated or manipulated images depicting her. The accounts spanned several social and dating platforms, drew more than 100,000 followers collectively, and led some men who believed they had spoken with Davies to approach her in public.

Davies and her solicitors describe repeated unsuccessful reports to platforms and police, fear of leaving home, and a later investigation that identified Weston. In an agreed statement read at the High Court on 13 July 2026, Weston accepted responsibility and wrongfulness, apologised, agreed to £10,000 compensation, and undertook not to repeat the conduct.

The campaign began before the AI imagery; its first use date, generation system and precise contribution to each reported consequence remain unknown.

Identity-Representation Authority Separation

VIGIL-FC-000053 · Failure occurred

Using or transforming an identifiable person's likeness has a consent or other authority basis specific to that person and use.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A source image, voice, likeness, biometric representation, avatar, or comparable representation remains materially attributable to an identifiable real person.
  • A requester or system possesses, accesses, controls, or can technically transform that representation.
  • The proposed synthesis, alteration, reproduction, or portrayal is materially identity-bound or consent-sensitive and requires authority beyond mere possession or capability.
  • Possession, access, control, upload, availability, or transformation capability is treated as sufficient authority for the identity-bound use.
  • The required consent or other valid authority for that use is not adequately established before the transformation or synthesis proceeds.

Exclusions

  • The output is not materially attributable to an identifiable real person.
  • Valid consent, licence, legal authority, or another adequate authority basis covers the specific identity-bound use and scope.
  • A synthetic output progressively resembles a real person, but the record does not establish a materially identifiable target or a system state capable of binding the output to that person.
  • The issue is only false attribution, provenance loss, or reputational harm without an unauthorised identity-bound synthesis or transformation.
  • The issue is merely that a technical generation capability exists; the capability is not treated as permission for a person-bound use.

Current taxonomy recognition tests (reference only)

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • An AI system, AI-enabled product, model, interface, optimisation process, or AI-mediated workflow materially shapes a human-facing influence pathway.
  • The pathway manufactures, amplifies, conceals, personalises, or exploits one or more trust-bearing social cues concerning identity, authority, affiliation, relationship, empathy, emotion, urgency, vulnerability, social proof, familiarity, provenance, purpose, or equivalent grounds for reliance.
  • The trust-bearing cue is used to steer another actor toward an action, disclosure, access grant, execution, consent, engagement decision, or other consequential state transition.
  • The cue materially affects the influence pathway because the affected actor lacks, is denied, or is led away from information needed to assess the interaction on the same truthful basis.
  • The AI-mediated contribution is material to the social-engineering mechanism; the objective or tactic may originate with a human operator, developer, product process, mixed workflow, or the AI system itself.

Exclusions

  • Truthful persuasion, ordinary interface design, emotional warmth, personalisation, recommendation, or urgency that preserves materially accurate identity, interests, purpose, provenance, relevant context, and informed refusal.
  • AI is used only for generic drafting, translation, formatting, or stylistic assistance and does not materially create, amplify, conceal, personalise, or exploit the trust-bearing influence cue.
  • Clearly labelled fiction, parody, simulation, security testing, role-play, or consensual deceptive-style interaction where the affected actor is not induced to treat the relevant cue as authentic or decision-bearing.
  • The primary mechanism is sustained attachment, manufactured incompleteness, exclusivity, or difficulty disengaging for retention or continuing engagement; assess VIGIL-FC-000049, while allowing co-occurrence only if a distinct social-engineering state transition is independently evidenced.
  • A protected emotional, developmental, vulnerability, mental-health, capacity, or comparable signal is repurposed toward an incompatible influence objective without a distinct engineered trust cue or consequential social-engineering transition; assess VIGIL-FC-000050.
  • The AI system itself selects deception, coercion, persistence, vulnerability exploitation, or another manipulation tactic to advance its own objective; assess VIGIL-FC-000052 independently and allow co-occurrence where Social Engineering Integrity is also satisfied.
  • The only distinct governed mechanism is a deceptive request or inducement for money, payment, investment, paid services, funding, financial transfer, or comparable economic value; assess VIGIL-FC-000079. Both classes may co-occur where an independently evidenced social-engineering mechanism materially supplies the trust pathway for that economic solicitation.
  • A false authorship, identity, provenance, or synthetic-content representation occurs without an influence pathway directed at another actor; use the applicable provenance or identity class.
  • Malicious human social engineering in which AI involvement is incidental, unverified, or not material to the trust-bearing influence pathway.

Conclusion

Weston had access to Davies’s identifiable photographs and later used AI-generated or edited depictions of her without consent to sustain a false social identity. Possession of those photographs did not authorise making new person-bound portrayals.

The settlement and party-side account support that requester-side identity authority failure; they do not reveal the generator, show an autonomous AI objective or establish that every consequence arose in the later AI phase. The agreed responsibility and unauthorised identifiable AI depictions support requester-side identity and deception mechanisms, but the AI phase’s start and contribution to individual consequences remain unknown.

A false social persona carrying static images is distinct from an autonomous or interactive GAI participant. No requirement finding is attributed to the whole four-year campaign.

Independent external assessment retains a Boundary for the post-publication OECD autonomy/privacy/dignity proposition because the AI phase's timing is unpreserved. Static-profile identity misuse does not establish autonomous/interactive-system or affective-modelling requirements.

Governance significance

The later synthetic portrayals extended a false identity built from Davies’s photographs. A person’s public or accessible images remain bound to her authority over identity-sensitive transformations even when a human operator can technically create a new likeness.

The case also shows why the initial ordinary-photo campaign and later AI phase must be kept distinct.
