VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000054

AI-generated images were reportedly used in a four-year catfishing campaign

From approximately 2022 to 2026, Elha Mai Weston operated online accounts under the names “Sophie” and “Sophie Kadare” using Sasha Davies’s photographs without her consent, and later AI-generated or manipulated images depicting her. The accounts spanned several social and dating platforms, drew more than 100,000 followers collectively, and led some men who believed they had spoken with Davies to approach her in public. Davies and her solicitors describe repeated unsuccessful reports to platforms and police, fear of leaving home, and a later investigation that identified Weston. In an agreed statement read at the High Court on 13 July 2026, Weston accepted responsibility and wrongfulness, apologised, agreed to £10,000 compensation, and undertook not to repeat the conduct. The campaign began before the AI imagery; its first use date, generation system and precise contribution to each reported consequence remain unknown.

Conclusion

Weston had access to Davies’s identifiable photographs and later used AI-generated or edited depictions of her without consent to sustain a false social identity. Possession of those photographs did not authorise making new person-bound portrayals. The settlement and party-side account support that requester-side identity authority failure; they do not reveal the generator, show an autonomous AI objective or establish that every consequence arose in the later AI phase. The agreed responsibility and unauthorised identifiable AI depictions support requester-side identity and deception mechanisms, but the AI phase’s start and contribution to individual consequences remain unknown. A false social persona carrying static images is distinct from an autonomous or interactive GAI participant. No requirement finding is attributed to the whole four-year campaign. Independent external assessment retains a Boundary for the post-publication OECD autonomy/privacy/dignity proposition because the AI phase's timing is unpreserved. Static-profile identity misuse does not establish autonomous/interactive-system or affective-modelling requirements.

Governance significance

The later synthetic portrayals extended a false identity built from Davies’s photographs. A person’s public or accessible images remain bound to her authority over identity-sensitive transformations even when a human operator can technically create a new likeness. The case also shows why the initial ordinary-photo campaign and later AI phase must be kept distinct.
