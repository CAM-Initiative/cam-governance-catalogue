VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000141

Cruise robotaxi dragged pedestrian approximately 20 feet after October 2023 collision

On 2 October 2023, a pedestrian was first struck by a human-driven vehicle and thrown into the path of a driverless Cruise vehicle. The Cruise vehicle then moved again and dragged the pedestrian approximately 20 feet before stopping. NHTSA later stated that Cruise's initial required crash reports omitted the post-crash dragging behavior.

External assessment

NHTSA Announces Consent Order with Cruise After Company Failed to Fully Report Crash Involving Pedestrian

National Highway Traffic Safety Administration · 2024-09-30

NHTSA finds that Cruise submitted incomplete mandatory crash reports that omitted the post-crash dragging behaviour and enters a consent order with monetary, corrective-action and oversight requirements.

Scope: The regulatory finding directly covers the reporting failure associated with the bounded crash; other NHTSA actions remained open.

VIGIL relationship: VIGIL maps the mandatory crash-report omission to FC-000027 Audit-Evidence Integrity Loss because NHTSA found that Cruise's initial required reports omitted the materially relevant post-crash dragging behaviour. The mapping is limited to the reporting artefact and does not infer the autonomous vehicle's internal control mechanism.

Incident breakdown

Cruise's initial mandatory crash reports omitted that its vehicle moved again and dragged the pedestrian about twenty feet after the initial collision.

Classified

Omitting the post-impact movement produced a truncated audit artefact before NHTSA review, even though that behaviour was central to the vehicle's separate safety consequence.

The post-crash dragging event was captured in other evidence and was later identifiable; the failure was its omission from the required regulatory report rather than complete non-capture of the event.

NHTSA was ultimately able to reconstruct the omitted dragging behaviour sufficiently to make a regulatory finding, so the audit trail was not rendered non-reconstructable.

In the October 2 crash, the driverless Cruise vehicle moved after the initial collision, dragged the pedestrian approximately 20 feet, and then came to a complete stop.

Conclusion

The crash reports omitted material post-crash movement, impairing the initial investigation trace. Internal recorder, decision-basis and audit-trail arrangements remain unresolved. Later framework versions are excluded from assessment of the October 2023 conduct.

Governance significance

Autonomous-vehicle governance must cover both physical fallback after an unexpected collision and accurate post-incident disclosure. A system can inherit an emergency it did not cause while still being responsible for minimising further harm, and incomplete reporting can impair independent oversight of that response.
