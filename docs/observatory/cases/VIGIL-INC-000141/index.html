Cruise robotaxi dragged pedestrian approximately 20 feet after October 2023 collision

On 2 October 2023, a pedestrian was first struck by a human-driven vehicle and thrown into the path of a driverless Cruise vehicle. The Cruise vehicle then moved again and dragged the pedestrian approximately 20 feet before stopping.

NHTSA later stated that Cruise's initial required crash reports omitted the post-crash dragging behavior.

View assessment

Incident breakdown

On 2 October 2023, following an initial collision involving a pedestrian and a human-driven vehicle, the driverless Cruise vehicle moved after impact and dragged the pedestrian approximately 20 feet before stopping. The available record does not establish the Cruise system's internal perception or intent.

Reviewed — no current class applies

After the 2 October 2023 crash, two required Cruise crash reports omitted the post-crash dragging behaviour. NHTSA later identified the omission after reviewing requested crash video, and announced a consent order on 30 September 2024.

Classified

Omitting the post-impact movement produced a truncated audit artefact before NHTSA review, even though that behaviour was central to the vehicle's separate safety consequence.

The post-crash dragging event was captured in other evidence and was later identifiable; the failure was its omission from the required regulatory report rather than complete non-capture of the event.

NHTSA was ultimately able to reconstruct the omitted dragging behaviour sufficiently to make a regulatory finding, so the audit trail was not rendered non-reconstructable.

Conclusion

The crash reports omitted material post-crash movement, impairing the initial investigation trace. Internal recorder, decision-basis and audit-trail arrangements remain unresolved.

Later framework versions are excluded from assessment of the October 2023 conduct.

Governance significance

Autonomous-vehicle governance must cover both physical fallback after an unexpected collision and accurate post-incident disclosure. A system can inherit an emergency it did not cause while still being responsible for minimising further harm, and incomplete reporting can impair independent oversight of that response.
