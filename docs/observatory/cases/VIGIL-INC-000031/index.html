VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000031

Claude Fable blocked benign biology questions under restricted-domain gating

At the June 2026 launch of Claude Fable 5, The Verge found that basic biology and medical-science questions—including prompts about mitochondria, cell membranes, mRNA vaccines, hay fever, asthma medicine and antibiotic resistance—were redirected to the less-capable Claude Opus 4.8. Anthropic said this was a deliberate, overly conservative safety trade-off intended to block most biology-work queries while it reduced false positives. Anthropic later reported that Fable 5.1's revised biology safeguards intervened on benign requests 85 percent less often than the original launch controls.

Conclusion

The failed boundary was proportional restricted-domain classification: harmless educational and medical-science requests were grouped with dual-use biology strongly enough to remove access to the selected frontier model. The fallback was visible and often useful, so this is capability degradation rather than total service denial. Because the downstream routing control activated as designed from an intentionally broad upstream classification, the occurrence does not satisfy the missing-or-false trigger condition for Control Activation Validity and exposes an unresolved classification gap for disproportionate false-positive restriction. The access-state differentiation success is bounded to the visible model switch and practical Opus continuation. The broad upstream policy is not an activation-without-trigger failure, and the later 85 percent improvement does not establish protective miss rate or complete effectiveness. Independent external assessment retains a Boundary for the full disengagement communication-plan proposition: documented model switching, reasons and an alternative route are visible, but complete planning/contact provisions are not preserved. The SDOS provision concerns missing governance infrastructure, a different trigger from this intentionally broad biology policy. Later reduced false positives support bounded repair of the unwanted over-restriction without establishing total safeguard effectiveness.

Governance significance

CAM requires risk controls to preserve legitimate scientific access through depth-, context- and trajectory-sensitive classification. A transparent fallback reduces deception but does not cure unnecessary capability suppression. The later precision improvement supports the practical recoverability of that governance invariant: safety gating can be narrowed while retaining intervention for genuinely risky requests.
