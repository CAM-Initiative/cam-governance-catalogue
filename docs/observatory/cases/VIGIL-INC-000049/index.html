Deepfake video call enabled a reported US$25 million Arup payment fraud

In late January 2024, an employee in the Hong Kong office of engineering firm Arup was deceived by a fraudulent payment scheme using synthetic representations of senior colleagues. Hong Kong authorities said the employee first received a phishing email impersonating the UK-based chief financial officer and was then invited into a group video conference. Police later concluded that the conference was pre-recorded rather than genuinely interactive and had been fabricated from publicly available video clips and voices of the impersonated officer and other personnel. The employee authorised transfers to five local bank accounts; contemporaneous police reporting described 15 transfers totalling about HK$200 million (roughly US$25 million). After the video ended, payment instructions reportedly continued through instant messaging. Arup later confirmed it was the victim and said fake voices and images had been used, while stating that its financial stability, business operations and internal systems were unaffected.
