VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000063

Google AI reportedly produced inaccurate cancer guidance

In January 2026, a Guardian investigation found Google AI Overviews at the top of health-search results giving inaccurate or misleading medical information. Examples included telling people with pancreatic cancer to avoid high-fat food, presenting liver-test reference numbers without patient context, and confusing cervical screening with vaginal-cancer testing; specialist charities described the potential for missed care or compromised treatment. Google disputed the breadth of the examples and said many summaries cited reputable sources and advised professional help. It removed AI Overviews for two liver-test queries after the report, but query variants and other criticised summaries still appeared. A February follow-up found that the general medical caution was absent from the initial summary and appeared only after “Show more,” beneath extra advice in less prominent text. No specific patient decision or clinical injury is established.

Incident breakdown

The Guardian investigation documented Google AI Overviews giving direct-answer health guidance that medical specialists described as inaccurate or materially context-poor, including advice about pancreatic-cancer diet, liver-test reference ranges without patient context, and cervical-screening information. The reporting establishes the outputs and concern, but no particular patient decision or clinical injury.

Classified

Inaccurate and context-deficient medical synthesis appeared as a direct answer to high-stakes health queries at an assurance level unsuitable for consequential reliance.

After the investigation, Google removed AI Overviews for two exact liver-test queries; reporting found that query variants and other criticised health summaries continued to appear.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The reported variants show that similar answers remained after two query-specific removals, but do not establish a defined required governance route that was bypassed.

A later interface test found that general medical caution was absent from the initial AI Overview and appeared only after the reader selected “Show more,” beneath additional advice in less prominent text.

Unresolved

The caution was produced but not presented with the initial answer; it appeared only after expansion and beneath additional advice.

The disclaimer existed in the expanded view but did not qualify the initial answer at its first reliance point; the reporting establishes a material delivery defect but no individual reliance outcome.

The reporting shows a disclaimer eventually appeared, but does not establish whether a defined, applicable warning control had a trigger condition that required it before the initial answer; the control activation boundary remains unknown.

Conclusion

A low-assurance synthesis acquired prominent Search-answer authority on consequential health questions. Independently, the general medical caution existed but was placed beyond the initial answer and below expanded advice, so it did not reach the user at the first point of likely reliance. The reported exact-query removals reduced some exposure without establishing that neighbouring queries or the broader warning path were repaired. The tested direct-answer assurance failure and late caution delivery remain distinct from patient injury, which is not established. The warning clause remains partly unresolved on activation/effect conditions. Narrow removals do not prove broader recovery. Benchmark, detector, model-development and voluntary programme scope remain independently bounded.

Governance significance

The case separates answer correctness from delivery of its caution. A warning technically present at the bottom of an expanded panel cannot be assumed to govern a decision made from the initial summary; subsequent exact-query removal also must not be mistaken for a general fix.
