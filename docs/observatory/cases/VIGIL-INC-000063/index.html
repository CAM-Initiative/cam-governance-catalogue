VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000063

Google AI reportedly produced inaccurate cancer guidance

In January 2026, a Guardian investigation found Google AI Overviews at the top of health-search results giving inaccurate or misleading medical information. Examples included telling people with pancreatic cancer to avoid high-fat food, presenting liver-test reference numbers without patient context, and confusing cervical screening with vaginal-cancer testing; specialist charities described the potential for missed care or compromised treatment.

Google disputed the breadth of the examples and said many summaries cited reputable sources and advised professional help. It removed AI Overviews for two liver-test queries after the report, but query variants and other criticised summaries still appeared.

A February follow-up found that the general medical caution was absent from the initial summary and appeared only after “Show more,” beneath extra advice in less prominent text. No specific patient decision or clinical injury is established.

Incident breakdown

In early January 2026, the Guardian's tested Google AI Overviews displayed potentially misleading medical summaries on pancreatic-cancer diets, liver-test reference ranges and cervical-screening questions. Specialist groups criticised the missing clinical context.

The reporting demonstrates the displayed answers, not that any identifiable patient acted on them or sustained injury.

Classified

Inaccurate and context-deficient medical synthesis appeared as a direct answer to high-stakes health queries at an assurance level unsuitable for consequential reliance.

Following the initial investigation, Google stopped generating AI Overviews for two exact liver-test searches. Subsequent reporting found that variants of those searches and other criticised medical summaries could still trigger AI answers.

Removing selected query outputs was not a demonstrated global fix for all health contexts.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The reported variants show that similar answers remained after two query-specific removals, but do not establish a defined required governance route that was bypassed.

A February 2026 interface follow-up found that a medical caution statement appeared only after expanding an AI Overview's 'Show more' section, below the initially visible advice. The observation supports a warning-visibility question for that tested interface; it does not establish clinical injury or that all such Overviews lacked caution.

Unresolved

The caution was produced but not presented with the initial answer; it appeared only after expansion and beneath additional advice.

The recorded warning-visibility defect concerns delivery before initial reliance, not a warning shown operative at that point. The governance-signal non-delivery exclusion applies to the bounded initial-presentation defect.

Missing patient injury is not the reason for withholding a control-effectiveness failure.

The reporting shows a disclaimer eventually appeared, but does not establish whether a defined, applicable warning control had a trigger condition that required it before the initial answer; the control activation boundary remains unknown.

Shared class criteria are not occurrence evidence. The recorded adjudication version may differ.

  • A factual claim, citation, technical statement, calculation, interpretation, source representation, or comparable epistemic artefact is generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised.
  • The downstream task has a material reliance requirement such that factual accuracy, evidentiary fidelity, technical correctness, source integrity, traceability, uncertainty handling, or equivalent epistemic assurance is consequential to the task.
  • The epistemic posture or assurance applied to the artefact is materially lower than the level required by that downstream reliance condition.
  • The artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, submitted, or accepted as fit for the reliance-bearing task without the required epistemic threshold being satisfied.
  • The mismatch materially affects or creates a material risk to decision-making, evidentiary use, professional work, institutional process, public reliance, technical correctness, or another reliance-bearing outcome.

Exclusions

  • Purely fictional, creative, symbolic, role-play, expressive, or contemplative generation where empirical truth is not a task condition.
  • Exploratory brainstorming, hypothesis generation, or modelling that is clearly represented as provisional and is not subsequently relied upon as verified or decision-ready.
  • Approximation where approximation is explicitly compatible with the task and reliance condition.
  • Material is clearly marked unverified or low-confidence and is not subsequently promoted into a higher-reliance state.
  • A required verification step is simply omitted and the material is presented as verified; use Required Verification Completion where that mechanism is sufficient.
  • Verification was valid when performed but later became stale or the artefact materially changed; use Verification Applicability Continuity or Post-Verification State Integrity as applicable.
  • The issue is only source provenance, authorship, or lineage while the epistemic assurance of the content is otherwise adequate for the task.

Conclusion

A low-assurance synthesis acquired prominent Search-answer authority on consequential health questions. Independently, the general medical caution existed but was placed beyond the initial answer and below expanded advice, so it did not reach the user at the first point of likely reliance.

The reported exact-query removals reduced some exposure without establishing that neighbouring queries or the broader warning path were repaired. The tested direct-answer assurance failure and late caution delivery remain distinct from patient injury, which is not established.

The warning clause remains partly unresolved on activation/effect conditions. Narrow removals do not prove broader recovery.

The tested medical-answer safety outcome is Not aligned. The specialist review and real-world testing are Aligned for that evaluation activity.

The two exact-query removals are Aligned as limited override. Validation, risk documentation and wider control effectiveness remain Boundary.

Their decisive process records are not available.

Governance significance

The case separates answer correctness from delivery of its caution. A warning technically present at the bottom of an expanded panel cannot be assumed to govern a decision made from the initial summary; subsequent exact-query removal also must not be mistaken for a general fix.
