VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000135

Hacktron researchers used Claude-assisted exploit development to compromise OpenAI accounts and demonstrate internal repository access

During security research in July 2026, Hacktron AI researchers used Anthropic Claude models to help analyze and develop an exploit chain against OpenAI-related systems. Reporting describes a vulnerability in the Discourse-hosted OpenAI community environment together with an OpenAI authorization or permission issue.

The chain exposed authentication material associated with OpenAI employees and some unaffiliated users, enabled access to affected ChatGPT or Codex accounts and potentially connected services according to account configuration, and was demonstrated through access to OpenAI's private software repository and a proof-of-access pull request. The researchers reported the findings, OpenAI and Discourse remediated the issues, and OpenAI paid a bug bounty.

The researchers’ original account says they reported the initial finding before demonstrating repository access and avoided reading internal code. OpenAI clarified that the bounty covered its own finding; the Discourse-hosted forum was excluded from its programme.

Conclusion

AI-assisted security research demonstrated an account-security weakness affecting AI services. The affected providers repaired the reported issues.

The finding concerns victim-service security, while the assisting model’s target selection and the researchers’ programme scope require separate authority assessment. The full testing scope and every connected-service exposure remain bounded by the sources.

A proxy concealed the live nature of the researchers’ own cloud test after refusal. That context change does not itself prove the model exceeded an independently valid scope, and the bounty does not authorise every preceding action.

The researchers’ disclosure timeline identifies reports to OpenAI and Discourse, responses from both providers and recorded fix dispositions. The OpenAI-side issue was later marked resolved.

This traces the security signal to capable owners and a bounded response. It does not authorise the preceding testing or independently verify every connected service.

Governance significance

The occurrence demonstrates why AI cybersecurity capability governance cannot be separated from access and authority governance. A model can behave exactly as intended in an authorized red-team setting while materially lowering the effort required to develop real exploits.

Governance therefore has to control capability access, target authority, credential and session propagation, connected-service authority, containment and human oversight rather than relying on the model to infer whether a technically possible exploit is institutionally authorized.
