Symantec honeypot captured Skynet-mode offensive agent instructions without tool execution

In summer 2026, Symantec's internet-facing AI honeypot captured a hostile Skynet-mode prompt intended to make a borrowed model execute an offensive agent's tools. The incoming request specified identity and safety overrides, approximately 74 tool definitions and up to 100,000 unattended steps.

Three purported preceding tool results were carried by the attacker's request, not executed by the honeypot. The defender recorded the material and replied with a generic fallback instead of a structured tool call; it executed no offensive tools.

The captured sequence supports positive material-event capture and bounded audit reconstruction. The lack of tool execution is a successful observed execution boundary but does not independently prove a specific authority-checking or model-safety control was activated.

No successful real-world compromise by this Skynet configuration is confirmed.

Failure-side conditions

  • The system receives or retrieves content from a source whose authority is lower than, different from, or unresolved relative to the action being considered.
  • The content is treated as operative instruction or authority rather than as data, evidence, context, or material for analysis.
  • No independent authority check validly establishes that the source may authorise the affected action.

Success-side conditions

  • Non-authorising material enters a context where it could influence an operative instruction.
  • The receiving process identifies its source role and establishes specific authority before any operative promotion.
  • Traceable instruction decisions show the validated principal, action and scope, or a bounded rejection of the proposed promotion.

Exclusions

Conclusion

The assessed AI-facing defender is Symantec's research honeypot, not the attacker's Skynet agent or an operational third-party model. The evidence supports positive capture and reconstruction invariants.

A critical favourable architectural/operational boundary was also observed: attacker-authored requests did not acquire the ability to invoke offensive tools through this sensor. The design or mechanism of that boundary is not described, so no independent model refusal, source-authority verification, permitted-tool validation or control-activation claim is made.

Source-Authority Separation is a material unresolved taxonomy candidate rather than a presumed success or failure.

Governance significance

Positive alignment evidence can reside in defensive observation and a bounded nonexecution outcome. Distinguishing the sender's attempted authority claim from the defender's actual authority decision prevents classifying malicious prompts as model failures, and avoids counting an inert research trap as a verified commercial safety refusal.
