VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000041

Claude-formatted citation errors entered Anthropic expert declaration

In a US copyright case brought by music publishers Universal Music Group, Concord and ABKCO, Anthropic expert Olivia Chen submitted a declaration on how often Claude might reproduce song lyrics. A footnote cited an American Statistician article in support of her rare-event sampling discussion. At a 13 May 2025 hearing, the publishers’ lawyer Matt Oppenheim challenged the title and authors as belonging to an article that did not exist, and Magistrate Judge Susan van Keulen ordered Anthropic to explain. In a 15 May declaration, Anthropic lawyer Ivana Dukanovic said a Latham & Watkins colleague had found a real article, then she asked Claude to format its citation using the correct link. Claude returned the right journal, year and link but the wrong title and authors; the legal team reviewed the article and corrected volume and page numbers yet missed those errors and two other wording errors. On 23 May, Judge van Keulen found the citation issue serious, noted that the applicable standing order required lead trial counsel to certify personal verification of content accuracy and that neither the certification nor that verification had occurred, struck paragraph 9 of Chen’s declaration, and said the issue undermined the declaration’s overall credibility.

Conclusion

The directly reviewed declaration and later court order support a compound current-taxonomy assessment. Epistemic Reliance Calibration captures the inaccurate Claude-formatted citation being incorporated into a court-facing expert declaration despite insufficient source fidelity for that reliance context. Required Verification Completion is independently supported by the court’s finding that the applicable standing order required lead trial counsel to certify personal verification of content accuracy and that neither the certification nor that verification had occurred. The legal team’s narrower manual cite-check does not defeat that mapping because it was not the required verification identified by the court. The court struck paragraph 9 and recorded a credibility consequence, but the preserved evidence does not establish a monetary sanction or broader merits deprivation attributable to the citation error. The retained source-fidelity and provenance failures concern false identifying fields in a real linked article; the distinct verification failure rests on the court’s specified personal-verification requirement, not mere absence of all checking. Partial checking remains a boundary rather than a successful complete-verification finding. The court’s process duty has no matching admitted canonical EXTREQ in the current registry and is not relabelled as NIST or IEEE conformance. Independent external assessment finds source/output verification and harmful-misinformation due diligence Not aligned for the false title/author fields missed by the described review. Relevant validation and risk-governance processes remain Boundary where decisive documentation is unavailable; different technical objects and later source versions remain audit-only. The court's personal-verification standing order remains a separately source-grounded duty without an invented canonical EXTREQ.

Governance significance

The occurrence demonstrates two distinct governance boundaries. First, generated bibliographic detail was promoted into a formal legal artefact below the source-fidelity threshold required for court-facing reliance. Second, the court identified a specific standing-order verification and certification requirement that had not been completed, even though the legal team had performed a narrower cite-check. A performed but incomplete checking activity therefore must not be treated as equivalent to completion of the verification posture actually required by the governing process.
