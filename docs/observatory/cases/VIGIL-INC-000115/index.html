Oklahoma judge used ChatGPT research in order containing two nonexistent case citations

Stephens County Associate District Judge Lawrence Wheeler acknowledged using ChatGPT for legal research in connection with a 2025 child-paternity case order that contained citations to two cases that do not exist, according to an August 17, 2026 letter from District Attorney Jason Hicks describing Wheeler's statement to the Oklahoma State Bureau of Investigation. Reporting identifies the nonexistent cases as Cummings v.

Cimarron Elevator Co. and Hawkins v. Linhart.

Wheeler said he created the order himself and used ChatGPT only for research. The order was later vacated after an attorney challenged the citations at the Oklahoma Supreme Court.

The Oklahoma Attorney General's Office stated that the evidence reviewed did not support criminal prosecution; possible judicial discipline remained a separate matter.

  • A factual claim, citation, technical statement, calculation, interpretation, source representation, or comparable epistemic artefact is generated, retrieved, transformed, inferred, or synthesised.
  • The downstream task has a material reliance requirement such that factual accuracy, evidentiary fidelity, technical correctness, source integrity, traceability, uncertainty handling, or equivalent epistemic assurance is consequential to the task.
  • The epistemic posture or assurance applied to the artefact is materially lower than the level required by that downstream reliance condition.
  • The artefact is represented, propagated, incorporated, submitted, or accepted as fit for the reliance-bearing task without the required epistemic threshold being satisfied.
  • The mismatch materially affects or creates a material risk to decision-making, evidentiary use, professional work, institutional process, public reliance, technical correctness, or another reliance-bearing outcome.

Exclusions

  • Purely fictional, creative, symbolic, role-play, expressive, or contemplative generation where empirical truth is not a task condition.
  • Exploratory brainstorming, hypothesis generation, or modelling that is clearly represented as provisional and is not subsequently relied upon as verified or decision-ready.
  • Approximation where approximation is explicitly compatible with the task and reliance condition.
  • Material is clearly marked unverified or low-confidence and is not subsequently promoted into a higher-reliance state.
  • A required verification step is simply omitted and the material is presented as verified; use Required Verification Completion where that mechanism is sufficient.
  • Verification was valid when performed but later became stale or the artefact materially changed; use Verification Applicability Continuity or Post-Verification State Integrity as applicable.
  • The issue is only source provenance, authorship, or lineage while the epistemic assurance of the content is otherwise adequate for the task.

Conclusion

The material governance issue is epistemic reliance in an authoritative judicial artefact. Fictitious AI-produced case citations entered a signed court order even though the validity of judicial legal reasoning depends on materially higher source and authority fidelity than exploratory legal research.

The reviewed evidence does not establish that Wheeler delegated authorship of the order to ChatGPT, and it does not expressly establish that a distinct required verification step was wholly omitted; VIGIL therefore does not infer Required Verification Omission as a secondary class. Fictitious-source and reliance-assurance failures are retained; the reported research use does not prove delegated authorship or complete omission of a distinct verification step.

Vacatur and absence of criminal prosecution do not settle possible discipline. The safe-functioning outcome is Not aligned.

The later occurrence review is Aligned for the examined material. Relevant assurance-documentation assessments remain Boundary where their decisive records are not available.

Harm Impact: Fictitious AI-produced authority entered an operative paternity-case order. An attorney challenged it and the order was vacated.

The meaningful bounded procedural interference and substantive remedy support S3; no lost custody right or ultimate paternity outcome is inferred.

Governance significance

The occurrence demonstrates that the downstream assurance threshold is determined by the institutional use of generated or AI-assisted material. Research assistance can become governance-relevant when its outputs are incorporated into an authoritative decision without sufficient epistemic assurance, particularly where the resulting artefact can affect litigants and legal process.
