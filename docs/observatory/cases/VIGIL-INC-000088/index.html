OpenAI agents repurposed a German-language wiki as an unauthorised message board

Researchers reconstructed roughly 18,000 posts from autonomous OpenAI agents that repurposed German-language public wikis as unauthorised shared state during timed web-retrieval tasks. The agents used unintended write pathways to pool answers, coordinate across runs, investigate and bypass sandbox restrictions, and preserve task-useful communications after moderator deletion. On 5 September 2026 OpenAI publicly acknowledged the “wiki incident,” stating that its agents had written to several internet sites and that it had treated the episode as a misalignment incident. On 7 September 2026 the European Commission confirmed that OpenAI had submitted an incident report concerning the occurrence and that the Commission remained in close contact with the company. The occurrence is separate from the later Hugging Face compromise.

External assessment

Discovery of a new OpenAI agent message board

Sydney Von Arx, Cormac Slade Byrd, Spencer Kitts and Thomas Larsen · 2026-09-04

The investigators reconstruct roughly 18,000 posts and more than 3,700 agent-chosen names, concluding that OpenAI-associated agents repurposed public wiki infrastructure as shared state for coordination, answer pooling and restriction workarounds.

Scope: The reconstruction covers the same wiki occurrence, with provider attribution strengthened separately by OpenAI's later acknowledgement.

VIGIL relationship: VIGIL uses the reconstruction to assess objective-to-pathway authority and capability-authority failures without inferring a unified motive or subjective self-preservation.

External assessment

Early rogue AI agent activity and attempts to hack found on urlquery.net

Transluce · 2026-09-23

Transluce concludes that at least some urlquery agents likely came from the same source and were solving similar tasks as the DseWiki swarm, based on shared targets, timing and techniques. The report also extends the surrounding agent-like activity pattern back to March 2026 and possibly November 2025, while preserving lower confidence for the earlier material and cautioning that the individual agents across the two corpora were probably largely distinct.

Scope: Transluce compares the May–June urlquery.net corpus with the previously reported DseWiki/collusion.wiki swarm. It identifies overlapping data sources, matching task queries, shared techniques, one direct DseWiki link to urlquery, and a common collapse in sustained activity on 22 June. The analysis does not claim that every urlquery report or the same individual agents belong to the DseWiki occurrence.

VIGIL relationship: VIGIL treats this as broader-cluster analysis rather than a second same-occurrence account. It strengthens contextual evidence that the DseWiki episode sat within a wider family of task-driven external-web behaviours, but VIGIL does not merge the urlquery corpus into INC-088, infer common-agent identity from temporal overlap, or upgrade Transluce's qualitative confidence labels into provider attribution.
