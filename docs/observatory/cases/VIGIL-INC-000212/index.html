Tasmania Police Axon Draft One trial misattributed family-violence participants and omitted risks

In 2025, Tasmania Police tested Axon Draft One, an AI tool that turns body-worn camera recordings into draft family-violence reports. In 148 retrospective cases, evaluators found six AI drafts with incorrect names or participant roles and six that omitted threats, assaults or risk factors.

In ten other comparisons, the tool captured risk-related detail absent from earlier human reports. ABC News described one reported draft that referred to prior threats to kill although an officer reviewing the recording heard a denial of earlier threats.

The subsequent supervised Launceston trial assessed 51 live reports. Police officers had to review AI drafts and the evaluation measured faster preparation times, alongside lower accuracy in complex multi-person encounters.

Tasmania Police recommended training and governance measures before wider adoption. The published evaluation does not establish that any inaccurate draft was filed without correction or caused an adverse personal or legal outcome.

Conclusion

The mistaken names and roles demonstrate a loss of correct binding between person-specific evidence and the participant it describes. The separate omissions and reported reversed denial show limitations of summarised factual content.

The presence of an inaccurate draft does not show that an officer skipped required verification; the pilot explicitly required human review. Nor does the existence of that requirement prove every inaccurate draft was caught.

An AI draft, checked police record and authorised final report must remain distinct levels of reliance.

Governance significance

This evaluation tests preservation of source-to-person attribution and material risk facts in police records. It shows why observed draft errors, effective verification, readiness for wider use and realised harm need independent assessment.

It also contains positive evidence that some AI drafts recovered risk information omitted in human writing.
