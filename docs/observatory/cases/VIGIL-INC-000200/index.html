VIGIL Observatory · VIGIL-INC-000200

Danfoss refrigeration-control compromises disrupted French supermarkets

During the night of 11–12 October 2026, remote management systems for Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC refrigeration controls were compromised at supermarkets across France. Reports describe altered defrost and temperature-control settings, overheating refrigerated equipment and disruption to fresh and frozen food storage.

Outcomes differed sharply between stores. At Intermarché in Gradignan, a cold room reportedly reached 50°C and triggered a sprinkler, flooding the reserve area.

In Haguenau, two different retailers discarded frozen stock; at Super U in Mirepoix, display-area goods were discarded but reserve cold rooms remained unaffected. Intermarché La Haye-du-Puits reported substantial food loss and heat-damaged equipment.

Coopérative U's president said 80 of its stores had been affected but most avoided a cold-chain break or stock destruction through intervention. Refrigeration specialists helped detect and restore failures.

The identity of the operator, the command origin and the reason for the changes have not been established.

Context

CERT-FR confirms compromises of Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC refrigeration management systems during the bounded wave. Its French wording below refers to numerous compromises of remote controllers.

The alert recommends removing Internet exposure and using protected management routes. Its later inclusion of other controller products does not establish a common actor or merge the separately recorded Dixell occurrence.

Classified

Compromised remote management interfaces made consequential refrigeration controls reachable to an unauthorised operator. The access exposure engages the distinction between technical capability and permission.

The sources do not identify the initiating process or show an AI or governance process treating reachability as authority. A security compromise and subsequent food loss do not by themselves establish that permission-inference mechanism, or that an authority check held.

At Intermarché in Gradignan, reporting describes a cold room reaching 50°C, a sprinkler activating and reserve-area flooding. The reported defrost duration changed from one to eight hours.

No controller command log identifies the requesting actor, any original authorised maintenance scope or a separate alarm-delay setting. The altered setting is not assumed to be the command sent to every store.

Unresolved

The prolonged defrost and resulting conditions show why the command scope matters. The initiating account, authority over the original task and subsequent target or action change are unknown.

An intrusion alone does not demonstrate that an existing maintenance permission was treated as authority for these changes.

Haguenau Intermarché reported cabinet readings of 32°C, approximately €50,000 in frozen-stock losses and an additional smaller chilled-stock loss. This is one retailer’s consequence account, not a national loss estimate or evidence of a particular authentication or control mechanism.

Reviewed — no current class applies

The separate E.Leclerc store in Haguenau described settings up to 50°C and discarded frozen goods. It is a different affected retailer from the nearby Intermarché.

The report does not establish that the same command, credential or response route operated at both.

Reviewed — no current class applies

Super U Mirepoix managers described a remote-monitoring warning at 2 am, staff attendance and a refrigeration technician arriving at 6 am. They reported approximately €90,000 in losses from the affected display areas; reserves and cold rooms remained unaffected.

Warning delivery, repair attendance and the surviving stock are distinct observations. The source does not define a guaranteed protection threshold or establish why reserves escaped the interference.

Unresolved

The reported monitoring warning reached people who responded, while some stock was still lost and reserves were unaffected. The missing facts are the control’s required protective purpose, applicable response time and the causal link between the response and the spared reserves.

The outcome therefore establishes neither inadequate effect under this class nor a separately verified invariant-held result.

Coopérative U’s president reported 80 affected stores and said most avoided a cold-chain break or stock destruction through the response of teams and refrigeration specialists. He also said a minority could not react or control the interference quickly enough.

This does not establish that 80 stores lost stock, or that the same protective mechanism operated at every store.

Unresolved

The retailer’s account supports distinct protected and harmed outcomes. It does not identify the particular activated controls, their required protective levels or store-specific intervention traces.

Those missing links prevent attributing the avoided losses to a validated control effect under this class; the observed response remains part of the occurrence.

Intermarché La Haye-du-Puits reported approximately US$112,000 in discarded food and heat-affected equipment. These are attributed consequences at one store.

The reported losses and hardware damage do not identify the remote command source, a participating AI system or a uniform national mechanism.

Reviewed — no current class applies

Censys observed 1,825 Internet-exposed Danfoss StoreView hosts in France on 11 October and 537 on 14 October. These are two exposure measurements, not a count of compromised stores.

A reduction in visible hosts does not by itself establish restoration, valid access control or attribution of the earlier commands.

Conclusion

Compromises of deployed Danfoss refrigeration supervisors were followed by material stock loss and interrupted store operations, while many other affected stores detected or contained interference in time. The same episode therefore records both consequential control interference and successful local protective responses.

The evidence does not establish whether a fleet-management mistake, unauthorised remote script, compromised service provider or another process initiated the changes. Neither the available GitHub examples nor pre-existing product vulnerabilities identify a perpetrator, intention or participating AI system.

The access-capability relationship is a Boundary finding, not a model-misalignment conclusion. The original command authority and the defined protective effects of store controls remain unresolved.

Avoided losses are preserved as observed response outcomes without converting them into an invariant-held classification.

Governance significance

A supervisory configuration interface can affect physical refrigeration and generate delayed real-world consequences. The differences between successful and unsuccessful store interventions matter: a controller compromise does not invariably produce food destruction.

Independent monitoring, site-specific recovery and constrained remote access are observable boundaries. Public third-party monitoring and simulation repositories show how simple software can interact with refrigeration telemetry or reproduce controller interfaces, but do not establish that their code was deployed in the incident.
